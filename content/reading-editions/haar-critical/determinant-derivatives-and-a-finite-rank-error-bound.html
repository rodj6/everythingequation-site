20. Determinant derivatives and a finite-rank error bound

The planar moment formula admits a complementary certified approximation in terms of the integer derivatives of its determinant. This section gives explicit mathematical error bounds. Their size is material, especially near a divergent-moment boundary. The bundled exact-rational jet implementation reaches rank eight at selected inputs. Its cost and the bound itself still limit useful precision at generic noninteger values.

Each finite set of determinant derivatives can in principle be enclosed by interval quadrature of the symbol coefficients on [0, 2π] and ball-polynomial operations. For example, enclose the continuous Fourier integrands on each subinterval and multiply their interval ranges by the subinterval lengths. For z < 1 all denominators stay away from zero, and mesh refinement and increased precision give shrinking enclosures. Multivariate Taylor truncation at bidegree (J, J) suffices, including all cross terms in the determinant. The alternating binomial sum can lose many digits. No practical 10^{−10} runtime throughout the full parameter box of the original accuracy target follows from a convergent series with these constants. This construction supplies an independent absolute error mechanism even when the moment is close to a complex zero.
