8.1 Comparison with Prior Literature

Covariant phase space and holographic quantum Fisher information. Our starting point is the Hollands–Wald canonical energy functional ℰ_{B}(h) = W_{ΣB}(γ, ℒ_{ξB}γ) [3, 4], whose identification with boundary quantum Fisher information (second variation of relative entropy) for spherical regions in vacuum-perturbed holographic CFTs was established by Lashkari and Van Raamsdonk [7] (building on the first-order linearized Einstein derivation of [5] and the CHM modular map [2]). While [7] proved general positivity of ℰ_{B} via energy-density inequalities and noted that Hollands–Wald gauge fixes the extremal surface, explicit closed-form evaluations of the Lorentzian timelike HRT surface variation A^{′′}_{ext}[h_{odd}] and its exact spectral competition against the bulk kinetic term −I[h_{odd}] had not been carried out. Our exact Green’s function (Lemma 3.1), one-line time-even identity (3.17), sharp spectral theorem ‖T‖_{op} = 1/10 (The-orem 3.2), and explicit O(ε_{z}) conformal-boundary corner audit (Section 3.4) give a complete analytical accounting of both bulk and boundary contributions in AdS_{4}.

Direct extension of Rodgers (2026). Rodgers [6, 8] formulated the boundary-ball readout map (M_{1}, M_{2}), characterized the fixed-frame first-order kernel ker M_{1}, derived the charge–area identity eliminating k in Poincaré AdS_{4}, and evaluated ℰ_{B} explicitly for the time-even polyno-mial mode E_{Rodgers} =Ẽ_{1} +^{1}_{3}E_{0} and oscillatory time-even modes h_{c}. The present paper resolves the open problems posed in [8, Section 6] and carries the programme to an infinite-dimensional reconstruction theorem inside ker M_{1}: we derive the +_{8}^{1}_{π} off-shell charge–area prefactor directly with consistent form signs and boundary orientations, extend the exact computation to all polynomial degrees and the full analytically diagonalized Lorentzian time-odd sector, construct explicit closed-form second-order vacuum completions k on the Poincaré patch, prove exact all-radii non-identifiability and all-times polarization-chirality blindness, compute the exact lifting under time shifts, m = 2/m = 4 multipoles, and time-translated Lorentz boosts (including an explicit six-ball constructive recovery design with sharp v, τ →0 conditioning rates), determine the exact minimax stability and SDP phase transition of M_{2}, construct smooth finite-energy localized wavepackets strictly inside ker M_{1} with a minimal constructive three-ball reconstruction theorem, prove an exact manifestly positive one-dimensional real-space canonical-energy identity for every centered ball (Theorem 7.2), and establish our headline infinite-dimensional reconstruction theorem (Theorem 7.7): at any fixed centered radius R_{0} > 0, exact canonical-energy data on any nonempty open time interval determine every perturbation in the compact positive width-measure class ℳ_{ℂ}([a_{min}, a_{max}]) (0 < a_{min} ≤a_{max} < ∞) up to one overall sign, sharply separated from the fixed-radius lifted rank obstruction (Theorem 7.6(ii)), multi-radius lifted injectivity (Theorem 7.9(ii)), and finite-window noisy stability barriers (Theorem 7.11).

Linear holographic bulk reconstruction and integral geometry. Classical bulk re-construction programmes – HKLL smearing [16], bulk-cone singularities [17], hole-ography and kinematic space [15, 18], entanglement-based linearized metric reconstruction [19, 20], and extremal-area inversion [21] – operate primarily at first order in the metric perturbation (or invert the X-ray / Radon transform of δA_{B} over all boundary regions, including boosted and deformed regions). Our results clarify precisely what is lost and what is recovered when mea-surements are restricted to spherical rest-frame balls: on a fixed time slice, the entire homoge-neous TT space V_{hom} and the infinite-dimensional localized axial sector h[Φ] lie in ker M_{1} for all rest-frame balls, so first-order spherical area readouts are completely blind to them. Second-order canonical energy detects every nonzero mode in V_{hom} and our localized family h[µ], yet still exhibits exact rest-frame degeneracies at t = 0 (Theorem 4.1) that require time shifts, m = 2/m = 4 shape deformations, or time-translated Lorentz boosts to resolve (Theorems 4.4– 4.5). Once time evolution on any nonempty open interval I ⊂ℝ is observed, Theorem 7.7 shows that even a single fixed centered radius R_{0} > 0 suffices for exact noiseless physical reconstruction across the entire compact positive width-measure class ℳ_{ℂ}([a_{min}, a_{max}])/{±1} – without vary-ing the ball radius at all. Under measurement noise, however, both Theorem 5.8 (exponential bulk-depth extrapolation instability (R_{∗}/R_{max})^{4n+2} in V_{n}^{even}) and Theorem 7.11(iii) (Bernstein-rate exponential/logarithmic ill-conditioning with unit-ball minimax error floor 1 and H^{s} rate Ω((log(1/η))^{−2s})) quantify severe stability barriers mirroring the analytic-continuation insta-bility well known in Euclidean and HKLL bulk reconstruction.

Phase retrieval, PhaseLift, and optimal recovery. Our stability theory in Section 5 connects directly to the phase-retrieval and optimal-recovery literatures [11, 12, 14, 23–25]. Unlike standard PhaseLift [23], where measurement matrices A_{k} = ϕ_{k}ϕ^{⊤}_{k} are rank-one and randomized measurements ensure SDP exactness in high dimensions, gravitational boundary-ball canonical energies are full-rank positive-definite matrices A_{n}(R) whose radial dependence is constrained by AdS_{4} scaling to the (2n −1)-dimensional Hankel subspace {R^{6+2s}}. As a result, SDP relaxation is universally exact at n = 2 (Theorem 5.3), yet collapses completely (L_{SDP} = ∞) for every n ≥ 3 even with continuum radial measurements, while pure-state recovery remains globally injective and Lipschitz stable from 2n −1 radii (Theorems 5.5–5.6).
