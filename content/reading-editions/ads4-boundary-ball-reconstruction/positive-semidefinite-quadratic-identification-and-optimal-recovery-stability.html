5 Positive-Semidefinite Quadratic Identification and Optimal

Recovery Stability

Having characterized the exact canonical-energy bilinears on V_{hom} ⊂ ker M_{1} and the blind spots of rest-frame balls in Sections 3–4, we now turn to the quantitative inverse problem inside an identifiable subspace: given finitely many noisy second-order canonical-energy readouts y_{k} = ℰ_{Bk}(h) + η_{k} (k = 1, . . . , m), when can a quadratic functional φ(h) = ⟨h, Qh⟩ (such as a mode amplitude squared or the canonical energy at an unobserved radius R_{∗}) be reconstructed uniquely, when does convex semidefinite lifting preserve the exact worst-case noise amplification, and how rapidly does stability degrade with the number of modes n and the extrapolation ratio
