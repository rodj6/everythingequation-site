5 The local parameter input

Let E be a number field, let Ω/E be an algebraic closure, and choose a valuation of Ω above a finite place w of E. Write k = E_{w}, and write W_{k} and I_{k} for the resulting Weil and inertia groups. We use the reci-procity convention in which a uniformizer corresponds to geometric Frobenius. Thus the inverse reciprocity homomorphism a : W_{k} −→k^{×} has v_{k}(a(σ)) = − deg(σ) when the degree of arithmetic Frobenius is one.

Write ℐ(k) = ∐_{n≥0} Irr(GL_{n}(k)) for isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth complex representa-tions, with their degrees, and write 𝒲(k) for the Weil–Deligne classes used below. A character twist of π means π ⊗(χ ∘ det); write πν^{s} when χ = | · |^{s}_{k}. A cuspidal class is called good if its degree is one, or if k ≃ ℚ_{p} as a topological field and the class is a character twist of a depth-zero cuspidal class. A general irreducible class is good if its cuspidal support consists of good cuspidal classes. Here depth zero means the existence of nonzero vectors fixed by the principal congruence subgroup K_{1} = 1 + 𝔭_{k}M_{n}(𝒪_{k}).

Definition 5.1 (The required parameter laws). The local parameter input is the existence, for every choice above, of a and a map R : ℐ(k) →𝒲(k) with the following properties. It preserves degree, sends a character χ to χ∘a, and sends cuspidal classes to irreducible Weil parameters. It commutes with real unramified twists. If 𝔪 is the Langlands multisegment of π, then

R(π) = R(ρ_{∆}) (ℓ_{∆}−1)/2 ⊗ Sp(ℓ_{∆}), (F13.1)

where ρ_{∆} is the first cuspidal class of the segment. For every central character ω_{π},

| det R(π)(σ)| = |ω_{π}(a(σ))|. (F13.2)

For good π the following stronger assertions hold:

det R(π)(σ) = ω_{π}(a(σ)), (F13.3)

If π^{′} is good and R(π) = R(π^{′}), then π = π^{′}. If good π has K_{1}-fixed vectors, then R(π) is tame. Finally R satisfies the explicit tame type condition of Proposition 5.5 below, including zero monodromy and the specified inertia characteristic polynomial.

These are precisely the twelve fields of the local statement called RecTExists in the source. The restric-tions to good classes are part of the statement: existence of this map is weaker than the full local Langlands correspondence. In particular, the source’s construction is permitted to choose unrelated parameters on other cuspidal twist orbits. We retain its statement rather than silently strengthening the claimed input.

Proposition 5.4 (Extension from cuspidal parameters). Assume the Langlands classification in the following precise form: each irreducible class π has a unique finite multiset 𝔪(π) of segments, and a cuspidal class ρ has the single segment (ρ, 1). Proof. For every π choose a list of the segments in 𝔪(π) and take the direct sum in (F13.1), with r in place of R on each first cuspidal class. Two lists of the same multiset differ by a permutation. Interchanging adjacent summands is implemented by (u, v) →→ (v, u); reassociating is implemented by ((u, v), z) →→ (u, (v, z)). Both maps intertwine the Weil action and monodromy. Thus the direct-sum isomorphism class is unchanged by a permutation, and the assignment is well defined. If another list is asserted to be the Langlands multisegment of π, uniqueness of that multisegment makes it a permutation of the chosen list, proving the required formula for every such list. The formula also determines R(π), giving uniqueness.

For cuspidal ρ, the one segment has length one. Its exponent (1 −1)/2 is zero, and Sp(1) is the trivial one-dimensional parameter with zero monodromy. It cannot be linked: if linked, its smaller first endpoint would make it precede the later segment, contrary to the allowed-order condition. Products of the two unlinked segment quotients are irreducible and commute, by Zelevinsky [5, Theorem 9.7(a)]; this uses the quotient segment representations in his notation.
