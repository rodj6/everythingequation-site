10.2 The prismatic normalization and its Hodge quotient

The separate prismatic input in the Hodge-bundle package requires a functor, not merely the existence of individual modules. We record the part whose precise normalization is already checked against the published construction.

Lemma 10.8 (The perfectoid base hypotheses). The ring O in Lemma 10.1 is an integral perfectoid ring. It is therefore among the quasisyntomic bases of prismatic Dieudonne theory.

Proof. It is p-complete by assumption and has no p-torsion, being a subring of a characteristic-zero field. Choose ϖ ∈C with ϖ^{p} = p. Its valuation is v(p)^{1/p} < 1, so ϖ ∈O and ϖ^{p} = p. Frobenius on O/p is surjective: given a lift x ∈O, choose y ∈C with y^{p} = x. Then v(y)^{p} = v(x) ≤1, so y ∈O. Its kernel on O/p consists of the classes of z with v(z)^{p} ≤v(p), equivalently v(z) ≤v(ϖ), equivalently z ∈ϖO. Thus Frobenius gives the required isomorphism O/ϖ ≃ O/p. These are the integral perfectoid criterion for a p-torsion-free complete ring, with ϖ^{p}= p. Equivalently, C is complete and uniform, with surjective Frobenius on O/p and nondiscrete valuation: the elements p^{1/pa} have valuations tending to one from below. The implication from integral perfectoid to quasisyntomic is the precise established base-ring fact in [10,

Proof. The tilt O^{♭} is a valuation ring, hence local. A Witt vector is a unit precisely when its zeroth component is a unit of O^{♭}. Thus A is local. A finite projective module over a local ring is free: lift a basis modulo the maximal ideal to a minimal generating set, split its surjection from a finite free module by projectivity, and reduce the kernel modulo that ideal. Its reduction is zero; Nakayama’s lemma gives zero kernel. Apply this to M_{G}. Reducing a basis modulo ξ gives a basis of D_{G} over A/(ξ) = O, of the same rank.

Every functorial map M_{H} →M_{G} carries ξM_{H} into ξM_{G}, so it descends to D_{H} →D_{G}. Composition, identities and addition descend as well. Scalar reduction is through θ, which proves the semilinearity of r_{G}; its surjectivity and kernel are the defining quotient identities.

Because O^{♭} is perfect, φ is an automorphism of A. The underlying abelian group of φ^{∗}M_{G} = A⊗_{φ,A}M_{G} is identified with that of M_{G} by a ⊗m →→φ^{−1}(a)m. Under this identification, the image of the linearized Frobenius is the image of the semilinear φ_{G}. Its cokernel being killed by ξ̃ therefore says exactly that, for every m, some n satisfies φ_{G}(n) =ξm̃. This is the source’s elementwise image condition, not just a rank assertion.

Finally the source asks only for the particular full faithfulness assertion between the constant and multi-plicative objects. Both of the displayed rank-one Frobenius modules are the objects of the published functor. Its full faithfulness provides one unique group homomorphism with any given Frobenius-compatible module map between them, with the direction reversed because the functor is contravariant. This is exactly the stated direction of the group homomorphism.
