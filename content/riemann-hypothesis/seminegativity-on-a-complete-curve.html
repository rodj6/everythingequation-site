10.6 Seminegativity on a complete curve

Background fact 10.23 (The characteristic-zero Lie positivity theorem). Let C be a smooth projective con-nected curve over a characteristic-zero field, let K = k(C), and let G/C be a smooth group scheme of finite type. If a subbundle F ⊂ Lie G is ample and its generic fiber is a Lie subalgebra, then that fiber is the Lie algebra of a connected unipotent algebraic subgroup of G_{K}. This is [18, Theorem 2.6,§2.4], read in the original author’s preprint, pp. 14–16. Its footnote 4 on p. 14 explains that the quasi-projectivity in its formulation is automatic for a smooth finite-type group scheme over a smooth curve.

Proposition 10.24 (The exact semiabelian seminegativity input). Let C/k be a smooth projective geometri-cally connected curve in characteristic zero, and let G/C be a semiabelian scheme in the source’s sense. If a vector bundle W admits a monomorphism W → Lie G, then

deg det W ≤0.

Thus SemiAbelianLieSeminegative holds, including monomorphisms whose cokernel has torsion.

Proof. The source group is smooth, separated and quasi-compact over the noetherian curve, hence is of finite type. Its Lie sheaf is the usual finite-rank vector bundle. Its commutative group law makes the Lie bracket zero, so every generic subspace is a Lie subalgebra. Every geometric fiber, in particular the geometric generic fiber, is an extension of an abelian variety by a torus.

Such a group has no nontrivial connected unipotent subgroup in characteristic zero. Indeed, after alge-braic closure a unipotent group has a filtration by additive groups. A homomorphism from G_{a} to an abelian variety extends to P^{1} by properness and is constant; abelian varieties have no rational curves. A homomor-phism from G_{a} to a torus is also zero, since invertible regular functions on A^{1} are constant. Induction through that filtration shows that any connected unipotent subgroup of the semiabelian group is trivial.

Suppose that the maximal Harder–Narasimhan slope of E = Lie G were positive. Its first Harder– Narasimhan term F is a nonzero semistable subbundle of positive slope. In characteristic zero such a bundle on a complete curve is ample; equivalently the positive-slope part has positive minimal slope. This is the curve-bundle ampleness criterion used in the proof of Bost’s theorem, loc. cit., pp. 16–17. Fact 10.23 would give a nonzero connected unipotent subgroup with Lie algebra F_{K}. The preceding paragraph excludes it. Hence µ_{max}(E) ≤0.

Saturate the image of W in E, obtaining a subbundle W^{sat}. Over the discrete valuation local rings of the smooth curve, a torsion-free coherent module is free; thus this saturation and its locally free quotient have the usual bundle meaning. Moreover W^{sat}/W is torsion, so

deg W = deg W^{sat}− length(W^{sat}/W) ≤ deg W^{sat}.

Every subbundle has slope at most µ_{max}(E), so deg W^{sat}≤0. This proves the inequality. For rank zero it is the empty determinant of degree zero. The source interNum of a line on this curve is its ordinary degree, by the one-difference calculation in Proposition 10.18. Thus the inequality is exactly its stated one. All argu-ments and the cited theorem apply over the original characteristic-zero field; alternatively a field extension multiplies curve degrees by positive factors and gives the same conclusion.
