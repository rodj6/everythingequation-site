The determinant of each factor has coefficients in T^{′} by the assumed characteristic-polynomial condition, after substituting cz^{j} for z. Multiplicativity of determinant shows that the coefficients of det U through degree N belong to T^{′}. As N was arbitrary, this holds for every coefficient.

Now apply this result to the diagonal pivot blocks in a formal block elimination of 1 −zΘ. Its first d by d block has constant term 1, so its inverse is a formal series with coefficients in A. Multiplying on the left by the block lower-unitriangular matrix which eliminates the first block column does not change the determinant. The remaining block matrix is the Schur complement; it again has constant term 1 and all coefficients in A. Induct on the number of block rows. Each pivot determinant has coefficients in T^{′}, and therefore so does det(1 −zΘ).

If Θ has g block rows, the coefficient of z^{gd} in this last polynomial is (−1)^{gd} det Θ. Since T^{′} is closed under signs, the required determinant function belongs to T^{′}. This argument uses only finite congruences at each coefficient; it involves no convergence assertion or division by a factorial in O.
