7.4 Complex uniformization and the marked period dictionary

The analytic orbit count above uses actual complex points and tangent spaces, rather than just a set with the same cardinality. We make the required comparison explicit. The classical results used in this comparison are the following precise ones. A compact connected complex Lie group is its tangent space modulo the kernel of its exponential. Polarizable complex tori are equivalent to complex abelian varieties. The Appell– Humbert description gives the factors of automorphy of line bundles; a positive form gives an ample bundle. These statements are recorded in Milne’s Complex Multiplication, Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 6.1 with its Appell–Humbert and Lefschetz statements on pages 46–47 [34]. For a projective complex variety, algebraic and analytic sections and homomorphisms of coherent sheaves agree: Serre’s original GAGA, number 12, Theorems 1–3, pages 19–20 [24]. Here positivity, compactness and projectivity hold for the polarized abelian varieties under discussion; GAGA is used on these projective fibers, not on the nonproper PEL base.

Proposition 7.29 (Holomorphic uniformization of the selected family). Suppose N ≥3, (N, p) = 1, and M is the smooth quasi-projective representing PEL model of the preceding proposition. Proof. The preceding proposition provides three exact properties: every point has a marked period descrip-tion, every permitted period occurs, and marked linearly equivalent periods give the same point. They are the source’s three FineAt clauses. Lemma 7.27 shows that the integral marked lattice class of a point is locally constant in every holomorphic chart. Thus it is locally constant on M(ℂ).

There are only finitely many values. Here one needs quasi-compactness of an algebraic scheme, not compactness of its complex points. Cover M by finitely many finite-type affine schemes, each with finitely many irreducible components. The complex points of an irreducible complex affine variety are connected. One direct reason for this last assertion is generic finite projection: on the complement of its discriminant, an irreducible variety is a connected finite unramified cover of a connected affine open. If the cover split, the elementary symmetric functions in the roots in one component would give a nontrivial factor of the defining irreducible function-field polynomial. These coefficients are holomorphic off the discriminant. The poly-nomial’s root bound gives polynomial growth after multiplication by a discriminant power, so they extend across it and are polynomials, by the Cauchy estimates on expanding polydiscs. This is the forbidden alge-braic factorization. The complement is dense in the original variety: the nonzero polynomial discriminant has empty interior on each smooth local branch, and smooth points are dense. Its connected closure is there-fore the whole variety. A locally constant function is constant on each such component, proving finite image. Choose one marked lattice (Λ_{i}, t_{i}, E_{i}) for each value.

For a permitted period P on Λ_{i}, choose its represented point u_{i}(P) and exponential. The classification proves surjectivity of these maps, disjointness for different i, and the exact fiber relation

for a marked integral isometry w and a complex linear isomorphism h. The choices themselves do not create a continuity assertion; we next prove it.

Let the algebraic base have local dimension d, and let the period coordinates of Lemma 7.26 have di-mension d^{′}. Lift a holomorphic base chart to periods by Lemma 7.27 and take their normalized coordinate Z(P). This is injective on a smaller chart: equal period coordinates give linearly equivalent periods and hence the same represented point. The holomorphic rank theorem implies d ≤d^{′}. The group of linear coordinate changes commuting with the order action has a countable dense subset, as a subspace of a finite dimensional matrix space. Every small normalized period is represented in one base chart; transport its lattice by an integral isometry and approximate its linear change by that dense subset. If d < d^{′}, each image has real 2d^{′}-dimensional measure zero: on compact coordinate balls the map is Lipschitz, and subdivision into cubes gives a total covering volume bounded by a constant times ε^{2d′−2d}. A countable union of such images cannot contain an open ball. Hence d^{′}= d.

The resulting holomorphic map from a base chart to period coordinates is injective between equal di-mensional complex domains, and its derivative is invertible at every point. The source proves this last fact analytically. In dimension one, the first nonzero Taylor term of an injective germ has degree one, since degree greater than one gives multiple nearby inverse values. Induct on dimension: if the derivative is nonzero, a nonzero scalar component provides one coordinate and slicing by it reduces to the injective germ on a codimension-one slice. If the derivative were zero, the determinant of the derivative could not vanish identically nearby, since a holomorphic map of deficient rank on an open set cannot be injective. On its zero locus the nonzero-derivative case already proved forces the entire derivative to vanish. A nontrivial holomorphic zero locus has a regular hypersurface piece: take a derivative of minimal order nonzero along its zero set and apply the implicit function theorem. Restriction to this positive dimensional piece has zero derivative, hence is constant on small connected slices, contradicting injectivity. This proves the assertion in all dimensions.

The inverse function theorem now identifies a smaller base chart holomorphically with the normalized period chart. Thus the exponential attached to the given periods is the jointly holomorphic chart exponential composed with this holomorphic linear change. There is no remaining ambiguity: an automorphism of a polarized abelian variety preserving a full level N ≥3 is trivial. Its action on the lattice lies in a finite group, by positivity, and in the principal congruence subgroup of level N, which has no nontrivial torsion. This is seen without a finiteness assumption on a congruence group. Choose an odd prime q | N, or, if none exists, use 4 | N. A nontrivial finite-order matrix in this subgroup has a power of prime order r, still in the subgroup. Write it 1+q^{s}A, with an entry of A not divisible by q, where s ≥1 for odd q and s ≥2 for q = 2. If r ̸= q, the first binomial term in its r-th power has valuation s and every later term has larger valuation. If r = q, the first term has valuation s + 1 and every later term has valuation at least s+2. Either conclusion contradicts that the power is one. The finite order therefore was trivial. The above argument applies to the pulled family and gives exactly its period uniformization.
