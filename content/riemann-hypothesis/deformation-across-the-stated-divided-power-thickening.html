10.7 Deformation across the stated divided-power thickening

Background fact 10.25 (Grothendieck’s general abelian deformation theorem). Let S_{0} ↪→S be a thickening with locally nilpotent divided powers. Abelian schemes over S are equivalent to pairs consisting of an abelian scheme A_{0}/S_{0} and a locally direct summand of its crystalline H^{1}(A_{0}/S) extending the Hodge submodule ω_{A0}. Morphisms are exactly the morphisms on S_{0} whose crystalline pullback preserves these submodules. This is the named deformation theorem in [19, Chapter V,§4,p. 116]. The original French text on pp. 114– 115 explicitly uses the general nilpotent crystalline site: there is no privileged prime and no assumption that some prime be nilpotent in the base. It also identifies its crystal at a lift with H_{dR}^{1} and gives the natural Hodge exact sequence.

Proposition 10.26 (The precise three deformation clauses). The source’s GrothendieckDeformation holds. Its specialization GrothendieckMessing holds as well, with the source’s dual cohomology, annihilator filtra-tion, and reduction maps.

Proof. Let R → R_{0} be the stated surjection with kernel I and divided powers γ. The source’s IsNilDP requires that for some N every product ∏_{j}γ_{aj}(x_{j}) with x_{j} ∈I and ∑ a_{j} ≥N vanish. This is precisely vanishing of the Nth divided-power filtration ideal. In particular take every a_{j} = 1; then I^{N} = 0. Thus Spec R_{0} ↪→ Spec R is an object of the nilpotent crystalline site in Fact 10.25. The uniform affine bound in the source is stronger than the theorem’s locally nilpotent condition.

We describe its cohomology convention. The source category consists of affine test schemes mapping to A_{0} and their nilpotent divided-power affine thickenings, with coefficient functor sending one to its thickening ring. Its category cochain complex is the usual product complex on composable chains of morphisms, with alternating faces. Its cohomology is crystalline cohomology of the structure sheaf. Indeed affine objects form a basis of this crystalline site. The structure sheaf is acyclic on them, since restriction to the opens of an affine thickening is its quasi-coherent structure sheaf. Resolve a cover by its intersections and then by

these composable chains. The resulting double complex computes sheaf cohomology in one direction and the displayed category complex in the other; the higher affine rows vanish. This also proves naturality for the source pullback maps. With zero divided powers the crystalline–de Rham comparison identifies it with ordinary algebraic de Rham cohomology, as in the original text, pp. 114–115.

For an abelian scheme all degree-one classes are primitive. The degree-one Künneth decomposition for its square is H^{1}(A) ⊕H^{1}(A). Restricting multiplication A × A →A to the two axes is the identity, so its pullback on H^{1} is c →→(c, c), the sum of the two projection pullbacks. This is exactly the source condition prim; pulling back along arbitrary pairs of maps gives its universal version. The same computation holds for the evaluated crystalline crystal and commutes with comparison. Consequently its Crys and HdR are the duals of the usual crystalline and de Rham degree-one modules. In particular

HdR(A) = H_{dR}^{1} (A/R)^{∨}, Fil(A) = Ann(ω_{A}).

The Hodge modules and their quotients are finite projective. Over the affine base their locally split exact sequence therefore has a global complement.

The crystal comparison of a lift A/R with A_{0} is an isomorphism. On cohomology it is the source’s liftMor.pull, since both are the restriction map induced by the same divided-power morphism. Its dual is crysToDR. The Hodge filtration is a direct summand and reduces onto that of A_{0}, by the natural exact sequence and base-change compatibility on p. 115 of the cited text. Taking annihilators of its two finite projective terms gives exactly the source IsAdmissible condition on its dual filtration. This proves both clauses of Canonical.

Its annihilator F^{⊥}⊂H_{dR}^{1} (A^{′}) is a direct summand reducing onto ω_{A0}. Apply the essential surjectivity in Fact 10.25. It produces a lift A with that cohomolog-ical filtration. For a direct summand of a finite projective module, double annihilation returns the original summand: split off a complement and take duals. Hence the homological filtration of A is precisely the preimage of F under the reference crysToDR map, as asserted in LiftObj. In one direction evaluate an an-nihilating functional on the pulled-back vector. In the other use the same evaluation and double annihilation for direct summands. Proof. The characteristic-free Riemann–Roch and index theorems for abelian varieties give χ(L)^{2} = deg(ϕ_{L}) and, for ample L, vanishing of H^{i}(A, L) for i > 0. The precise results are [20, Theorems 9.11 and 9.14, Proposition 9.18(iv), pp. 131–135]. This is also the specified section module, rather than merely an isomorphic numerical invariant with no identified model. The definition LPow pulls back L^{⊗a}, and SecM consists of local functions satisfying its transition equations. Gluing gives an invertible linear map

Its inverse restricts a section to the given trivializing cover. The scalar structure is pullback from the first projection, which becomes the ordinary k action when that factor is Spec k.

For the torsion assertion, choose an ample symmetric line bundle M on A, replacing any ample bundle N by N ⊗[−1]^{∗}N. The theorem of the cube gives [n]^{∗}M ≃M^{⊗n2}: [21, Corollary 2.12, p. 21]. In the source, arrows from the unit object are precisely these points, and the point-group power x^{n}= 1 is [n]x = 0. The hypothesis excludes n = 0. For g = 0 the connected smooth proper group is a point and both sides of the formula are one.

Proposition 10.28 (The precise ray-field input). The source field RayClassFieldSt holds, with the positivity at all real embeddings required by its definition.

Proof. Let K be a number field and 0 ̸= 𝔪 ⊂𝒪_{K}. Set

The existence theorem gives the ray class field for this modulus, with Artin isomorphism

I^{𝔪}/P_{𝔐,1}−−→ Gal(L/K).

Here I^{𝔪} is the group of fractional ideals with zero valuations at primes dividing 𝔪; P_{𝔐,1} consists of principal ideals generated by x with x ≡1 at the finite modulus and x > 0 at every real embedding. The extension is finite abelian, unramified at finite primes outside 𝔪, and Art(𝔭) is arithmetic Frobenius there. These are the existence and Artin-normalization conclusions of [23, Chapter V, Theorem 3.6 and the paragraph following it, pp. 158–159; proof in Chapter VII, §9, pp. 223–226].

We identify the source quotient, including its denominator condition. A unit of the fractional-ideal monoid is precisely a nonzero fractional ideal, and its count is the usual prime valuation. Thus its idealsPrimeTo is I^{𝔪}. A source RayPair (a, b) says that a, b ̸= 0, both principal ideals are coprime to 𝔪, a −b ∈𝔪, and σ(a)σ(b) > 0 for every real embedding σ. Consequently x = a/b is a unit at all primes dividing 𝔪, satisfies x ≡1 there, and is totally positive.

Conversely suppose x has those three properties. The denominator ideal D = {b ∈𝒪_{K} : bx ∈𝒪_{K}} is nonzero and coprime to 𝔪. For proper 𝔪, the Chinese remainder theorem supplies b ∈ D with b ≡ 1

(mod 𝔪); this b is nonzero. If 𝔪 = 𝒪_{K}, choose any nonzero b ∈D. Put a = bx. At a prime dividing 𝔪, both a and b have valuation zero, and a−b = b(x−1) has valuation at least that of 𝔪. Since a−b is integral, these local inequalities give a −b ∈𝔪. Finally σ(a)σ(b) = σ(x)σ(b)^{2}> 0. Thus (a, b) is a source RayPair. Its rayUnits is exactly the subgroup just described, and mapping to principal fractional ideals identifies rayPrincipal with P_{𝔐,1}. The displayed Artin map therefore has exactly the source’s domain.

For clarity, this also proves the actual predicate NT.RayField, rather than only its quotient formulation. In an abelian extension every prime above an unramified 𝔭 gives the same Frobenius. On residue rings its action is z →→z^{N𝔭}, exactly the congruence in IsFrob. The inertia subgroup is trivial, exactly UnramIn. Since fractional ideals prime to 𝔪 are generated by the primes outside 𝔪, their Frobenius images generate the finite Galois group. Their subgroup closure is consequently the entire group.

For a nonzero integral ideal I prime to 𝔪, unique factorization and the now unramified local factors give

heckeIdeal(L, χ, I) = χ Art([I]) .

The source’s finite-support product is the ordinary finite prime-factor product: exponents at all other primes are zero. Characters into ℂ^{×} separate a finite abelian group. Indeed write it as a product of finite cyclic groups; a nonidentity coordinate is detected by sending its cyclic generator to a primitive root of the cor-responding order and the other generators to one. Hence all these values are one if and only if [I] = 1 in the ray class group. By the quotient identification this is equivalent to I = (a/b) for a RayPair (a, b), or, equivalently, I(b) = (a). Proposition 10.29 (Holomorphic sections and the source atlas). The source field SerreHolomorphicSections holds for every supplied AnRead atlas.

Proof. Let Y /ℂ be smooth and projective and let B be the supplied invertible sheaf. Serre’s comparison theorem, [24, §12, Theorem 1, p. 19, with q = 0], states that restriction gives a bijection H^{0}(Y, B) −→ H^{0}(Y^{an}, B^{an}). The invertible sheaf is coherent since Y is noetherian. Smoothness makes Y reduced, so Serre’s variety formulation applies. If Y has several components, they are finitely many disjoint smooth projective varieties; apply the theorem to each and take their product.

It remains to identify its target with the target actually specified. An AlgChart is an open partial home-omorphism whose coordinate functions are evaluations of regular functions giving an etale map to affine space. In the usual analytification this map is locally biholomorphic: its Jacobian is invertible, and the complex inverse function theorem applies. Since the supplied homeomorphism agrees with these coordi-nate evaluations, its local inverse is precisely that holomorphic inverse. Its restriction to every sufficiently small open is therefore an ordinary holomorphic coordinate chart. Conversely, the charts cover all complex points. Differentiability on the open target domains in HoloOn is thus equivalent to ordinary holomorphicity, independently of the chosen atlas.

The compatibility in fibSec is s_{i} = g_{ij}s_{j} on the overlap of the trivializations of B. Accordingly a tuple in holSec glues to a unique analytic section of B^{an}. There is an additional detail in this model: each s_{i} is a function on all complex points, and fibSec requires it to be zero outside its trivializing open. An analytic section therefore recovers exactly one such tuple by extending its local coefficient functions by zero. No holomorphicity across the boundary of that open is required by HoloOn. These operations are inverse. The analogous algebraic gluing identifies B.Sec with H^{0}(Y, B), and the source’s secVal is exactly restriction/evaluation, with that same zero extension. Conjugating Serre’s bijection by these two identified gluing maps gives the claimed bijection, including equality of all the tuple entries outside their domains.

Proposition 10.30 (Both formal algebraization clauses). The fields FormalAlgebraisation.Morphisms and FormalAlgebraisation.Schemes hold with the source’s stated noetherian and completeness hypotheses.

Proof. Let R be noetherian and complete and separated for its I-adic topology, and put R_{e} = R/I^{e+1}. Thus R is an adic noetherian ring with ideal of definition I. For a proper X/R and separated finite-type Z/R, the original comparison theorem [25, III_{1}, Theorem 5.4.1 and Scholie 5.4.2, pp. 156–157] identifies Hom_{R}(X, Z) with the compatible morphisms on the reductions modulo I^{e+1}. The target need not be proper. In the source it is locally of finite type and quasi-compact, hence of finite type.

For each e, the specified pullback square identifies Y_{e} with X ×_{R} R_{e} by a unique isomorphism com-muting with its two projections. Under these identifications the given β_{e} is the canonical reduction map: its composition with X is specified, and its composition with Spec R_{e+1} is forced, because that closed im-mersion is a monomorphism and both composites agree over Spec R. The source compatibility for f_{e} is therefore exactly the compatibility in Scholie 5.4.2. The resulting unique R-morphism g : X → Z satis-fies b_{e}g = f_{e}, as required. Here each Y_{e} is proper over the noetherian ring R_{e}, hence noetherian and of finite type. The algebraization theorem [25, III_{1}, Theorem 5.4.5, pp. 157–158] now gives a projective X/R and an ample invertible sheaf M whose completions are (𝔜, 𝔑). Proposition 10.31 (Extension of semiabelian homomorphisms). The source’s SemiAbelianHomExtension holds over its arbitrary normal noetherian base.

Proof. The exact extension theorem is [26, §2, Lemma 1, pp. 10–11]: a homomorphism between two semi-abelian schemes on a dense open of a normal base extends uniquely over the base. The original proof takes the closure of its graph, obtains a proper projection with singleton fibers by the valuation case, and uses normality to identify this projection with the source.

We check its scope here. The source normality predicate says that every affine coordinate ring is an integrally closed domain locally on each irreducible component; it is the usual normality condition. Its IsSemiAbelian requires smoothness, separatedness, quasi-compactness, geometric connectedness, and on each geometric fiber an extension of an abelian variety by a torus. Smoothness supplies local finite presentation; quasi-compactness over the noetherian base makes the morphisms finite type. The rank of the invariant differentials is locally constant on the base, so one can apply the theorem on the open and closed components of constant relative dimension. Normal noetherian schemes have finitely many disjoint normal integral components; the resulting maps glue without an overlap issue. The dense open meets each of them.

The source IsMonHom is the assertion that the morphism respects the given multiplication and unit, so the supplied f is precisely the theorem’s homomorphism. Its unique extension F is a morphism over the base

and respects those operations; alternatively their two composites agree on the dense open, and separatedness gives agreement globally. Restriction of F is f as a morphism, which is the asserted equality under the source’s pullback functor. Every other source witness is another such homomorphism extension and is equal to F by uniqueness. This proves its entire existence-and-uniqueness statement.

Proposition 10.32 (The degree-one de Rham clauses). For an abelian scheme A/ Spec R of relative dimen-sion g, the first de Rham module is finite projective of rank 2g, every degree-one class is primitive, and the source’s AbelianDeRham.Hodge, AbelianDeRham.BaseChange and AbelianDeRham.Rigid clauses hold.

Proof. The crystal and exact sequence in [19, Chapter V, §4, pp. 114–115] give, naturally and compatibly with arbitrary base change,

The cohomology and reduction maps here are identified with the source maps by the affine crystalline/de Rham comparison in Proposition 10.26.

The multiplication map restricts to the identity along each axis, hence its pullback sends c to (c, c). Pull back this equality along any pair of maps to A. This is exactly the universally quantified condition prim; consequently AllPrim holds.

It follows that the source primDR is the full H_{dR}^{1} and PrimitiveSubmodule is ω_{A}.
