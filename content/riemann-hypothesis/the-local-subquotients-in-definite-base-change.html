Background fact 4.21 (Unitary local products). For a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero, normalized parabolic induction of a finite list of irreducible unitarizable representations of general linear groups is irreducible and unitarizable.

This exact assertion follows from Tadić’s classification: Theorem D(ii) writes each factor as a product of the specified unitary building blocks, and Theorem D(i) makes the concatenated product irreducible and unitary [6, pp. 338–339, Theorem D]. The nonarchimedean and characteristic-zero hypotheses hold here because E_{w} is a finite extension of a p-adic field. The factors are unitarizable because they are local factors of the discrete automorphic representations V_{i}, realized in the unitary discrete spectrum. Thus this is the full unitary-product assertion required by the bridge, without a genericity or a temperedness assumption.

Proposition 4.22 (Removing the auxiliary local subquotients). The preliminary isobaric base-change state-ment just specified implies the source’s DefiniteN: the same conclusions hold for I_{w} itself, in place of P_{w}, at every place.

Proof. Keep the ranks, discrete representations, local factors and infinitesimal characters supplied by the preliminary statement. For a fixed w, Fact 4.21 makes I_{w} irreducible. By the definition of subquotient there is an invariant subspace S ⊆ I_{w} and a surjective intertwiner S → P_{w}. The target is nonzero, so S ̸= 0. Irreducibility gives S = I_{w}. The kernel of the intertwiner is an invariant subspace of I_{w}; it cannot be all of I_{w}, since the map is surjective onto a nonzero space. It is therefore zero. The intertwiner is an isomorphism.

An isomorphism of representations carries fixed vectors to fixed vectors. It also commutes with every finite Hecke operator, because it commutes with the action of every group element and hence with the in-tegrals, or finite double-coset sums, defining those operators. Consequently it preserves sphericality and all the Satake eigenvalue identities. Composing or inverting this isomorphism in the local-factor intertwiner preserves the split local-factor assertion as well. Transfer the three local assertions from P_{w} to I_{w} in this way. The construction applies independently at every w and does not change any quantifier on the inert-place data. All the other assertions concern n_{i}, V_{i}, σ_{i,w}, m_{i,τ} and were retained unchanged. They give precisely the nine fields of the definite base-change output.

Lemma 4.23 (A finite candidate list works at every inert place). Fix π and a set S of at most one specified split place. Suppose there is a finite list of spectral candidates D_{1}, . . . , D_{k} such that each has the required infinitesimal characters, matches π at all but finitely many split places, and matches at every place of S. Suppose moreover that for each finite set T of places, one of these same candidates has the required inert Satake identities at every unramified nonsplit place of T. Then one candidate has the required identities at every unramified nonsplit place.

Proof. If none did, choose for each j an unramified nonsplit place w_{j} where D_{j} fails. The set T = {w_{1}, . . . , w_{k}} is finite. The hypothesis gives an index j whose candidate works throughout T, in particular at its own w_{j}, a contradiction. This also rules out the empty candidate list: apply the finite-set hypothesis to T = ∅ to obtain an index. The chosen candidate retains its infinitesimal and split-place assertions since those hold for every member of the list.
