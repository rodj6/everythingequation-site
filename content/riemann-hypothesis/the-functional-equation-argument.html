Proof. Let s satisfy the three hypotheses on the left of (1). If Re s > 1/2, (5) contradicts ζ(s) = 0. If Re s = 1/2, the conclusion already holds. It remains to exclude Re s < 1/2.

First s ̸= 0, since otherwise ζ(s) = −1/2 by (i). Set w = 1 −s. Then Re w = 1 − Re s > 1/2 and w ̸= 1. For every n ∈ ℕ, the real part of −n is nonpositive, whereas Re w > 0; hence w ̸= −n. These verify every hypothesis of (ii), so (2) applies. Its left side is zero.

The factors 2 and (2π)^{−w} are nonzero. The factor Γ(w) is nonzero by (iii), since Re w > 0. The factor ζ(w) is nonzero by (5), since Re w > 1/2 and w ̸= 1. To handle the remaining factor, suppose cos(πw/2) = 0. By (iv), πw/2 = (2k+1)π/2 for an integer k. Cancelling the nonzero real number π gives w = 2k + 1, and therefore s = −2k. If k < 0, then k ≤−1 and Re s = −2k ≥2, contrary to Re s < 1/2. If k = 0, then s = 0, already excluded. If k > 0, set n = k −1 ∈ℕ; then s = −2(n + 1), contrary to the nontrivial-zero hypothesis. Thus the cosine factor is also nonzero. The right side of (2) is consequently a product of nonzero complex numbers, contradicting its zero left side. This excludes the remaining case.
