10.1 Matching the project model

The following identifications specify the conversion required to apply Proposition 10.5 to the project, rather than merely replacing one theorem name by another.

Its PDivGroup presents G[p^{n}] as the spectrum of a finite projective commutative Hopf O-algebra A_{n} of rank p^{nh}. The surjective transition homomorphisms are the closed inclusions of the p^{n}-torsion subgroup; the equality of their kernels with the torsion ideals is precisely that assertion on coordinate algebras. Thus these are exactly the finite-group and kernel conditions in Tate’s definition [8, §2.1, p. 161]. Their associated group is the resulting p-divisible group, with its p-adic formal cotangent module. The rank-zero level is the trivial group: its rank-one augmented algebra is O, as the unit and counit split it and their finite projective complement has rank zero at every prime.

Lemma 10.7 (Freeness of the completed cotangent model). For this system over O = O_{C}, put W_{n} = ω_{G[p}n_{]} = I_{n}/I_{n}^{2}, n ≥1. Then

W_{n+1}/p^{n}W_{n+1} ≃W_{n}, lim W_{n} ≃O^{d}

for a fixed finite integer d. Consequently the source’s completed ω_{G} is finite free and torsion free.

Proof. The differential of multiplication by an integer a on the cotangent space of a commutative group is multiplication by a. Indeed, the differential of the group law at the identity is addition: restricting it to either coordinate gives the identity, which determines that linear map on the direct sum. Iteration gives the assertion for positive a. Since [p^{n}] is zero on G[p^{n}], it follows that p^{n}W_{n} = 0.

Formation of invariant cotangent modules commutes with base change: the augmentation ideal modulo its square is equivalently the module representing derivations at the identity, and the universal derivation commutes with tensoring the base ring. Thus W_{n} equals the cotangent module of G[p^{n}] over O/p^{n}. This is a truncated Barsotti–Tate group of level n. The finite-level cotangent theorem says that its invariant cotangent module is finite locally free over O/p^{n}; the precise cited statement is Illusie [9, Corollary 2.2.5, p. 164], applied to the implication from a truncated Barsotti–Tate group to its two locally free cotangent modules. Its hypothesis p^{n}= 0 holds on this base. Since O/p^{n} is local, W_{n} is finite free over that ring.

The transition identifies G[p^{n}] with the kernel of [p^{n}] on G[p^{n+1}]. For a kernel of group schemes, the cotangent sequence at the identity is right exact. Here it is

To see the right exactness directly, present the kernel by its fiber product with the identity section. Its coordi-nate algebra is the corresponding tensor product, and the universal rule for differentials of a tensor product gives the cokernel of the two cotangent maps. The identity section has zero relative cotangent module; the remaining map is the differential of [p^{n}] computed above. This proves the first displayed isomorphism.

Reduction modulo p shows that the ranks of all W_{n} are one fixed d. Choose a basis at level one. Lift it successively along the surjective transition maps. Any such lifts at level n + 1 form a basis: relative to an existing basis of the free module W_{n+1}, their determinant is a unit modulo p, so is a unit in the local ring O/p^{n+1}. The adjugate identity then inverts the matrix. We obtain compatible basis isomorphisms W_{n} ≃ (O/p^{n})^{d}. Taking inverse limits and using the assumed p-adic completeness and separation of O gives lim_{←−}W_{n} ≃O^{d}. An element of O^{d} killed by a nonzero element of the domain O has every coordinate zero. This proves the last assertion.

The source’s Tate module uses O-valued points, whereas the published theorem writes C-valued points. These are equal at every finite level. Indeed, a homomorphism A_{n} →C sends each element to one integral over O, because A_{n} is finite over O. A root z of a monic polynomial with coefficients in O has valuation at most one: if v(z) > 1, its leading term has strictly larger valuation than every lower term and cannot cancel their sum. Thus its image lies in O. The factorization is unique since O ↪→ C is injective. These factorizations commute with the transition maps, so the two inverse-limit Tate modules agree.

For an augmented coordinate algebra, the invariant cotangent module is I/I^{2}, where I is the augmen-tation ideal. The source takes the compatible inverse limit of these modules at finite level. This is the completed invariant cotangent module of the associated p-adic formal group. Its Lie is the O-linear dual of this completed cotangent module. In particular one must not take the tangent space after passing first to the characteristic-zero torsion sheaf, where each finite level is etale and would give the wrong answer.

Finally the source’s dlogLinear sends c ⊗u to c times the pullback of dt/t under the Cartier character G →µ_{p}∞ represented by u. This is α_{G}∨ in the publication. Its hodgeTateLinear first uses biduality to regard a Tate vector as a character of G^{∨}, and then takes this same pullback. This is α_{G}. The description of α_{G} on page 99 of the cited theorem is exactly this construction. Biduality makes the pairing the Cartier pairing used for the transpose arrow, and Lemma 10.6 fixes its scalar normalization for every primitive root system allowed in the source.
