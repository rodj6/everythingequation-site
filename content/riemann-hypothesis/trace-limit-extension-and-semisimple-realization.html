Fix F, c_{i}, ε satisfying the original inequality, and fix a polynomial Q in finitely many group variables g_{1}, . . . , g_{s}. Proof. Every trace of an a-dimensional representation satisfies t(1) = a, t(gh) = t(hg), and the degree-a+1 trace identity ∑

These are integer polynomial identities in group-indexed trace values. In (198) apply the corresponding identity polynomial. It vanishes for every Tr ρ_{i}, and its value at t is bounded by every positive error. Hence it vanishes at t. Extending t linearly to the group algebra gives a degree-a pseudocharacter. Here the char-acteristic is zero, so a! is invertible.

The algebraic realization theorem for pseudocharacters now gives a semisimple r : Γ → GL_{a}(ℚ_{ℓ}) with trace t. One precise reference is Chenevier, The p-adic analytic space of pseudocharacters of a profi-nite group and pseudorepresentations over arbitrary rings, arXiv:0809.0415v2, Proposition 1.27 and The-orem 2.12: over a characteristic-zero algebra, pseudocharacters correspond to determinants, and over an algebraically closed field such a determinant has a unique semisimple realization. We use only this algebraic statement, for the group algebra over ℚ_{ℓ}.

Continuity is checked separately. Let A be the algebra spanned by r(Γ). Since r is semisimple, A is a product of matrix algebras, acting on the representation with positive integer multiplicities. The trace pairing (u, v) →→ Tr(uv) on A is nondegenerate: on each matrix factor it is a nonzero integer multiple of the usual matrix trace pairing. Choose a basis r(g_{1}), . . . , r(g_{s}) from the spanning set. Its Gram matrix has entries t(g_{i}g_{j}) ∈L and is invertible. The coefficients of r(g) in this basis are obtained by applying its inverse to (t(gg_{j}))_{j}. They are continuous L-valued functions of g. Finitely many entries of the basis matrices lie in one finite extension L^{′}/L. Every entry of every r(g) lies in L^{′}, and is continuous. Inversion is continuous because r(g)^{−1}= r(g^{−1}). Thus r is continuous as a GL_{a}(L^{′})-valued representation. Algebraic uniqueness gives the stated uniqueness.

Proposition 7.98 (Finiteness at definite unitary level). For the definite unitary group G = U(X) over F = E^{+}, the adelic quotient G(F)\G(𝔸_{F} ) is compact and has finite invariant volume. For every compact open K ⊂G(𝔸_{F,f}), the class set G(F)\G(𝔸_{F} )/(G(F_{∞})K) is finite.

Proof. After extension to an algebraic closure, G is GL_{n}; it is thus connected and reductive. Its character group is generated by the determinant, and the nontrivial automorphism of E/F acts on this group by inver-sion. Hence it has no nontrivial F-rational characters. It also has no nontrivial rational unipotents: at a real place it is a compact unitary group, where a unipotent unitary matrix is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues one and therefore is the identity. The real embedding of the rational matrix entries is injective.

Borel, Some finiteness properties of adele groups over number fields, Theorem 5.6(i),(ii), now gives finite invariant volume and compactness; the rational-character and unipotent hypotheses have just been checked.

Theorem 5.1 gives the finite class set for the standard integral finite-adelic subgroup K_{0}. For general K, the intersection K ∩K_{0} has finite index in K_{0}, since it is open and K_{0} is compact. Refining each of finitely many K_{0}-classes by those finitely many cosets gives a finite class set at K ∩K_{0}, hence at K.

Proposition 7.99 (A common space for coefficient conjugates). The coefficient-conjugate automorphic forms used for one definite-unitary eigenform lie in a common finite-dimensional complex space.

Proof. Choose a common compact open finite level fixing the original form f. Its finite-dimensional infinite-type span expresses its infinite translates using finitely many polynomial matrix coefficients. These are polynomials in the coordinate functions g →→(g_{τ})_{ij}, allowing every complex embedding τ : E →ℂ. To recall why no inverse coordinates are missing, an irreducible compact-unitary highest weight can be shifted to a nonnegative weight by a determinant power. Its matrix coefficients then occur in tensor powers of the standard representation and the inverse determinant. On the compact unitary group the inverse determinant is the conjugate determinant, provided by the conjugate embedding coordinates already allowed. Thus a finite degree bound N suffices for the finitely many matrix coefficients of f.

Coefficient conjugation by σ ∈ Aut ℂ permutes the embeddings τ →→ στ and conjugates the scalar coefficients. It preserves this same degree bound N. The finite-level invariance is preserved on finite-adelic points by the equality with σf, and on all adelic points by rational invariance, density and continuity. There are only finitely many monomials of degree at most N in these finitely many coordinate functions; write P_{N} for their finite-dimensional span.

By Proposition 7.98, choose finitely many class representatives S. Let M consist of left-rational-invariant, right-level-invariant functions whose restriction to each sG(F_{∞}), s ∈ S, belongs to P_{N}.

Restriction defines an injective linear map

It is injective because every adelic point is a rational translate of a representative times an infinite point and a level element. The target is finite-dimensional, so M is finite-dimensional. The conjugate polynomial calculation puts every coefficient-conjugate form in M: on rational infinite points it is exactly the conjugated original polynomial, and equality extends to the infinite group by density and continuity. This is the common space required.

Proposition 7.100 (The common coefficient field). For a fixed algebraic automorphic representation, weight and level as in the source, the eigenvalues of the entire family of rational Hecke operators lie in one number field, hence under ι^{−1} in one finite extension of ℚ_{ℓ}.

Proof. Choose a nonzero level-fixed eigenform f. There is a finite-adelic point g_{0} where f(g_{0}) ̸= 0. Otherwise left invariance under rational points and density of rational times finite-adelic points, followed by continuity, would make f zero everywhere. For every field automorphism σ of ℂ, the coefficient-conjugation construction gives an automorphic form f_{σ}, agreeing with σ ∘f on finite-adelic points. In particular f_{σ}(g_{0}) = σ(f(g_{0})) ̸= 0. Rational Hecke coefficients commute with this construction, so f_{σ} is a joint eigenvector with eigencharacter σθ.

Joint eigenvectors with distinct eigencharacters are linearly independent. Indeed, in a shortest nontrivial linear relation, choose an operator separating one eigencharacter from another, apply that operator minus one of its eigenvalues, and obtain a shorter nontrivial relation. This is impossible. Since the forms f_{σ} lie in a common finite-dimensional space, only finitely many distinct tuples σθ can occur.

Each coordinate of θ is therefore algebraic over ℚ: a transcendental complex number has infinitely many images under complex field automorphisms, by extending a transcendence basis. The field generated by all coordinates has only finitely many embeddings into ℂ, since every such embedding extends to a complex field automorphism and is determined by that tuple. An algebraic extension of ℚ with a bounded finite number of embeddings is finite: otherwise its finite subextensions would have unbounded degrees and hence unbounded numbers of embeddings. Thus all coordinates lie in a single number field. The closure of its image under ι^{−1} is the completion at one place over ℓ, a finite extension of ℚ_{ℓ}.

The common finite-dimensional space in this proof is supplied by the conjugate-form construction at the prescribed finite level. It is important that the level is common: separate finite-dimensional spaces with unbounded dimensions would give no bound on the orbit of the whole eigencharacter.

The next transition uses congruent odd-rank seeds to remove the weight and auxiliary local-type restric-tions. Its finite-control parameter includes a finite set Y of places, a congruent automorphic representation U^{′}, and a Hecke eigensystem. The congruence assertion controls both finitely many ordinary Hecke oper-ators and the scaled Satake power sums at the places in Y . This joint control is essential: convergence of unramified Hecke values alone does not prove the local assertions above ℓ.

For each such parameter the odd seed gives a continuous semisimple rank-a representation. The source constructs a dense set of Frobenius operators where its traces equal the chosen Hecke eigenvalues. For any finite collection of parameters, the intersection remains dense: the seed trace comparisons hold on open

dense sets and Chebotarev supplies Frobenius elements after excluding the union of their finite exceptional sets. The trace-limit theorem is then applied to the jointly approximated eigensystems. Its conclusion is one continuous degree-a trace function, realized by one semisimple representation r. To check a local condition at v, the proof restricts to the subfamily with Y = {v}. The same limit trace is used for every such subfamily. Closedness of the relevant local conditions, together with the prescribed common Hodge weights and the joint power-sum identities, transfers the seed conclusions to this same r. Thus the source does not interchange ∀v ∃r_{v} and ∃r ∀v.
