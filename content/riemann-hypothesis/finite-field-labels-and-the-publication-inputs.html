For each polynomial label P, choose a root x_{P} . Its minimal polynomial is P. The finite-field Frobenius orbit has length d and is the full set of roots of P; in particular x_{P} ̸= 0 and x^{p}_{P}^{d−1}= 1. Thus θ_{xP} is regular, and we set τ(P) = χ_{θxP} . The asserted cuspidality and degree now follow from the corresponding parts of the classical input.

We check the value formula with its quantification over all roots, as the source does. Let Q be the irreducible characteristic polynomial of y. It is monic of degree d, and its constant term is nonzero because y is invertible. Every root z of Q therefore satisfies z^{N} = 1 and lies in the embedded copy of K. Choose its nonzero preimage k ∈K^{×}. Injectivity of the embedding shows that Q(k) = 0, so the classical character formula applies at k. Its summands are θ_{xP} (k^{pi}) = Θ_{xP} (z^{pi}).

Any other root x of P is x^{p}_{P}^{j} for some j < d. From the cyclic-generator definition one has

Both lists z, z^{p}, . . . , z^{pd−1} and z^{pj}, (z^{pj})^{p}, . . . , (z^{pj})^{pd−1} enumerate the roots of Q without multiplicity. Their sums under Θ_{xP} therefore agree. This is the source’s comparison of the two root-multiset sums, and accounts for the independence of that choice.

If τ(P) = τ(P^{′}), the classical orbit criterion gives θ_{xP ′} = θ_{x}^{pj}_{P} for some j < d. The character-root

bijection then gives x_{P} ′ = x^{p}_{P}^{j}. This is a root of P, so P and P^{ ′} are the same monic minimal polynomial. Thus τ is injective.

Conversely, let χ be cuspidal irreducible. Choose a regular θ with χ = χ_{θ} and write θ = θ_{x} by the character-root bijection. Let P be the minimal polynomial of x and put e = deg P. Because x^{pd} = x, finite-field Frobenius gives e | d. Also e > 0. If e < d, then x^{pe} = x contradicts the regularity criterion at the two indices e and 0. Hence e = d. The element x is nonzero, so P(0) ̸= 0, making P a polynomial label. The chosen root x_{P} is a Frobenius conjugate of x. The character-root identity and the classical orbit criterion give χ = τ(P). This proves surjectivity and completes the construction.
