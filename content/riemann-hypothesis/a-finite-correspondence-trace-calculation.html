7.2 A finite-correspondence trace calculation

Proposition 7.2 (The holomorphic fixed-point formula). Let Z be a smooth projective complex scheme, purely of dimension d, and let c_{1}, c_{2} : Y → Z be finite etale morphisms. Suppose that their fixed-point scheme is finite and transverse. At a fixed point y, put z = c_{1}(y) = c_{2}(y), identify the tangent space at y with that at z by dc_{1}, and put A_{y} = dc_{2} (dc_{1})^{−1}. For every integer a ≥0,

[T^{a}] det(1 + TA_{y})

det(1 −A_{y})

The trace map in the left side is the finite-etale trace on differentials, after identifying pullbacks of differentials by etaleness. In particular its direction is from Y to Z along c_{1}.

Proof. We use the coherent Lefschetz–Verdier formula [36, Exposé III, Théorème 6.7, (6.7.2), and §6.8– 6.11]. Here the base is Spec ℂ, the coherent complex is the vector bundle Ω^{a}_{Z}, one correspondence is (c_{2}, c_{1}), and the other is the diagonal. Because c_{1} is finite étale, the finite-étale trace induces the canonical duality isomorphism c^{!}_{1}Ω^{a}_{Z} ≃c^{∗}_{1}Ω^{a}_{Z} ≃Ω_{Y}^{a}, which together with c^{∗}_{2}Ω^{a}_{Z} ≃Ω_{Y}^{a} defines the cohomological correspon-dence c^{∗}_{2}Ω^{a}_{Z} →c^{!}_{1}Ω_{Z}^{a}. The reversal in this ordered pair makes direct image of the correspondence equal to trace along c_{1} after pullback along c_{2}. Properness follows from projectivity and finiteness. The complexes are perfect because Z is smooth, and all base Tor-independence conditions hold over a field. The diagonal

is a regular immersion; the fixed-point transversality assumption makes its intersection with the correspon-dence Tor-independent and zero-dimensional. Thus the formula gives a sum of local residues, with no higher intersection terms. Proper coherent cohomology is finite-dimensional, so its alternating trace is defined.

We compute a residue without changing the global correspondence into a global self-map. Because c_{1} is etale, its completed local map at y identifies the local branch with the completed local ring at z. Choose regular parameters x_{1}, . . . , x_{d} there. On this branch c_{2} is expressed by power series F_{1}, . . . , F_{d}, and the fixed equations are x_{i} −F_{i}(x). Their linear coefficient matrix is 1 −A_{y}. Its determinant is nonzero by transversality, so these equations are again a regular system of parameters. The change-of-parameters rule for a zero-dimensional residue gives the factor det(1−A_{y})^{−1}. This is the local calculation of [36, Exposé III, §6.11–6.12], applied on the etale branch; it only uses the local power series, not the existence of a global map Z →Z.

The coefficient map on the fiber of Ω^{a}_{Z} is the a-th exterior power of the transpose of A_{y}. Its trace is [T^{a}] det(1 + TA_{y}): in a basis, both sides are the sum of the principal a-by-a minors. Multiplying by the residue factor gives the summand in (173). Summing the local terms proves the formula. For a > d both the exterior power and the coefficient vanish. (In the Lean formalization, the same formula is proved directly via finite covers of P^{d} and Čech differentials.)

Proposition 7.3 (Descent across components). The formula above is unchanged if the proof is first made for integral components of Z and Y , and then assembled by finite clopen descent.

Proof. A smooth scheme over a field is regular. In a noetherian regular scheme the irreducible components are disjoint: two components through one point would give two minimal primes in its regular local ring, whereas a regular local ring is a domain. Consequently the finitely many irreducible components of Z are integral and clopen. The same applies to ∐ Y , which is smooth and proper because it is finite etale over Z.

Write Z = ∐_{j}Z_{j}, Y =_{r}Y_{r}. An integral Y_{r} maps under each c_{i} into a single Z_{j}. Cohomology of a finite clopen union is the direct sum of the component cohomology groups. If e_{j} and p_{j} are its inclusion and projection, the trace of an endomorphism u is ∑_{j} Tr(p_{j}ue_{j}), as is seen by taking the concatenated component bases. A component Y_{r} whose two images lie in distinct Z_{j}’s contributes only an off-diagonal block and hence contributes zero trace. It also has no fixed point. Each remaining Y_{r} contributes to exactly one diagonal block, to which the integral formula applies. Its fixed points are exactly its share of the fixed points of Y . Restricting a scheme to a clopen neighborhood preserves local rings and tangent maps, so it preserves each local determinant. Addition over the diagonal blocks proves the assertion, including the sign (−1)^{b} in each cohomological degree.
