11 Resolution and principalization in the geometric input

The covolume argument uses two separate characteristic-zero statements. Their precise scheme-theoretic forms matter: principalization must respect an existing boundary, and resolution must make the entire inverse image of the nonsmooth locus a simple normal crossings divisor. We verify both forms here against the published resolution theorem.

Definition 11.1 (The boundary and blowup conventions). A reduced divisor E on a smooth k-scheme has simple normal crossings if, at every point x, its ideal on some affine neighbourhood is generated by a product of distinct members of a regular system of parameters of 𝒪_{X,x}. Outside E the product is empty and is 1. A smooth centre Z has normal crossings with E if, in such a neighbourhood, the ideal of Z is generated by a subset of the same parameters. Proof. Smoothness is locally of finite presentation. Quasi-compactness therefore makes X finite type over k; hence it is noetherian. Its quasi-coherent ideal is coherent. Since k has characteristic zero, it is perfect, so regular finite-type k-schemes are smooth over k. The source’s local boundary equations imply that every component of E is smooth and that any collection meeting at a point is given there by distinct parameters. There are finitely many components. Order them arbitrarily, thereby obtaining the boundary required in Fact 11.2. At points outside E, its ideal is the unit ideal; after shrinking to an affine open disjoint from E, the empty product verifies the same convention there.

Apply the marked-ideal theorem to (M, N, E, ℐ, d) = (X, X, E, ℐ, 1). Transversality to N = X is automatic. The hypothesis excluding whole components makes the ideal nonzero at each generic point. Proposition 11.4 (Resolution with the full nonsmooth inverse image). For a reduced separated finite-type k-scheme X over any characteristic-zero field, there is a finite sequence of blowups s : X^{′}→X such that X^{′} is smooth, s is an isomorphism over the smooth locus U of X/k, and the reduced inverse image of X \U has simple normal crossings. This is the full statement ResolutionCharZero used in the covolume package.

Source: Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/PublishedTheorems/Geometry2/ConclusionOfSmooth.lean:17--32,53--59.

Proof. Apply the first part of Fact 11.2 to obtain a smooth Y and a blowup sequence r : Y →X that is an isomorphism over U. Over a perfect field the smooth and regular loci agree for a finite-type reduced scheme. In particular U contains each generic point of X. Each centre in the stated resolution lies over the original singular locus; equivalently its sequence is the identity over U. The resulting Y has no component supported over X \U: blowing up a generically nonzero ideal is birational on each component, as is seen by restricting its Rees algebra to that generic point. Thus r^{−1}U is dense in every component of Y .

Let 𝒥 be the ideal of the reduced closed subset X \ U, and set ℐ = 𝒥 𝒪_{Y} . It is coherent and is the unit ideal on r^{−1}U, so it vanishes on no entire component of Y . Principalize it by Proposition 11.3, with empty initial boundary, obtaining t : Y^{′}→Y . If the smooth components have different dimensions, apply the proposition to each pure-dimensional open and closed part. There are finitely many such parts; the resulting blowups on their disjoint union give one finite sequence (insert identity steps on the other parts). This removes the pure-dimension restriction in that application.

The composite s = r ∘t is a finite blowup sequence: concatenate the two lists of centres. The source’s Resolution.IsBlowupSeq imposes no smooth-centre restriction, so in particular accepts this sequence. It is an isomorphism over U, since both r and t are isomorphisms there. Its reduced inverse image of X \ U is exactly the reduced support of ℐ𝒪_{Y} ′, which has simple normal crossings by principalization. The ambient Y^{′} is smooth, so all of its stalks are regular. This proves every conjunct of the source statement, including the condition on the whole inverse image, rather than only on the exceptional divisors listed by a chosen resolution algorithm.

Both results are used in the fifteen-input geometric package before the point-separation and Arakelov es-timates produce the covolume bounds. They supply slots 1 and 2 of that package. They do not by themselves prove the remaining Hodge, toroidal, metric or quantitative inputs.

Proof. Projectivity gives quasi-compactness and separatedness. The nonzero ideal on the integral scheme vanishes on no entire component, so Proposition 11.3 applies, with empty initial boundary. Its centres are smooth and contained over V (ℐ): on the inverse image of its complement the total ideal is a unit, and the controlled-transform relation in that proof makes the controlled ideal a unit as well. No admissible centre can meet that open set. Thus its sequence satisfies exactly the source’s IsSmoothBlowupSeq f (I.support).

Each blowup of an integral scheme in a generically nonzero coherent ideal is integral. On an integral affine chart its nonempty blowup charts are rings A[ℐ/f] inside Frac A, hence domains; their common dense open is the unchanged complement of the ideal, so their union is reduced and irreducible. That open is nonempty because the original ideal is nonzero. These observations also apply at each subsequent step. Each blowup is projective, being the relative Proj of its finitely generated Rees algebra. Its composite with the original projective morphism is therefore projective.

The resulting scheme is smooth by the proposition and birational to Y . Its function field has transcen-dence degree d over k. On an integral smooth scheme the relative dimension is locally constant, and its constant value equals this transcendence degree; therefore the new scheme is smooth of relative dimension d. The isomorphism on the complement, effective-Cartier property and simple normal crossings conclusion were already proved in Proposition 11.3. All extra conjuncts in this Hodge-package formulation are now verified.
