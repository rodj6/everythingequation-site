10.5 The arithmetic fields of the Hodge package

Proposition 10.21 (The volume fields and the fixed twist). The fields VolGe, VolNef and ArithHilbertSamuel of the source’s Hodge package hold with its extended-real volume definition.

Proof. The scheme presentation in these fields is the one treated in Proposition 10.18: X is integral, projec-tive and flat over Z, of dimension d = D + 1. Its coordinate line sections are ordinary global sections, and smallSec says that their squared norm is at most 1 at every complex point and embedding. This is the same sup-norm unit ball. The group H^{0}(X, L^{m}⊗N) is a finite torsion-free Z-module. Torsion freeness follows by restricting to the generic point of the integral flat X; finiteness follows from projectivity. Its real span has a genuine norm: a section vanishing at every complex point vanishes on the dense generic fiber, and hence is zero. Thus each unit ball meets the section lattice in a finite set. All encard values occurring eventually in arVol are ordinary finite positive integers. For m ≥ 1, the factor d!/m^{d} is exactly division by m^{d}/d!. Therefore its ENNReal.log and extended-real limsup agree with the usual finite arithmetic volume; the term m = 0 does not affect a limit at infinity.

For VolNef, the three fields of IsNef are semipositive curvature, nonnegative degrees on vertical curves, and nonnegative arithmetic degrees on horizontal curves. The last condition supplies one rational section; the product formula, or equivalently the uniqueness argument for curve heights, makes its computed degree independent of that choice. They are precisely arithmetic nefness. Fact 10.16 then identifies the volume with the canonical self-intersection, which equals the supplied real a by Proposition 10.18.

For VolGe, only finite-fiber nefness is stated separately. Semipositivity supplies generic nefness. To check this for a possibly singular generic fiber, pull the smooth metric to a resolution. Its curvature is non-negative on the inverse image of the smooth locus by the source’s chart Levi condition. This is a dense open subset; continuity of the smooth curvature extends nonnegativity to the resolution. For a curve downstairs, take a curve upstairs mapping onto it: intersect the inverse image with sufficiently many general ample hyperplanes until a multisection remains. Its degree is a positive integer times the downstairs degree. The upstairs degree is the integral of a nonnegative Chern form and so is nonnegative. This also persists over the algebraic closure. The finite-fiber condition and its base-extension argument in Proposition 10.17 therefore verify every geometric fiber of (AG.7). That formula gives exactly VolGe.

For ArProj, the morphism π : Y →X is proper by the closed-graph argument already used above. The same dimension and dominant generic point make it generically finite, of the positive degree e recorded by the residue-field degree. The algebraic projection formula pushes [Y ] to e[X]. At the complex places the finite map is, off its branch and exceptional loci, e local biholomorphic sheets, so the change-of-variables formula multiplies each pulled-back integral by e. The omitted sets have smaller dimension on a resolution and contribute zero. The divisor length calculation in Proposition 12.2 ensures the same multiplicities in the finite terms of the section recursion. Inducting in that recursion proves the arithmetic projection formula for the lines in question. Consequently their self- intersection is multiplied by e, and each mixed polynomial coefficient is multiplied by e. These are both clauses of ArProj.

An isometry preserves the norm of every rational section, its divisor, and its Chern form. The height recursion and its uniqueness therefore give ArIso. Its last change-of-variables assertion is simply multilin-earity: substituting X_{j} →→ ∑_{i}A_{ji}X_{i} expands the mixed tensor products of the integral combinations. Its degree vanishing is preserved by this homogeneous substitution. This proves all seven conjuncts of ArCalc.
