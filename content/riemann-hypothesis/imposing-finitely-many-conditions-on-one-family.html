For clarity we spell out the precise choice principle used by the family argument. A design for a class 𝒞^{′} and constant C_{0} provides a positive threshold such that, at every smaller positive accuracy and above every largeness parameter, there exist a family F ∈𝒞^{′} and H ≥0, together with the prescribed choice condition, a uniform bound on member sizes, and a uniform factorisation bound on its lattice determinants. The last three conditions are imposed at the time F is chosen; the bounds subsequently required of F may be imposed by raising the largeness parameter before making that choice.

Proposition 8.6 (Designs satisfying every eventual requirement). Suppose designs exist in 𝒞^{′}, and every such design belongs to a class 𝒟. Suppose P_{1}, . . . , P_{r} are uniformly eventual on 𝒟. Then, at every sufficiently small positive accuracy, a single design can be chosen which satisfies every P_{j}, with the same height constant and the original choice and size conditions.

Proof. Take the minimum of the design threshold and the finitely many thresholds of Lemma 8.5. At a smaller positive accuracy choose a common largeness parameter for the conjunction P_{1} ∧· · · ∧P_{r}. Apply design existence to that parameter. The resulting family lies in 𝒟, so the eventual implication gives every P_{j}. The height constant, choice condition and size conditions are those of this one chosen design. No limit of families and no exchange of two existential quantifiers is used.
