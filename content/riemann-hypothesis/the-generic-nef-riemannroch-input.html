12.2 The generic nef Riemann–Roch input

Lemma 12.3 (The chart lattice is the normalized generic section space). Let P = mCoordIdeal(y) satisfy the source’s arithmetic variety hypothesis of arithmetic dimension D + 1, and assume Birational y. Let X_{ℚ} be its multiprojective generic fibre, let ν :X̃_{ℚ} →X_{ℚ} be its normalization, and put ℒ = ν^{∗}𝒪(a) for the integer multidegree a. Evaluation at the generic multicone point identifies

span_{ℚ} chartLatNm(y, a, τ) = H^{0}(X̃_{ℚ}, ℒ^{⊗τ}) ⊂Ω. (MN.5)

Moreover genDeg(P, D, a) = c_{1}(ℒ)^{D} and NefOnGenericDeg P a says precisely that 𝒪(a) has nonnegative degree on every integral generic curve.

Proof. Choose one nonzero coordinate y_{k,c(k)} in each block. The associated standard affine chart has coor-dinate ring generated by the ratios y_{k,i}/y_{k,c(k)}. The hypothesis Birational says that every element of Ω is the quotient of evaluations of two integer multihomogeneous polynomials of the same degree, with nonzero denominator. It identifies Ω with the function field of X_{ℚ}, rather than an unspecified extension of it. On this chart a rational section represented by s ∈Ω is regular on the normalization if and only if

belongs to the integral closure of the rational chart ring in Ω. The same formula over the integer chart ring is the definition of chartLatNm. Integrality commutes with rational localization: if x satisfies a monic equation over A ⊗ℚ, choose a nonzero integer N clearing its coefficients with the powers required in that equation; then Nx satisfies a monic equation over A. Conversely an integral element stays integral after localization. There are only finitely many standard charts. A common multiple of their finitely many integers N clears all charts simultaneously. Hence any normalized generic section is a rational multiple of an element of the integer chart lattice, and the reverse inclusion follows immediately from integrality. This proves (MN.5), including both inclusions.

The multigraded homogeneous coordinate ring has, in all sufficiently large multidegrees m, its Hilbert polynomial H(m), whose degree-D part records the intersection products of the coordinate hyperplane classes. The source’s intNum takes D differences H(m) −H(m −a) and their eventual value. Lower-degree terms vanish; for the homogeneous degree-D part this value is D!H_{D}(a), which is c_{1}(𝒪(a))^{D}. This calculation is valid for arbitrary integer a: the base multidegree m tends to infinity in every coordinate, so each of the finitely many shifted arguments is eventually in the Hilbert-polynomial range. Normalization is finite and birational. Its pushforward structure sheaf has rank one, with quotient supported in dimension at most D −1, so it does not change that leading intersection coefficient. The same computation with one difference on a curve gives the ordinary degree of 𝒪(a) there. The horizontal prime ideals of arithmetic dimension two are exactly the closures of the integral curves of X_{ℚ}. This proves the final assertion.

Proposition 12.4 (The full source form of generic nef asymptotics). Under the hypotheses of Lemma 12.3, assume NefOnGenericDeg P a. Then every vector space in (MN.5) is finite dimensional and

lim _{τD} dim_{ℚ} span_{ℚ} chartLatNm(y, a, τ) = genDeg(P, D, a). (MN.6)

Thus the source’s NefRR holds without a bigness hypothesis and with exactly its normalization by D!.

Source: Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/PublishedTheorems/Geometry2/NefRR.lean:8--17.

Proof. The normalization of the integral projective ℚ-variety X_{ℚ} is finite and projective, and has dimension D. Its line bundle ℒ is nef. To see the field convention explicitly, a curve on its normalization maps finitely onto a curve of X_{ℚ}, unless it maps to a point, in which case its pulled-back degree is zero. In the first case its degree is the positive map degree times the degree downstairs, which is nonnegative by the hypothesis. Nefness remains true after extension to ℂ: an algebraic curve and its line bundle degree can be spread to a finite-type parameter scheme, with the degree constant in a flat family; specialization to an algebraic point reduces to a curve over a finite extension of ℚ. Pushing that curve down to ℚ multiplies its degree by a positive integer. This checks the curve convention used in the complex nef theorem, including curves not initially defined over ℚ.

Apply the asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem for nef divisors, in Lazarsfeld, Positivity in Algebraic Geometry I, Corollary 1.4.41, p. 69, following Theorem 1.4.40 [46]. It gives, on a projective variety or scheme,

h^{0}(X̃_{ℂ}, ℒ^{τ}_{ℂ}) = τ^{D}+ O(τ^{D−1}). (MN.7)

For a geometrically reducible base change, apply the coherent-sheaf version to its components, or the stated pure-dimensional scheme version; the top intersections and leading coefficients add. Finite-dimensional coherent cohomology commutes with the flat field extension ℚ → ℂ, so its dimension is the original ℚ-dimension. Lemma 12.3 identifies both the section space and the intersection number. For D ≥1, divide (MN.7) by τ^{D}/D!; the error tends to zero. If D = 0, the normal projective scheme is a finite field extension (or its finite reduced complex base change); every invertible sheaf has the same section dimension as its coordinate algebra. The constant Hilbert polynomial and the empty difference in the source give the same degree. Hence (MN.6) also holds in dimension zero. No estimate uniform in a or P is claimed: the source quantifies these first, and then takes the limit in τ.
