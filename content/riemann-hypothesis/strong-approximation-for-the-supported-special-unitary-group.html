15.2 Strong approximation for the supported special unitary group

We use full adeles, including the infinite places. This convention matters: the finite set of places at which approximation is discarded can omit some or all infinite places.

Lemma 15.8 (The matrix and adelic models). Let E/F be the CM extension in the source, let σ be its conjugation, let m ≥2, and let J ∈M_{m}(E) satisfy J^{t} = J and det J ̸= 0. For an F-algebra R put

G(R) = {A ∈ GL_{m}(R ⊗_{F} E) : A^{t}JA = J, det A = 1}. (SA.1)

This is a connected, absolutely almost simple, simply connected algebraic F-group. The source’s SUAdelic is G(𝔸_{F} ) with its usual adelic topology, and SURat is the diagonal image of G(F).

Proof. The source’s CM condition says that F is totally real, E totally complex, [E : F] = 2, and every complex embedding takes σ to complex conjugation. Injectivity of one such embedding proves σ^{2}= 1. If σ = 1, every embedding of E would have real image, a contradiction. Thus σ is the nonidentity automor-phism of the separable quadratic extension, with fixed field F. The source’s IsHermND is precisely the two conditions on J above. Its sigR is 1 ⊗σ and its JR is the scalar extension of J. Its group HR imposes the unitary equation; the kernel of its unit-valued determinant imposes exactly the last equation of (SA.1).

These are polynomial equations defining an affine algebraic group. Over an algebraic closure K of F, K ⊗_{F} E = K × K, with conjugation interchanging the factors. Write J = (J_{1}, J_{1}^{t}). The unitary equation for a pair (A, B) is B^{t}J_{1}A = J_{1}, so

B = J_{1}^{−t}A^{−t}J_{1}^{t}, det B = (det A)^{−1}.

Consequently projection to A is an algebraic isomorphism G_{K} ≃ SL_{m,K}; its inverse is regular because inverse matrices on determinant-one matrices are adjugates. For completeness, the elementary structural

facts about this last group can be seen as follows. Elementary root subgroups 1+aE_{ij} generate SL_{m} over an algebraically closed field by row reduction, and their products are connected; hence the group is connected. A nonzero ideal in its characteristic zero Lie algebra, decomposed under the diagonal torus, contains either a matrix unit or a nonzero diagonal matrix. Bracketing the latter with a suitable matrix unit gives the former; brackets [E_{ij}, E_{jk}] = E_{ik} and [E_{ij}, E_{ji}] = E_{ii} −E_{jj} then give all of 𝔰𝔩_{m}. This proves absolute almost simplicity. Finally embed K in ℂ. The analytic group SL_{m}(ℂ) retracts onto SU(m) by polar decomposition. The transitive action of SU(m) on the unit sphere has stabilizer SU(m −1); the homotopy sequence of SU(m −1) → SU(m) → S^{2m−1}, starting with SU(2) = S^{3}, proves simple connectivity. A central algebraic isogeny in characteristic zero is finite étale and analytically a covering map; a connected covering of this simply connected analytic group has degree one. Degree is unchanged by base extension. Thus the algebraic group is simply connected.

Choose an F-basis of E. It identifies 𝔸_{F} ⊗_{F} E with a finite product of copies of 𝔸_{F} . The source uses the module topology, and these coordinates and their inverse are continuous: coordinate maps are linear, and the inverse is a finite sum of scalar multiples of fixed basis vectors. At all but finitely many finite places this basis identifies the integral lattices up to fixed integral denominators. It therefore gives exactly the restricted-product adelic topology on E ⊗_{F} F_{v}, rather than a different topology. On the determinant-one locus the matrix inverse is the continuous adjugate polynomial, so the source’s topology inherited from matrix units agrees with the topology on matrix entries. These observations identify its HA unitary subgroup and determinant kernel with (SA.1) on full adeles.

Background fact 15.9 (Strong approximation in the precise support convention). For a number field F, a connected absolutely almost simple simply connected F-group G, and a finite set S of places, if ∏_{v∈S}G(F_{v}) is noncompact, the image of G(F) is dense in G(𝔸^{S}_{F}).

The original result is Platonov’s Theorem A [53, Theorem A, p. 1140]. Its initial convention requires S to contain the infinite places. The author’s addendum explicitly removes that restriction, proving the remaining one-finite-place case [54, §3, p. 785]. Thus the stated version applies to an arbitrary finite S. The addendum also revises the type-A argument in §2; we use the corrected theorem, not the unrevised proof in isolation.

Proposition 15.10 (The full source strong-approximation input). Let G, J, F, E be as in Lemma 15.8. Let S_{1} be a set of infinite places, S_{2} a finite set of finite places, and let ϵ ∈𝔸_{F} have component 1 exactly on S = S_{1} ∪S_{2} and component 0 elsewhere. Define

G_{S} = {h ∈G(𝔸_{F} ) : ϵ(h_{ij} −δ_{ij}) = h_{ij} −δ_{ij} for all i, j}. (SA.2)

If G_{S} is noncompact, every g ∈G(𝔸_{F} ) belongs to G(F)G_{S}. This proves StrongApproxSU with every one of its quantified hypotheses.

Proof. An adelic element is determined by its place components. At a place outside S, (SA.2) reads h_{ij} = δ_{ij}; at a place in S it imposes no condition. Hence G_{S} is exactly the subgroup supported on S, homeomorphic to ∏_{v∈S}G(F_{v}). There is no ambiguity about existence of ϵ: at infinity use the characteristic function of S_{1}, and in the finite restricted product sum the adeles supported at one v ∈ S_{2} with value 1. Evaluation at v gives 1 for v ∈S_{2} and 0 otherwise. Its finite support makes it an adele. These are exactly the four clauses of IsPlacesIdem. If S were empty, G_{S} = {1} would be compact, so the noncompactness hypothesis already excludes that case.

The finite splitting of the adelic ring gives a topological decomposition

G(𝔸_{F} ) = G(F_{v}) × G(𝔸^{S}_{F}). (SA.3)

The hypotheses of Fact 15.9 have now all been checked: F is a number field, G has the required three group properties by the preceding lemma, S is finite, and its local product is noncompact by (SA.2). Take a basic open neighborhood U_{S} × U^{S} of g. Choose γ ∈G(F) whose component away from S lies in U^{S}. Define h ∈G_{S} by h_{v} = γ_{v}^{−1}g_{v} for v ∈S and h_{v}= 1 otherwise. Then γh has exactly the S-components of g and has components γ away from S. It belongs to the prescribed neighborhood. This proves the closure assertion with the source’s order γh. Its ambient closure in the unitary group contains the same points: every ambient open neighborhood restricts to an open neighborhood in its special unitary subgroup. This is precisely the closure and matrix model used in the source conclusion.

Lemma 15.11 (Why the split support used later is noncompact). Write B = 𝔸_{F} ⊗_{F} E, c = 1 ⊗σ and ι : 𝔸_{F} →B. Suppose that v is a finite place, ϵ_{v} = 1, and

e^{2}= e, e c(e) = 0, e + c(e) = ι(ϵ). (SA.4)

Then the supported set (SA.2) is noncompact.

Proof. We give the matrix construction, which avoids assuming a particular diagonalization of J at v. Put K = J^{−1} and choose i ̸= j, possible since m ≥2. For a, b ∈B with ab = 0, c(a) = b and c(b) = a, form

X(a, b) = 1 + aE_{ij} −bKE_{ji}J. (SA.5)

Its determinant is one. Indeed it factors as (1 + aE_{ij})K(1 − bE_{ji})J; the omitted cross term is −abE_{ij}KE_{ji}J = 0, and both elementary factors have determinant one. The conjugate transpose of K is K, because the same holds for J. Thus

Multiplying this by (SA.5), the linear terms cancel; the remaining terms vanish by ab = ba = 0, E_{ij}^{2} = E_{ji}^{2} = 0, and JK = KJ = 1. Consequently X(a, b) is unitary as well.

For y ∈F_{v}, take t ∈𝔸_{F} supported at v with t_{v} = y, and set a = eι(t), b = c(e)ι(t). The identities (SA.4) imply c^{2}(e) = e, c(e)^{2} = c(e), and ι(ϵ)e = e and ι(ϵ)c(e) = c(e); hence the hypotheses on a, b hold. They also imply that every entry of X(a, b) −1 is fixed by multiplication by ι(ϵ). The matrix (SA.5) therefore belongs to G_{S}.

Choose an F-linear map ϕ : E →F with ϕ(1) = 1, by extending 1 to an F-basis. Its scalar extension gives a continuous 𝔸_{F} -linear map λ : B →𝔸_{F} with λ(ι(t)) = t. The map

is continuous. Since i ̸= j, multiplying the (i, j) entry of (SA.5) by e kills its b-term and gives eι(t). It follows that L(X(a, b)_{ij}) = λ((e + c(e))ι(t)) = ϵt. Evaluation at v gives y. Thus the continuous function g →→L(g_{ij})_{v} maps G_{S} onto F_{v}. The latter is noncompact: a compact subset of its normed-field topology has bounded norm, whereas the powers of an element of norm greater than one have unbounded norm. A compact G_{S} would have compact continuous image F_{v}, a contradiction.
