Embed X_{ℂ} by the Segre map. A general linear projection to P^{e}_{ℂ} has centre disjoint from X_{ℂ} and is finite: choose e + 1 linear forms giving a homogeneous Noether normalization of its coordinate ring. This supplies a GoodProj; hence the branch returning zero when no such projection exists is inapplicable. On the affine chart where the first projection coordinate is 1, outside the branch and singular loci, the finite map is a finite union of holomorphic sheets. Their complement is algebraic of smaller complex dimension and therefore has Lebesgue measure zero. A sheet lifts locally to nonzero multicone coordinates. Two such lifts differ by nonzero holomorphic scalars in the blocks. The differences of the pulled-back potentials are sums of log |c_{k}|, whose dd^{c} is zero; g is unchanged. The density is thus independent of the arbitrary sheet chosen in the source’s existential definition. The real change-of-variables formula on these sheets, with (MN.2), identifies its finite sum and Lebesgue integral with (MN.1). The missing projective hyperplane also has smaller complex dimension. For e = 0 the same argument is just the finite sum over the points of the zero-dimensional reduced cycle, with density 1.

To justify convergence at the omitted sets, take a projective resolution of the complex variety, using

Proposition 11.4. The pulled-back line metrics are smooth on this compact manifold. The logarithmic norm of a nonzero section is locally integrable: in a local frame it is log |f| minus a smooth function, and the local integrability assertion is part of the Poincaré–Lelong theorem [45, III,(2.15),p. 143]. It is therefore absolutely integrable against each smooth top-degree density on the compact resolution. Smooth g is simpler. The exceptional sets and omitted hyperplanes have measure zero there. Applying change of variables on increasing relatively compact portions of the sheets and then absolute integrability gives the stated equality of the full integrals. The argument is unchanged for a horizontal prime divisor. Vertical arithmetic components have empty complex fibre and contribute no such integral.

We check the multiplicities when pushing (MN.4) down to the original, possibly singular variety. At a codimension-one local ring R of an integral component, f is a nonzerodivisor and R/(f) has finite length. Let R̃ be its finite semilocal normalization. The module R̃/R has finite length. Apply multiplication by f to 0 →R →R̃ →R̃/R →0. The kernel and cokernel of multiplication by f on the last finite-length module have equal length. The resulting exact sequence consequently shows
