4.3 The precise Kato input

For this subsection, H_{f}^{1}(L, V ) has its ordinary Bloch–Kato meaning: a continuous cohomology class is unramified at finite places away from ℓ and becomes a coboundary after tensoring with B_{cris} at places above ℓ. For a stable lattice T ⊂V , the discrete Selmer group uses, locally, the image of these finite rational classes in H^{1}(L_{v}, V /T). These are exactly the conventions in Kato’s §14.1.

Background fact 4.10 (Noncentral critical Selmer finiteness). Let f be a normalized newform of weight k ≥2, and let V_{f} be its two-dimensional cohomological ℓ-adic representation over a finite coefficient field. For a finite abelian extension L/ℚ, 1 ≤ r ≤ k −1 and 2r ̸= k, every stable lattice in V_{f}(r) has finite discrete Bloch–Kato Selmer group over L.

This is Kato [44, Theorem 14.2(1), pp. 235–236]. It imposes no good-ordinary hypothesis; that hypoth-esis belongs to the different Iwasawa statement, Theorem 17.4. The representation here is Kato’s cohomo-logical V_{f}, of Hodge–Tate weights 0, 1 −k, and the twist is the cyclotomic twist.

Lemma 4.11 (From finite discrete Selmer to rational vanishing). If the discrete Bloch–Kato Selmer group of a stable lattice T ⊂V over a number field L is finite, then H_{f}^{1}(L, V ) = 0.

Proof. Choose a finite set of places S containing the coefficient prime and all ramification of V . The finite local conditions place the rational Selmer group in H^{1}(G_{L,S}, V ). The coefficient exact sequence 0 →T → V →V /T →0 sends it into the discrete Selmer group: each local finite rational class maps to its allowed local image by definition. The source is a vector space over the coefficient field and in particular is a divisible abelian group. Its image in a finite group is divisible and must be zero. Exactness therefore places every rational Selmer class in the image of H^{1}(G_{L,S}, T).

This image is a bounded lattice in H^{1}(G_{L,S}, V ). One can check boundedness directly from the usual finite coefficient construction: H^{1}(G_{L,S}, T) is the inverse limit of the finite groups H^{1}(G_{L,S}, T/ℓ^{n}T), and its image is a finitely generated integral module in the finite-dimensional rational cohomology. Finiteness of the finite groups follows from the finiteness of extensions of bounded degree unramified outside S; the passage to rational cohomology is localization of this integral module. If a nonzero vector x and all ℓ^{−n}x belonged to this lattice, a coordinate functional nonzero on x would give integral coordinates of valuations tending to minus infinity, a contradiction. Since the Selmer group is a vector space contained in that lattice, it is zero.

Proposition 4.12 (The odd-weight twist and finite characters). Let e ≥1, let f have weight 2e + 1, and let φ be a finite-order Galois character factoring through a finite abelian extension of ℚ. Then

Proof. Take a finite abelian extension L on which φ is trivial, and enlarge the finite coefficient field to contain its values. A stable lattice exists: the continuous image of the compact Galois group is bounded, so the integral span of its translates of one lattice is again a stable lattice. Now 1 ≤e ≤2e and 2e ̸= 2e + 1. Apply Fact 4.10 with k = 2e + 1, r = e and then Lemma 4.11. This gives H_{f}^{1}(L, V_{f}(e)) = 0.

The restriction of a finite local class is finite: an unramified cocycle remains unramified after restricting inertia, and a B coboundary remains a coboundary after restricting the local Galois group. Thus restriction

cris sends the asserted Selmer group to this zero group. Its kernel lies, by inflation–restriction, in

H^{1} Gal(L/ℚ), (V_{f}(e) ⊗φ)^{GL}).

This is zero in characteristic zero. Indeed, for a cocycle c of a finite group G, put x = −|G|^{−1} ∑_{g∈G}c(g); the cocycle identity gives c(h) = hx−x for every h. The restriction map is therefore injective on the Selmer group, proving its vanishing.

It asks for the two finite characters 1, θ, where θ cuts out an imaginary quadratic field, and more generally uses characters killing the subgroup fixing the Mth roots of unity. The latter factor through ℚ(µ_{M}) and hence meet the finite abelian hypothesis. Thus the critical range

and both twist exponents have been checked, with no appeal to a central critical value or an ordinary main conjecture.
