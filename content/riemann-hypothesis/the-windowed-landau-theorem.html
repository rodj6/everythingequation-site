(a) There is D ≤σ −θ for which B(σ, D) ∩Z = ∅, and a pole ρ_{1} with σ −ℜρ_{1} < D. (b) A real pole β > θ is the only pole in B(σ, r), for some σ −β < r ≤σ −θ. To verify the dichotomy, if that finite set is empty use D = σ−θ and the original pole. Otherwise choose a member of minimum distance d from σ. If some pole satisfies σ −ℜρ < d, take D = d. If no pole does, a member at distance d must be the real point β = σ −d: the real-part distance is at least d, and its squared modulus is that distance squared plus the imaginary part squared. Also d < σ −θ, because the original pole has real part greater than θ. The positive gap to the remaining finitely many distances gives the radius r in (b).
