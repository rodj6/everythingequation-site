Lemma 8.22 (The convex-body argument). Let Λ be a full lattice in a real vector space of dimension d, of covolume v. A convex, symmetric measurable set of volume greater than 2^{d}v contains a nonzero lattice vector. For d ≥1, if a real symmetric matrix G satisfies z^{t}Gz ≥1 for every nonzero z ∈ℤ^{d}, then

G > 0, det G ≥d^{−d}.

Proof. If a measurable set B has volume greater than v, its translates by Λ cannot be disjoint: intersect them with a measurable fundamental parallelepiped P, and translate back. If disjoint, countable additivity would give ∑

vol(B) = vol((B + λ) ∩P) ≤ vol(P) = v.

Apply this to B =^{1}_{2}A. Two points of B whose difference is a nonzero lattice vector give a point of A, since b_{1} −b_{2} =^{1}_{2}(2b_{1}) +^{ 1}_{2}(−2b_{2}). This proves the convex-body assertion.

The quadratic form of G is nonnegative on rational vectors: multiply by a denominator to reduce to an integer vector. By continuity it is nonnegative on all real vectors. For δ > 0, G_{δ} = G + δI is positive definite and still has integral minimum at least one. The open ellipsoid x^{t}G_{δ}x < 1 contains no nonzero integer vector. Proof. We spell out the geometric properties of the complex. Near a positive form, only finitely many of the inequalities q_{h}(y) ≥ 1 can be active: compare with a fixed positive form and use discreteness of the embedded integral lattice. Thus ℛ is locally polyhedral. It lies in the positive cone by Lemma 8.23. A linear form strictly positive on the semidefinite cone has bounded sublevel sets there: on the compact unit section of that cone it has a positive minimum. Its minimum on the nonempty closed set ℛ is attained and its minimising face is compact. That face has a vertex, since a nonempty compact polyhedron has one. Consequently the normal cones of the vertices cover the open dual cone. They are locally finite there; a compact dual subregion bounds the associated minimising primal forms, where local polyhedrality gives only finitely many vertices. Every finite intersection with the open dual cone is convex.

The augmented complex of tuples in this cover is acyclic. One direct verification is the usual convex-cover double complex: take singular chains in the finite intersections in one direction and alternating deletion in the other. In one direction, convex contraction gives homology only in degree zero, namely the tuple complex. In the other, subdivide a singular simplex until its pieces lie in members of the cover; its augmented finite cover complex is a simplex and is exact. The resulting homology is that of the convex open dual cone, which is ℤ in degree zero. For closed polyhedral members, carry out this argument with small open thickenings on the compact support of any given finite chain. Local finiteness makes the relevant collection finite; empty intersections remain empty for sufficiently small thickening on that compact set. Thus the tuple nerve and its finite-chain filling are unchanged. This proves the claimed augmented exactness.

Lemma 8.27 (Torsion from an exact permutation complex). Suppose an augmented complex · · · → ℤ[X_{1}] →ℤ[X_{0}] →M →0 of G-modules is exact through degree m −1. Suppose X_{p} has at most A orbits and stabilisers of order at most s ≥1, for p ≤m, and each boundary of a generator of X_{m+1} has ℓ^{1}-norm at most b ≥1. Then

| tors H_{m}(G, M)| ≤b^{A}s^{mAsm}.

Proof. For m = 0, coinvariants have at most A generators, and their relations have Euclidean norm at most their ℓ^{1}-norm, hence at most b. Choose linearly independent relation columns spanning their rational span. Hadamard’s inequality bounds their nonzero-volume determinant by b^{r}, r ≤A; the index of the full relation lattice in its saturation is no greater, since adding relations decreases that index. This proves the torsion bound b^{A}.

For the induction, put K = ker(ℤ[X_{0}] → M). The shifted augmented complex resolves K. The homology exact sequence of 0 → K → ℤ[X_{0}] → M → 0 compares the desired torsion with that of H_{m−1}(G, K) and H_{m}(G, ℤ[X_{0}]). The latter is the sum, over the at most A orbits, of the stabiliser homology, by the explicit induced-module bar resolution. For a finite group of order at most s, positive-degree homology is killed by its order (sum the translates in the bar resolution) and has at most s^{m} generators (the rank of the degree-m bar group). Its order is therefore at most s^{sm}. Thus |H_{m}(G, ℤ[X_{0}])| ≤ s^{Asm}. The shifted induction gives b^{A}s^{(m−1)Asm−1}. Their product is at most b^{A}s^{mAsm}. In this exact-sequence comparison the intervening positive stabiliser homology is finite, so taking its quotient cannot create torsion from an uncontrolled free quotient.
