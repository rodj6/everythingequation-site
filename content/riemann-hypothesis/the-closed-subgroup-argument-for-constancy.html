4.18 The closed-subgroup argument for constancy

The constancy argument is separate from the existence and matching of theta integrals. It applies to an already constructed continuous function. We give its density ingredients explicitly, since left rational invariance alone would not force constancy.

Lemma 4.80 (Detection at degree-one prime ideals). Let K be a number field and χ a continuous unitary idele-class character. Suppose there is a finite set S of prime ideals such that the entire local character χ_{w} : K_{w}^{×}→ℂ^{×} is trivial whenever w /∈S and Nw is a rational prime. Then χ = 1.

Proof. Suppose χ ̸= 1. Tate’s continuation theorem makes its finite L-function holomorphic in a neighbor-hood of 1. For a norm character χ = | · |^{it} with t ̸= 0 the possible pole is 1 −it, which is different from 1; for a character not of this form the completed function is entire. Reciprocal gamma factors are entire, so the same local holomorphy holds for the finite function. It follows that |L(χ, s)| stays bounded as real s ↓1.

Put a_{w} = (Nw)^{−s} for s > 1. At one of the specified trivial local characters the Euler factor is (1−a_{w})^{−1} and its modulus is at least exp(a_{w}). Lemma 4.81 (The norm-one density used by the determinant). Let E/F be a CM quadratic extension, assume E/ℚ is Galois, and let T be a finite set of finite places of F. Write U_{1}(𝔸_{F} ) = {z ∈𝔸^{×}_{E}: zz̄ = 1}. A closed subgroup of this group that contains U_{1}(F) and all the local U_{1}(F_{v}) at the fully split places outside T is the whole group.

Proof. First record an idele version. Let M ⊂𝔸^{×}_{E} be a closed subgroup containing E^{×}, one fixed positive infinite idele e with |e|_{𝔸E} > 1, and the entire local E_{w}^{×} outside a finite exceptional set whenever Nw is prime. The quotient 𝔸^{×}_{E}/M is a compact abelian group. To see compactness, the norm-one idele-class group is compact, and division by a power of e puts the modulus of any idele into [1, |e|]. Compactness of the norm-one class group is the usual ideal-class and unit-lattice argument: use finitely many ideal-class representatives for finite valuations, a compact product of finite local units, and a bounded fundamental parallelepiped for the logarithms of the global units at infinity. The remaining archimedean angular factors are compact. Adjoining the bounded modulus interval consequently gives a compact set surjecting onto the displayed quotient.
