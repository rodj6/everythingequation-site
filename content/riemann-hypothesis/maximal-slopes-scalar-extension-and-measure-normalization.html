Lemma 16.1 (The largest sublattice of maximal slope). A nonzero Hermitian lattice L has a largest sublattice of maximal slope. It is saturated in L. Any additive automorphism of L which carries 𝒪_{K}-submodules to 𝒪_{K}-submodules and preserves its global real norm preserves this sublattice; in particular this holds for a semilinear isometry.

Source: Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ MaxSlopeExtendedLatticeGaloisLe.lean:20--73; MaxSlopeSqFieldExtensionLeComparableNorms.lean:26--49.

Proof. Saturating a sublattice within its vector-space span keeps its rank and divides its covolume by a positive integer. It therefore increases its degree. Among saturated sublattices of a fixed rank and slope at least a given t, only finitely many occur. To see this in the real realization, choose an integral basis of L. The wedge of an oriented integral basis of a rank-k saturated integer sublattice is a primitive vector of the discrete lattice ∧^{k}L. Its Euclidean norm is the sublattice covolume. A lower bound for the slope is an upper bound for that norm, with a fixed field/rank normalization. A bounded ball contains only finitely many vectors of a discrete finite-rank lattice. The primitive wedge determines the real span and hence its saturated intersection with L. There are only finitely many ranks. Taking t = µ(L) proves attainment of the maximal slope θ.

The degree is supermodular:

deg A + deg B ≤ deg(A ∩B) + deg(A + B), rk A + rk B = rk(A ∩B) + rk(A + B).

For the degree inequality, project a basis of B orthogonally off the real span of A and use the quotient covolume formula. Projection can only decrease the relevant Gram volume, by the contraction argument of

Lemma 9.1. The finite index between the sum lattice and its saturation only strengthens the inequality. The rank identity is the vector-space dimension formula. If A, B have degree θ times rank, the intersection has degree at most θ times its rank. These two formulas force A + B to have degree at least θ times rank, hence equality.

The sum of all maximal-slope sublattices is a finite sum, since L is Noetherian. The preceding argument makes this sum a maximal-slope sublattice containing every other one. Its saturation again has slope at least θ and so equals it by maximality. An isometry preserves ranks and covolumes; its image of this largest sublattice has the same universal property and is therefore equal to it. This proves the canonical invariance used in the source.
