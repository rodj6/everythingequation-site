3 The analytic window principle

The analytic argument needs an upper bound on arbitrarily distant power-length windows, rather than an estimate at every sufficiently large point. We first prove this implication for finite positive combinations of

Dirichlet characters. We then specify the uniform estimate and host existence statement required to produce those windows.

Throughout this section, L(s, χ) denotes the meromorphic continuation of ∑_{n≥1}χ(n)n^{−s}. Characters are extended by zero off the units. The character of conductor 1 gives ζ(s). In the analytic arguments below, the simple pole of ζ(s) at s = 1 is always excluded from the zero sets Z_{F} (just as s = 1 is excluded for the trivial character in EC and ordinary RH). For a positive real number y, complex powers mean y^{s}= exp(s log y), using the real logarithm.
