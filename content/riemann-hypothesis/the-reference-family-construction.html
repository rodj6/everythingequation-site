6.2 The reference-family construction

A local index is called live when some selected eigencharacter has a compatible Hecke–Galois representation whose specified rank-r complement is residually absolutely irreducible. This is an existence condition on a local index; it does not specify one reference representation in advance. The assertion needed in the comparison is that every live index has a good reference family with adapted inertia and corner data, and with all required corner roots in its coefficient ring. We now describe the finite enlargement which the source uses to arrange the last condition simultaneously.

Lemma 6.17 (Residual absolute irreducibility and matrix span). If a representation of a group on a nonzero finite-dimensional k-space V is absolutely irreducible, the k-span of its group matrices is End_{k}(V ).

Proof. First let k be algebraically closed and let A be that span, which is a unital algebra. An endomorphism commuting with A is scalar: it has an eigenvalue λ, and its nonzero λ-eigenspace is A-stable, hence all of V . We show by induction that for linearly independent v_{1}, . . . , v_{s}, the set U = {(av_{1}, . . . , av_{s}) : a ∈A} is all of V^{s}. For s = 1 it is a nonzero invariant subspace. For the induction step, projection onto the first s −1 coordinates is surjective by induction. Its kernel in U is either zero or the entire last copy of V , by irreducibility. In the latter case U = V^{s}. In the former case U is the graph of an A-linear map V^{s−1}→V . Each of its coordinate maps commutes with A and is therefore scalar. The identity element of A would then give v_{s} =∑_{i<s}λ_{i}v_{i}, a contradiction. Taking the v_{i} to be a basis shows that A realizes every possible list of images of a basis, so A = End_{k}(V ).

For arbitrary k, extend scalars to an algebraic closure. Absolute irreducibility allows the preceding argument there. The extended matrix span is the extension of the original span, so its dimension is both dim_{k} A and (dim_{k} V )^{2}. Since A ⊂ End_{k}(V ) already has this full dimension, equality follows over k.

Lemma 6.19 (Equality from the common Frobenius polynomials). Two continuous integral Galois represen-tations with the same characteristic polynomials on unramified Frobenius elements at primes of the CM field E split from their complex conjugates, outside a fixed finite set, have the same characteristic polynomials at every element of G_{E}.

Proof. We check the extra split-prime restriction in the density argument of Lemma 8.43. Choose a ∈𝒪_{E} with a ̸= a, and exclude the finitely many prime divisors of a −a. Such an a exists because complex conjugation on a CM field is nontrivial. Prime ideals of residue degree at least two over ℚ have Dirichlet density zero: for real s > 1 their norm series is bounded by [E : ℚ]∑_{p}p^{−2s}, which stays bounded as s decreases to one. Each characteristic coefficient is a continuous class function, since it is a polynomial in the matrix entries. The local Hecke algebra has a single residue evaluation. To verify this assertion, let T ⊂O^{X} be local. Each evaluation T → O → k is surjective, since T contains the constants, and its kernel is therefore the unique maximal ideal of T. For f ∈T, the element f −f(x) is a nonunit because its value at x is zero. Its image under evaluation at any other y is therefore zero in k. Thus f(x) and f(y) have equal residues.

Apply this to the characteristic-polynomial coefficient functions of a good family. After reduction, the characteristic polynomial of r_{0}(g, x) is independent of x. At each selected x its ordered decomposition is ρ_{x} ⊕⊕_{c}χ_{c}, with the same character summands for every x. The characteristic polynomials therefore factor as det(z −ρ_{x}(g))∏_{c}(z −χ_{c}(g)). The common character product is monic and nonzero, so it can be cancelled in k[z]. The residual main representations have equal characteristic polynomials, and hence equal traces, at every g. At a live point one main representation is absolutely irreducible. It can be compared with the chosen good family at that same point by Lemma 6.19: their good Hecke–Galois condition specifies the same Frobenius polynomials. Cancellation of the common monic character product then identifies the residual main characteristic polynomials as well. Lemmas 6.17 and 6.18 show that the residual main matrix span is full at every selected x. Full matrix span remains full after scalar extension and forces every invariant subspace to be zero or the whole space. It therefore also proves absolute irreducibility for all these residual main representations. This is the residual-irreducibility part of the reference-setting construction, and it justifies the residue-span hypothesis used in Lemma 6.10.
