9.1 Lattices, heights and an integral orthogonal cut

Let K be a totally complex number field, let n_{K} be the number of its infinite places, and let V be a finite-dimensional K-vector space. Choose one embedding σ : K → ℂ at each infinite place and a positive definite Hermitian norm at that place. A lattice means a finitely generated 𝒪_{K}-submodule of V . Its rank is the dimension of its K-span. Realizing the lattice in the direct sum of the complex spaces at these places gives a discrete integer lattice of real rank 2n_{K}r, where r is its K-rank. All covolumes are taken in its own real span.

Proof. Write ρ for the real realization. The sublattice ρ(L_{0}) is saturated in ρ(L): if a nonzero integer multiple of x ∈ L lies in L_{0}, then that same multiple of ϕ(x) is zero, and the characteristic-zero vector space has no such torsion. Equivalently, the quotient is the torsion-free integer group I. A basis of L_{0} as an integer group can therefore be extended to an integer basis of L. Project the additional basis vectors orthogonally off ⟨ρ(L_{0})⟩_{ℝ}. They form a basis of the quotient lattice Q. In orthonormal coordinates adapted to this subspace, the basis matrix is block triangular. Taking its determinant gives

covol(L) = covol(L_{0}) covol(Q).

The factors in (335) multiply in the same way because the ranks add. This proves the equality in (336).

The realized map kills the real span of L_{0}, so it induces a linear map from the span of Q to the span of I and an integer-group isomorphism Q →I. Both spans split orthogonally by infinite places, with real dimension 2r in each part. To see the dimension assertion, the K-span of L/L_{0} has dimension r; at each chosen complex embedding it becomes a complex r-space, hence a real 2r-space. The realized map preserves these parts and has norm at most c_{v} on part v.

Choose an orthonormal basis within each part. If c_{v} = 0 on a nonzero part, the image determinant is zero and the inequality follows directly. Otherwise divide that part’s map by c_{v}. Each resulting part is a contraction. Its Gram matrix is positive semidefinite and its quadratic form is at most the identity quadratic form; the real Hermitian spectral theorem therefore puts all its eigenvalues in [0, 1]. Its determinant is at most one. Undoing the scaling and taking the square root of the Gram determinant bounds the volume multiplier by ∏_{v}c_{v}^{2r}. Changing from these orthonormal bases to an integer basis multiplies both volumes by the same absolute determinant. Thus covol(I) ≤∏_{v}c_{v}^{2r} covol(Q). The height normalizations for I and Q have the same rank r and the same field K, so their factors agree. This proves the inequality. This is the source’s placewise Gram-determinant argument, including its zero-scaling case.
