The placement of the quantifiers in this proposition is essential. The exponents e_{D}, e_{L} are chosen before ϵ, while the family, the admissible set, and its threshold may depend on ϵ. At a fixed ϵ, the constants controlling its family must work at every sufficiently large admissible x, and not be chosen again after x is specified. No uniformity over all primitive characters is asserted: the level, its conductor data, and all fixed constants may depend on the primitive character under consideration.

For completeness, every primitive character ψ belongs to a level of the kind used here. Take the finite cyclic subgroup generated by ψ in the character group of its conductor, replace each member by its inducing primitive character, take no exceptional character, and put o equal to the size of that cyclic group. The trivial member induces the conductor 1 character. Taking the inducing primitive character commutes with inversion: the conductor of an inverse equals the original conductor, because induction commutes with inversion in both directions, and injectivity of induction identifies the two primitive characters. Thus the resulting finite set is conjugation-stable. The primitive character inducing ψ is ψ itself, so it is indeed contained in this cyclic level. Consequently proving the small-scale hypothesis for these cyclic levels suffices for the primitive-character conclusion used in the reduction 2.4.
