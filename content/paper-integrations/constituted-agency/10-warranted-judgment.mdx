**A constituted judgment can be a genuine commitment.** Understanding how a view was formed does not automatically justify either retaining or rejecting it. The question is whether the grounds survive examination and whether that examination actually governs later conduct. The full account here connects avowal, evidential warrant, practical implementation and historical ownership without inventing an independently originating executive behind the person.

<a id="section-10"></a>

## 10 Recognition and the authority of a constituted judgment

<a id="section-10-1"></a>

### 10.1 Judgment, avowal and commitment

A history that includes deliberation includes the work through which a person makes a commitment. Describing that work as constituted does not yet say whether its result should guide action. The positive question is how an organized participant can acquire grounds for retaining or changing a practical rule. Ismael’s account helps locate the participant without supplying an independent author behind the inquiry. She expressly accepts that fixing a person’s complete initial microstate and the stimulus fixes their behavior in a deterministic setting. Her response concerns the differentiated dependencies within that setting: the person’s history, beliefs and reflective organization contribute through deciding, and that activity constitutes the person as author of the resulting judgment ([Ismael 2016, 106-8](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). The present account takes this constituted authorship seriously while keeping the question of warranted authority separate.

The separation matters because a judgment can create a commitment. Ismael distinguishes reporting an already settled attitude from avowing an attitude in a unified personal voice. A jury’s verdict belongs to the jury, rather than to whichever member announces it; its pronouncement can settle what the collective affirms. Similarly, judging can settle what a person undertakes to defend and use in further reasoning ([Ismael 2016, 43-45](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). This gives construction a positive significance. An attitude need not precede the processes that integrate and affirm it in order to become a real commitment. Its formation can change what the person owes to evidence, earlier undertakings and other people. The fact that those processes have an unchosen developmental basis does not cancel the commitments they establish.

This account also corrects a possible misunderstanding of the executive machinery in Sections [9](/consciousness/constituted-agency/09-recognition-and-authority#section-9) and [11](/consciousness/constituted-agency/11-acquired-self-model#section-11). The assessor is a functional component of the participant. It is not the person standing apart from the rest of the organization. Ismael emphasizes that the appropriate corporate analogue of the self is the whole company, rather than its chief executive or central office ([Ismael 2016, 82](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). Accordingly, acquiring a model, comparing policies and installing a change are activities attributable to the organized participant through their relations within it. A register’s output does not become a personal judgment merely because it occupies a privileged location. Attribution requires an account of the organization through which information, assessment and action belong together. The finite example specifies a small functional route within such an interpretation; it does not establish sufficient conditions for human personhood.

Once genuine commitment is allowed, recognition cannot mean that constructed self-models should generally lose authority. Their construction may be how useful commitments acquire a stable place in conduct. A person who understands how an undertaking was formed may find that it remains supported by good reasons. Memory of the undertaking, its effects on others and the projects it enables can warrant its continuing influence. Ismael’s temporally extended self is maintained partly through honoring intentions while permitting rational reconsideration ([Ismael 2016, 69-72](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). Understanding therefore has two possible practical outcomes: it can make retention more answerable to its grounds, or expose a reason for revision. The achievement is a better supported relation to the commitment, which can include a deliberate decision to continue it.

The narrower challenge concerns a recommendation whose alleged entitlement exceeds those grounds. Suppose a self-model presents a response as compulsory because it expresses an absolutely originating author. Recognition of its actual dependence defeats that particular justification, if the constitutive and non-origination premises are warranted. It leaves open whether the recommendation has another justification. Its personal history may explain its salience; its protective role may justify keeping it; a responsibility to another person may favor acting on it despite an immediate cost. None of these possibilities is decided by tracing its origin alone. Conversely, ownership supplies no blanket answer to evidence that a recommendation conflicts with the person’s other warranted commitments. The relevant assessment asks what the recommendation is doing and which reasons support allowing it that role.

<a id="section-10-2"></a>

### 10.2 Warrant and implementation

A constitutive avowal does not answer that assessment simply by occurring. Establishing what I am committed to differs from establishing that I should be committed to it. Ismael’s own analogy allows records of deliberation to serve descriptive and justificatory purposes that pull against one another: an organization may sanitize its account of how it decided ([Ismael 2016, 74](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). A coherent personal narrative can likewise conceal bypass, omit inconvenient evidence or redescribe submission as endorsement. Representational warrant is therefore indispensable. The retained account must concern the actual process, with possibilities of error and correction. In Section [11](/consciousness/constituted-agency/11-acquired-self-model#section-11), the estimate is constrained by actual probe returns and fresh predictions. Those relations provide evidence for the account within the nominated mechanism family; a declaration of insight would not supply the same evidence.

Evaluative warrant then connects that account to reasons for action. The mechanism’s origin is relevant when knowing it changes an assessment under independently specified commitments, evidence, costs or constraints. It is not automatically disqualifying. The conditional principle in Section [9](/consciousness/constituted-agency/09-recognition-and-authority#section-9) isolates a limited case: an additional attribution supplies no further task information once the relevant evidence and practical circumstances are represented. If attribution changes legitimate preferences, responsibilities or available actions, the principle’s antecedent fails. This qualification is philosophically substantive. It preserves the possibility that knowing a decision is mine matters because of commitments incurred through making it. The argument removes an unsupported claim to compulsory priority while retaining the forms of personal significance that can themselves give reasons.

Section [13](/consciousness/constituted-agency/13-selectivity-and-failure#section-13) gives this distinction a determinate application. In one mechanism class, retaining the installed route preserves its declared task function and avoids a needless revision charge. In the other, the acquired account supports changing the route because the expected improvement exceeds that charge. A correct decision to retain is consequently an exercise of assessment, rather than an incomplete attempt at liberation. These conclusions depend on the task law, available policies and actual costs; the equations do not decide which human projects deserve protection. They show how understanding can warrant a selective difference when those evaluative premises are supplied. Reflection earns a role by discriminating situations in which the reasons favor change from situations in which they favor continuity.

The remaining requirement is that the assessment enter practical organization. Ismael distinguishes recognizing what is best from having the strength or structure to act accordingly; her discussions of weakness of will concern precisely that gap ([Ismael 2016, 66-71](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). Implementation warrant keeps it visible. Blocking the model-read route tests whether the retained explanation contributes to assessment. Blocking the commit route preserves the possibility of a correct account without its intended later effect. Intervening on the retained model tests whether its content can change the assessment through the declared read route. Removing compensation distinguishes a changed authority relation from continued dependence on downstream cancellation. These comparisons do more than establish that something internal causes an output. They test the proposed relation between knowing how a recommendation arises, assessing its future role and carrying that assessment into subsequent selection.

Even that evidence must be read at the right level. Direct neutralization can match the revised route’s raw performance without acquiring the model. Its success prevents us from identifying improved output with recognition. The distinctive claim concerns a route in which an account is acquired, retained, used and assessed under a criterion that can favor either retention or revision. Its epistemic contribution lies in making those differences available to judgment. Ismael’s distinction between predicting normal evolution and knowing a system’s working mechanisms is useful here: working knowledge supports judgments about hypothetical interventions that an intact trajectory alone does not disclose ([Ismael 2016, 118-28](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). The finite tests investigate that additional structure without claiming complete knowledge of the participant or a privileged view outside its history.

This account fits a temporally complete history because understanding changes which practical relations obtain within that history. At the relevant stage, a retained description may become available, an assessment may be formed, and a previously installed rule may cease to govern later action. Their occurrence does not require the earlier history to disappear or the complete history to contain incompatible outcomes. Counterfactual comparisons ask how admitted changes in preparation or operation affect the process while specified structure remains fixed. They do not hold the entire world fixed and then alter one of its constituents. Ismael’s concession about complete microscopic fixation is therefore essential: her constituted author does not acquire authorship by occupying an exception to that fixation ([Ismael 2016, 107-8](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). The present argument concerns what understanding contributes through the actual organization of judgment.

<a id="section-10-3"></a>

### 10.3 Self-formation and historical ownership

There is a corresponding limit on self-formation. Reflection can reorganize inherited dispositions, shape later environments and establish commitments that influence further reflection. Ismael develops this as a graded process and acknowledges difficult cases involving manipulation and damaging upbringing ([Ismael 2016, 99-105](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)). Such development can deepen historical ownership without making the person author of the whole basis on which reflection first became possible. Repeating the process does not supply the missing ultimate authorization. Its positive significance is more specific: a received capacity can be exercised in forming this judgment, and the resulting commitment can become a warranted element in later judgment. Whether that supports particular forms of moral responsibility requires further premises about the person’s circumstances, opportunities and the responses being justified.

Nor should coherence across time confer unrestricted authority on a person’s earlier conception of their life. A long-term project can organize otherwise conflicting activities, but its persistence does not establish its merit. New evidence, altered responsibilities or an improved understanding of harm can warrant reconsideration. Conversely, present discomfort need not defeat a commitment whose grounds remain sound. The practical question concerns the reasons for allowing one judgment to govern another across time. Preserving records while revising their authority allows earlier experience to inform that question without dictating its answer. It also explains why relapse and successful maintenance are substantive matters: retained understanding, continuing endorsement and effective application can come apart. Inquiry remains part of the continuing participant rather than the achievement of a final controller.

The philosophical bridge is thus conditional but positive. Understanding can warrant a change in authority when it supplies a credible account of a process, exposes a discrepancy under independently defensible standards, and supports an executable response to that discrepancy. It can warrant retention when the same examination confirms the commitment’s grounds or shows revision to be unwarranted under actual constraints. The three warrants in Section [9](/consciousness/constituted-agency/09-recognition-and-authority#section-9) articulate these conditions without demanding an author who first created them. Ismael’s constituted avowal explains how the ensuing judgment can belong to the person; the present distinctions explain why its authority still requires assessment. This remains a functional account with a declared human interpretation. Ismael explicitly brackets an explanation of phenomenal consciousness ([Ismael 2016, 76](/consciousness/constituted-agency/21-references#ref-ismael2016)), and the binary construction supplies no such explanation. Its contribution is to make a limited relation between understanding, warranted commitment and practical self-revision available for examination.
