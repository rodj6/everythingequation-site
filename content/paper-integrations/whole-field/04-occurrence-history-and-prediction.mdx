## 7. Constitutive extent and numerical occurrence identity

**The whole occurrence is an actual token, not merely a matching pattern.** Two descriptions of one token do not create a second occurrence. Two separately enacted tokens can match in organization and contents without becoming numerically identical.

The realization must first separate current constituents from support and formative influence. A past encounter can form a disposition without remaining a current implementing event. A remote process can support an operation or participate directly in its constitution, depending on its actual realized relations. Indispensability alone decides neither present content nor physical membership. The complete realization identifies the relations and implementation required by the account; it does not collect every cause in the unit's ancestry.

Containment is another relation. Suppose $O$ has dynamics $T_u^O$, while a containing apparatus $H$ adds a record state $z$:

<a id="equation-5"></a>
$$
T_u^H(o,z)=\bigl(T_u^O(o),G_u(o,z)\bigr). \tag{5}
$$

Projection onto $o$ preserves the original trajectory. The added record can concern the enactment without entering a conjoint interpretation at the containing scale. An extra enclosure or recording channel therefore does not establish another experiencing unit. Conversely, restricting a description to $O$ is incomplete for the nominated $A$ occurrence when it omits an actual constituent or constitutive relation of $K_A$. The judgment concerns actual organization, not how many boxes a diagram contains.

On the adopted identity, $e_A=K_A$ denotes the same actual occurrence. It does not merely assert an isomorphism between types. Counting occurrences requires actual token identity. A common label, formal structure or report cannot supply it.

The perspective is identified with its qualifying enactment rather than assigned to another owner behind it. This belongs to the constitution; an ordinary rule for counting events does not independently derive owner identity. Within a fixed unit, actual incorporation and completeness distinguish an aspect from the whole. A claim about another unit needs its own qualification and realization. Containment settles neither that admission nor a universal claim of exclusivity.

## 8. Common-prefix invariance and later loss

An occurrence can extend through time while depending on organization enacted by its current stage. Reception, retention and uptake occupy physical events. They need not be compressed into a universal simultaneous slice.

Let $\Gamma_A(t)$ denote the complete relevant enacted prefix for the scoped assignment. It includes the unit, operative history, relevant realization and constitutive boundary facts. Fixed constitutive maps evaluate qualification and experiential organization on that complete argument.

<a id="proposition-8-1"></a>
### Proposition 8.1: common-prefix invariance

If two histories agree on $\Gamma_A(t)$ under the same constitutive maps, their assignment for the occurrence at $t$ is the same. Later loss of its record or later addressed retrieval cannot alter that earlier assignment.

**Proof.** The maps receive equal complete arguments and therefore yield equal values. Later events can alter later arguments, including subsequent access to the occurrence. They do not change the assignment to the earlier common prefix.

The agreement must include operative organization, not just matching answers or a neural snapshot. Where full A2 is jointly invoked, the native continuation contract, relevant dispositions and actual predictive point are included. They are features of the organization at the stage. They do not require future actual events already to have been received or a later acknowledgment to complete the earlier experience.

This locates later recollection correctly. Losing a trace after uptake changes what can later be retrieved. It does not remove that earlier uptake or its occurrence. When a construction is first taken up during a later reconstruction, that experience concerns an earlier event; it does not supply experience retrospectively. Optional $R_{\mathrm{later}}$ keeps the occurrence distinct from later evidence about it or access to it.

Current unity and temporal continuation also differ. A melody can change its content during a continuing activity. A record can survive an interruption. Neither fact alone determines identity across every temporal segment. The invariance result concerns a fixed earlier occurrence under continuations preserving its complete constitutive prefix.

## 9. Complete organization and the predictive framework

SPC-2 distinguishes perspective admission through A1, complete phenomenal organization through A2, and nonbranching continuation through A3 on a specified physical realization. The enacted-scaffolding formulation has not been proved equivalent to these laws. Its present within-unit constitution follows from the explicit identity, incorporation and unity principles. A joint application therefore requires an independently justified correspondence between the qualifying unit, its realizing organization and the SPC-2 assignment.

Full A2 identifies complete phenomenal organization with the complete endogenous predictive object, pointed by the realized predictive class. Native operation and record labels, every admitted finite continuation and the successor structure belong to that object. A current task state, passive trace or final answer cannot replace it. Consequently $\operatorname{Cur}_A$ in Equation (3) is a current-incorporation projection, rather than a replacement for complete organization.

That commitment yields a definite compatibility condition. Fix an exact intrinsic carrier $X$ and its native contract. Let $q_\infty:X\to Z_\infty$ be the complete native predictive quotient. Any fixed phenomenal property assigned jointly through A2 and the enacted correspondence must be constant on $q_\infty$ fibres. The final chapter states the full factorization criterion and its proof.

Equal current foregrounds can coexist with differences in complete organization. A dormant disposition need not be explicitly recalled in order to remain part of the relevant native structure. Stored organization and currently incorporated content are distinct without being disconnected.

An inventory of present items therefore leaves out roles, modes, realization and token identity. The content-summary results in Appendix A determine precisely what finite existential inventories preserve and provide a same-occurrence completeness certificate. Those results identify information supplied by a partial description. The enacted account instead supplies the actual unit enactment and its interpreted relations; it does not manufacture a whole from an inventory alone.

The published A0–A3 constitution and the [dated A0 clarification](/consciousness#a0-clarification) retain their own stated status. The within-unit account does not silently replace those laws or resolve subject selection, grain or general continuity by reusing a current-content projection.
