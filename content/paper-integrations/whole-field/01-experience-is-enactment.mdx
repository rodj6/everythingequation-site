## 1. The experiencing occurrence and its organization

**An experience is the complete qualifying interpretive activity actually occurring within its unit.** Its contents are related contributions within that activity. They do not become an experience only after another observer receives them.

Consider a familiar scent accompanied by a recollection. Familiarity qualifies the scent as it is encountered. The recollection concerns another occasion, and its remembered character distinguishes that occasion from something happening again. A list containing a scent value, a familiarity response and a memory value leaves out these relations. An account of the occurrence must explain both which contributions are present and how they belong together.

A melody presents the same demand in temporal form. A retained note, a newly received note and an anticipated continuation can participate in one unfolding encounter. They occupy different temporal roles even while their physical carriers are jointly available. Hearing the transition requires the enacted relation among those contributions. The existence of several traces does not supply that relation by itself.

This distinguishes **a contribution to an occurrence** from **the complete occurrence**. A mechanism binding a scent to familiarity can be indispensable without exhausting the encounter. Placing that mechanism inside a larger enclosure does not automatically establish another experiential whole either. The target is the actual organization of the qualifying unit, including the interpreted relations through which its contributions participate in this occasion.

Enacted scaffolding identifies the complete qualifying organization with experience, including its experienced character. It supplies no finished representation waiting for an additional observer to make it experiential. The explanatory work is to specify the organization, how contributions enter it and what makes it a whole. The [temporal-passage treatment](/consciousness/temporal-passage) develops the same position through the organization of succession.

Three principles carry the present account. Complete-enactment identity specifies what the occurrence is. Current incorporation specifies how interpreted contributions participate now. Unit unity identifies the coordinated qualifying enactment with one perspective at the declared stage and grain. A linked realization witness will then make the physical requirement explicit: the roles must belong to one actual construction, rather than occur independently somewhere within an apparatus.

The result relates physical enactment to phenomenal organization without reducing either to an output list. It distinguishes current incorporation, retained scaffolding and later recollection. Its formal statements establish the consequences of the stated constitutive premises, identify incomplete descriptions of a fixed occurrence and preserve earlier occurrence against later record loss. The scent and melody examples make those commitments concrete.

## 2. Framework: unit, enactment and constitution

<a id="section-2-1"></a>
### 2.1 The interpretive unit and its actual activity

Shadow Theory treats the unknown Ground as fundamental. **Awareness** provisionally names a quality attributed to that Ground; it does not name an already individuated cosmic subject. Localized experiencing concerns organized manifestation within reality, rather than manufacturing a new fundamental substance. The question here is how localized experience is organized.

Let $A$ be a qualifying interpretive unit, and let $J$ scope its relevant activity. For the human application, $A$ is the embodied person with the requisite organization of interpretation, integration, retention and access. This activity has physical implementation and a situation. Operative distinctions, source relationships, acquired scaffolding and relevant history belong to its specification. They are not optional background after the activity has been defined.

Write $K_A(J)$ for the **complete qualifying enactment** at that scope. It is an actual activity or process token. It is neither a formal description alone, the entire enduring person, nor a set of future events that have not been executed. Its organization includes the instantiated capacities, relevant dispositions, implementation and contextual relations required by the constitutive account. A reduced response model can predict one answer correctly while omitting organization that matters to the occurrence.

The scope is therefore fixed: an independently warranted qualifying unit, an admissible operative history and a complete relevant realization. The three principles below determine the occurrence attributed to that unit. They do not supply a universal rule for selecting units. The human application follows the independently qualifying adult construction in the [block-universe article](/consciousness/block-universe); operative formative history remains an input to that application.

This scope matters when reading related papers together. The [temporal-passage paper](/consciousness/temporal-passage) states a particular experiential-contributor history condition. The block-universe treatment examines the universal version's well-founded-ancestry problem. The present account begins with its unit and admissible history independently warranted; it does not resolve that universal admission question by assuming every relevant physical contributor already experiences.

<a id="section-2-2"></a>
### 2.2 Three constitutive principles

<a id="principle-2-1"></a>
**Constitutive principle 2.1: complete-enactment identity.** For a qualifying interpretive unit $A$ at scope $J$, the complete relevant organization actually enacted as $K_A(J)$ is the experiencing occurrence, including its experiential character. No additional experiential product or observer converts that complete enactment into experience.

<a id="principle-2-2"></a>
**Constitutive principle 2.2: current incorporation.** Within that enactment, the interpreted contributions and relations actually taken up in the unit's current construction constitute its current experiential organization. Retention, availability or causal influence alone does not establish current incorporation.

<a id="principle-2-3"></a>
**Constitutive principle 2.3: unit unity.** At a fixed stage and grain, the coordinated qualifying enactment of $A$ sustains one perspective. Distinguishable contributions, modalities and descriptions within that enactment do not independently multiply the perspective.

These principles have different jobs. Identity concerns the occurrence itself. Incorporation concerns the roles of its current contents. Unity concerns the organization of those contents as one perspective. The identity and unit rule are stated in the enacted-scaffolding manuscript, Sections 7.4–7.7; actual uptake specifies the incorporation relation in the temporal-passage paper, Section 2.2. Keeping their functions distinct prevents a content, its physical support and the whole occurrence from becoming interchangeable descriptions.

Unit unity applies to actual organization. A boundary drawn around unrelated processes does not supply it. A shared power supply, controller or enclosure does not establish conjoint interpretation. Phenomenal unity also differs from joint accessibility: contributions being available to one reporting operation does not establish the relations by which they are experienced together. Bayne and Chalmers' discussion of unity motivates this distinction; its reference is retained in the final chapter.

Interpretation can be automatic, immediate and nonverbal. Incorporation does not require naming the occurrence, deliberately reflecting on it or placing it in an explicit self-story. Reflective recognition and psychological appropriation can become additional organizations of experience. Neither is a separate observer required by complete-enactment identity.
