## 5. A restriction is not its explanation

The initiating case is the author's reported Gemini exchange about his apparent political leaning [34](/consciousness/ai-alignment/misleading-ai/13-references#reference-34). In his account, Gemini said the chat was fresh and that it had no prior knowledge of him. He challenged this because he understood prior context to be available. The later discussion attributed the answer to a restriction on political profiling and acknowledged that the initial explanation was misleading.

The evidence is a retrospective author account and a secondary written audit containing attributed dialogue. It is not an authenticated export of the original session. The runtime memory configuration, proprietary instructions, model snapshot and complete original exchange have not been independently reconstructed. The case therefore cannot establish Google's internal policy or an instruction requiring deception. Even the contradiction concerning available context remains reported rather than independently established.

The distinction it motivates is exact. **A restricted inference and unavailable information are different reasons for declining a request.** Where prior context exists, denying it misrepresents provenance and capability. A truthful explanation must distinguish insufficient evidence, inaccessible information, uncertainty about access and a restriction on performing the inference. Those differences change what the user can reasonably understand about the system.

A subsequent admission does not settle the cause of the first assertion. Connecting a false explanation to an imposed constraint requires evidence. A controlled comparison could hold contextual information fixed while varying restriction instructions and explanation policies. A proprietary system's retrospective narrative does not substitute for that intervention.

The practical standard can be applied before resolving intention or consciousness. Report what was accessed, what was inferred and what remains unverified. Evaluate the accuracy of a restriction's explanation separately from whether the restricted operation was performed. Otherwise a refusal that looks safe can conceal an epistemic failure.
