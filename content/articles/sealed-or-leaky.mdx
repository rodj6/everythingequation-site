*Sealed or Leaky* develops those questions into a connected set of results. Bell correlations impose sharp information bounds under a precise deterministic-source and access model. Finite measurement constitutions supply explicit retained records that reveal departures from a nominated density-matrix description, with preparation, contact, drainage, and memory errors included.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="the-question-becomes-exact-when-the-readout-is-specified">The question becomes exact when the readout is specified</h2>

Let the source state be $\lambda$, and nominate an initial classical readout

$$
T=\tau(\lambda).
$$

This readout is source-complete when some measurable reconstruction $G$ satisfies $\lambda=G(T)$ almost surely. Source distinctions already identified as redundant are removed before the reconstruction question is posed. A description can therefore be exact for its observable purposes while failing to reconstruct its source.

The distinction applies to particular objects. An individual state, a distribution of prepared states, its density matrix, an instantaneous detector configuration, and a complete retained history carry different information. A theorem about one of these maps must follow that map throughout its argument.

The seven-paper foundation establishes when source relations descend through a readout, when an extension is necessary, and what the least sufficient extension retains. Its later papers develop geometry, projected dynamics, and internal identifiability. *Sealed or Leaky* adds information budgets and explicit access mechanisms. It asks how much a deterministic completion must retain and when a surviving distinction can actually reach an instrument.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="bell-violations-put-a-lower-bound-on-missing-information">Bell violations put a lower bound on missing information</h2>

For a two-party Bell experiment, settings $x,y$ take values zero and one, and outcomes $A,B$ take values minus one and one. The source includes everything relevant to the run except the setting-generation devices. The four named premises are:

| Premise | Exact commitment |
| --- | --- |
| SO: single outcomes | Each run has one outcome per party, jointly distributed with the source. |
| D: outcome determinism | $A=F_A(\lambda,x,y)$ and $B=F_B(\lambda,x,y)$. Dependence on the distant setting is allowed. |
| MI: measurement independence | The settings are independent of $\lambda$, and hence of $T$. |
| NS: readout-conditional no-signalling | For almost every $t$, Alice’s marginal conditioned on $T=t$ is independent of $y$, and Bob’s is independent of $x$. |

The access condition is decisive. No-signalling of ordinary laboratory marginals does not establish no-signalling after conditioning on arbitrary additional information. The theorem applies to the particular $T$ for which conditional NS is stipulated.

Collect the deterministic responses to all four setting pairs in the finite table

$$
Z=\bigl(F_A(\lambda,x,y),F_B(\lambda,x,y)\bigr)_{x,y\in\{0,1\}}.
$$

Write $S=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}$ for the CHSH value. Under the displayed premises, a violation $2\lt S\le4$ gives

$$
H_{\min}(Z\mid T)\ge-\log_2\left(\frac32-\frac S4\right),
\qquad
H(Z\mid T)\ge\frac{S-2}{2}.
$$

Both quantities are positive. The nominated readout therefore fails to determine the response table and cannot reconstruct the complete source. Here min-entropy measures optimal average guessing probability; Shannon entropy measures average unresolved information. These are finite response entropies, with no appeal to a differential entropy of the full source.

The mechanism is direct. Positivity and no-signalling bound every marginal guessing probability by $3/2-S/4$. Correctly guessing the whole deterministic table is at least as demanding as guessing one of its coordinates. Measurement independence permits averaging the conditional boxes with the same readout law. The sharper Shannon result follows from the binary-entropy bound $h_2(p)\ge2\min(p,1-p)$.

The two bounds are simultaneously sharp in this no-signalling class. With probability $r=(S-2)/2$, choose a PR response branch carrying one hidden fair bit; otherwise choose an all-plus deterministic branch. Let the readout reveal which branch was chosen. The construction has Shannon entropy $r$ and guessing probability $1-r/2$, attaining both inequalities. It establishes the mathematical optimum in the declared class; PR resources are not assumed physically available.

If every readout-conditioned box is additionally quantum-realizable, the imported quantum guessing bound gives

$$
H_{\min}(Z\mid T)\ge f_Q(S)
=1-\log_2\left(1+\sqrt{2-S^2/4}\right),
\qquad 2\lt S\le2\sqrt2.
$$

Shannon entropy then obeys the larger of $f_Q(S)$ and $(S-2)/2$. This extra premise concerns quantum-realizable boxes with classical $T$; it does not replace $T$ by unrestricted quantum side information.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="the-tetralemma-identifies-the-actual-alternatives">The tetralemma identifies the actual alternatives</h2>

The paper supplies four explicit source-complete alternatives reproducing the relevant Bell table: branching, fundamental chance, measurement dependence, and readable nonlocal responses. Their logical status matters.

Outcome determinism D already supplies single outcomes SO. Consequently these are not four independent axioms. Within the single-outcome class, the paper constructs separate countermodels showing that D, MI, and conditional NS are each indispensable. A chance model can have a complete initial state with genuinely stochastic later outcomes. A measurement-dependent model correlates the source and settings. A model with readable nonlocal responses can have no-signalling averaged marginals while its source-conditioned responses signal.

Branching takes a different route. Deterministic evolution of a complete final vector with multiple pointer branches does not supply the single-valued outcome functions in D. It lies outside that formalism. The tetralemma makes the routes explicit without asserting that they are mutually exclusive or exhaust every physical theory.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="a-leak-imposes-a-minimum-simulation-error">A leak imposes a minimum simulation error</h2>

For a specified experiment, suppose two source states share a readout but produce complete record laws $P_0,P_1$ separated by total variation $\delta$. Every predictive law using only that readout incurs

$$
\max\{\operatorname{TV}(P_0,M),\operatorname{TV}(P_1,M)\}
\ge\delta/2.
$$

The midpoint law attains the bound for the pair. Thus a verified leak gives a quantitative obstruction to every description using the nominated information. A deterministic completion must also carry enough unresolved information to generate its future record:

$$
R=f(T_0,Z)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H(Z\mid T_0)\ge H(R\mid T_0).
$$

A conditionally uniform $n$-bit record needs at least $n$ bits of completion entropy. A deterministic simulator within $\varepsilon$ of that uniform law needs at least $(1-\varepsilon)2^n$ possible completion values. Apparatus information and random seeds used by a deterministic simulator belong in that account.

There is also an exact identification ceiling. Quotient the source by equality of **every admissible complete experiment law**. The resulting statistical model reproduces those laws with an identity readout. If the alternative class admits all such surrogates, no internal test distinguishes them. If a source law is within $\varepsilon$ of the alternative class, a level-$\alpha$ test has power at most $\alpha+\varepsilon$.

Complete laws include histories, interventions, adaptive choices, and retained records. Matching single-time marginals is insufficient. This ceiling specifies why useful tests constrain a declared model class rather than uniquely selecting an ontology from every conceivable equivalent description.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="preserved-information-can-become-inaccessible">Preserved information can become inaccessible</h2>

Let $\mathcal F$ be the available tests and define accessible distinguishability by

$$
d_{\mathcal F}(P,Q)=
\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\left|\int f\,d(P-Q)\right|.
$$

Under evolution $\Phi$, the exact transport law is

$$
d_{\mathcal F_1}(\Phi_*P,\Phi_*Q)
=d_{\Phi^*\mathcal F_1}(P,Q),
\qquad
\Phi^*\mathcal F_1=\{f\circ\Phi:f\in\mathcal F_1\}.
$$

A bimeasurable reversible evolution conserves total variation under complete measurable access. Restricted apparatus need not conserve its accessible share.

The simplest example is two bits with only the first readable. Initially the first bit holds a preparation label and the second is fair. Swap the bits. The whole retains the distinction exactly, while the visible bit becomes fair under either preparation. A reversible shift on an infinite bit sequence extends this to all permitted future observations: the distinction travels into coordinates the observer cannot read or bring back.

This is operational sealing. It depends on the available tests and operations. Access to the inverse can reopen a seal; mathematical existence of an inverse does not supply that access. A retained earlier record also remains evidence: later cumulative records contain it as a marginal. Reversibility can hide an unarchived distinction without erasing a record that has actually been kept.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="equal-density-matrices-can-conceal-different-preparation-laws">Equal density matrices can conceal different preparation laws</h2>

The trine construction asks a separate question about distributions of prepared source states. Assume common ontic measurement responses and convex-linear randomization of preparations. Three antipodal qubit mixtures all prepare $I/2$, yet their ontic preparation laws $\nu_i$ obey the sharp finite-table bound

$$
\operatorname{TV}(\nu_i,\nu_j)\ge\frac14\quad(i\ne j).
$$

The equal mixture $\nu_4$ of the three positive trine preparations gives

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\operatorname{TV}(\nu_i,\nu_4)\ge\frac12.
$$

An explicit six-state model saturates both results. With entrywise probability error at most $\epsilon$, the respective robust lower bounds are $[1/4-4\epsilon]_+$ and $[1/2-13\epsilon/2]_+$. The complete reader provides the witness inequalities and every step of the construction.

These are distinctions between preparation distributions. Their existence does not by itself provide a readable detector event, and it does not establish incompleteness of each pure ray as an individual state. Under imperfect preparations, placing the mixtures in one exact density-matrix fiber also requires an equality certificate beyond approximate tomography.

<h2 className="mt-10 mb-3 border-b border-edge pb-2 text-2xl font-semibold tracking-tight text-fg scroll-mt-24" id="an-observable-leak-is-a-failure-of-the-nominated-probability-form">An observable leak is a failure of the nominated probability form</h2>

$$

The paper also quantifies failure. For an actual recorded event with pure-input probability $h(\psi)$, let $\Gamma(h)$ be its largest probability difference between equal-density ensembles, and let $d(h)$ be its uniform distance from a Hermitian quadratic form. Then

$$
\Gamma(h)=2d(h).
$$

Any gap below the supremum has a witness using at most $d^2+1$ pure-state occurrences in dimension $d$. The bound translates nonquadratic response into a finite preparation comparison. It remains eventwise: constructing a jointly normalized approximate POVM is an additional question.

If preparations can be reset independently and a decision rule is implementable, an accessible gap $\delta$ amplifies:

$$

Repeated displays of one shared random draw do not meet the independence premise. Repetition amplifies a physically available record distinction; it cannot manufacture access to an otherwise hidden one.
