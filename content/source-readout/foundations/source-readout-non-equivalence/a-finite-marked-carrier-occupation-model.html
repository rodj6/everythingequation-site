A finite marked-carrier occupation model

We now realize the equivariant obstruction in a completely explicit finite setting.

The physical status of the marks is a modeling declaration, and here it is made explicitly: the carrier identities are physically meaningful source distinctions. Remark 23 (The modeling boundary).

The obstruction in Example 7.3 depends entirely on the declaration made after (25), and it is worth stating exactly where the line falls.

Suppose instead that the carrier marks were declared physically redundant , that permuting which carrier is which is a relabeling and not a physical operation. Then the permutations (29) belong to ≃phys\simeq_{\mathrm{phys}}≃phys​, and the reduction of Definition 2.1 must be performed with respect to them. The reduced source space becomes S′={1,…,K}{1,…,N}/SN,S'=\{1,\ldots,K\}^{\{1,\ldots,N\}}\big/ S_{N},S′={1,…,K}{1,…,N}/SN​, whose elements are exactly the occupation vectors: two assignments have the same SNS_{N}SN​-orbit if and only if they induce the same (n1,…,nK)(n_{1},\ldots,n_{K})(n1​,…,nK​), by the transitivity established in Proposition 7.2. The induced readout p′ ⁣:S′→Tp'\colon S'\to Tp′:S′→T is then a bijection, every reduced fiber is a singleton, and by Proposition 3.2 the readout is source-equivalent. The obstruction has vanished , and correctly so, because on this declaration there was never anything to lose.

The two models are mathematically adjacent and physically opposite. The theorems of Section 4, Section 6 detect lost source structure only after the theory has independently specified which distinctions are physical. They do not, and cannot, make that specification on the theory’s behalf. In particular, in Example 7.3 the group SNS_{N}SN​ is not a gauge group: it acts on physically distinct states and its orbits are not points of the reduced source space. Treating it as gauge would be a different model, the one just described, with a different and trivial conclusion.
