Equivariant reconstruction

In structured settings one typically wishes to restrict attention to sections compatible with the structure at hand, and the results below concern one such restriction. Suppose the theory carries a symmetry acting on both source and readout and respected by the readout map. A selection rule that fails to respect that symmetry introduces a preference among source states that the symmetry does not distinguish, and that preference has to come from somewhere.

The status of this restriction should be stated carefully, because the theorem below is conditional and its interpretation must be too. If GGG-equivariance is imposed as an admissibility requirement on reconstruction, then failure of the stabilizer-fixed-point condition excludes every selector in that class , which is a strong and unconditional mathematical conclusion within the class. It is not a claim that a non-equivariant selector is inherently unlawful or physically impossible. A non-equivariant selection rule may be perfectly legitimate once it is justified by something further: symmetry-breaking data, boundary conditions, an environment, additional structure on SSS, or an openly stipulated convention.

The distinction must be drawn carefully, since it is easy to overstate. A non-equivariant section is, formally, a map T→ST\to ST→S like any other: it takes a readout value as input and returns a source state, and in that sense it is certainly a function of the readout variable. What it is not is determined by the readout and the declared GGG-symmetric structure alone. Its particular choice among the many available maps T→ST\to ST→S cannot be selected, derived, or justified from those ingredients; it encodes an additional convention or symmetry-breaking ingredient supplied from outside them, and different conventions yield different and equally admissible non-equivariant sections with no symmetric ground for preferring one. The obstruction is therefore an obstruction relative to a declared reconstruction class, and the class must be declared.

This section characterizes exactly when a GGG-equivariant selection rule exists.

Selecting one of them is then necessarily an act of breaking the symmetry by fiat.

Remark 16 (Where choice enters).

Two independent selections were made in the proof of 3⇒\Rightarrow⇒1, and both require a choice assumption; neither one suffices alone.

The first is the selection of one orbit representative tit_{i}ti​ from each GGG-orbit in TTT: a choice function for the family { O }O∈T/G\{\,\mathcal{O}\,\}_{\mathcal{O}\in T/G}{O}O∈T/G​ of nonempty orbits. The second is the selection of one point sis_{i}si​ from each nonempty fixed set FixGti(Fti)\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}_{G_{t_{i}}}(F_{t_{i}})FixGti​​​(Fti​​): a choice function for the family {FixGti(Fti)}i∈I\{\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}_{G_{t_{i}}}(F_{t_{i}})\}_{i\in I}{FixGti​​​(Fti​​)}i∈I​ indexed by the representatives produced by the first selection. The second family is not the first, and hypothesizing choice for the orbits alone leaves the fixed points unselected. This is exactly the point recorded in Remark 6.5: a nonempty FixGti(Fti)\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}_{G_{t_{i}}}(F_{t_{i}})FixGti​​​(Fti​​) may have more than one element, and nothing in the hypotheses distinguishes one of them. Both choice functions are therefore needed, and it is their conjunction that the theorem assumes.

Neither assumption is needed when T/GT/GT/G is finite and each relevant fixed set is finite, since finite choice is a theorem; nor when the problem independently supplies canonical orbit representatives and canonical fixed points, in which case the selections are definable rather than chosen.

The obstruction direction is a different matter. The implication 1⇒\Rightarrow⇒2 constructs nothing: it merely evaluates a given σ\sigmaσ at a given ttt and observes that σ(t)\sigma(t)σ(t) is fixed. It uses no choice principle whatever, and consequently Corollary 6.6, Corollary 6.7, Example 7.3, Proposition 9.1, and the equivariant half of Theorem 10.1 , that is, every negative result in this paper , are choice-free.

Remark 17 (Existence, uniqueness, and canonicity are three things).

Theorem 6.3 characterizes the existence of an equivariant section. It characterizes nothing else, and three statements should be kept apart.

Existence. An equivariant section exists if and only if every stabilizer fixes a point of its fiber (together with the choice assumptions of Remark 6.4). This is the theorem.

Uniqueness. An equivariant section need not be unique. If some FixGt(Ft)\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}_{G_{t}}(F_{t})FixGt​​(Ft​) contains two distinct points s≠s′s\neq s's=s′, then the construction (23) run with sss and with s′s's′ produces two equivariant sections differing at ttt, and both are equally valid. Nonempty fixed sets certify possibility, not uniqueness.

Canonicity. A fortiori, an element of FixGt(Ft)\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}_{G_{t}}(F_{t})FixGt​​(Ft​) is not thereby a canonical representative. It is a representative that the symmetry permits; the symmetry has simply declined to rule it out. Selecting among several permitted representatives requires a principle external to GGG, and any such principle is additional structure, not a consequence of the symmetry.

The theorem should therefore be read as delimiting a reconstruction class, not as producing a preferred reconstruction within it. Where the fixed set happens to be a singleton for every ttt, the equivariant section is unique and is canonical relative to GGG; but that is an extra hypothesis, and it is not what Theorem 6.3 asserts.

Corollary 6.7 isolates the mechanism in its cleanest form. If the readout-invisible symmetries can move any point of a fiber to any other, then no point of that fiber is distinguished, and any selection must distinguish one. The resulting obstruction is not the observation that ppp is many-to-one, and it is important to see why. A many-to-one map may perfectly well admit sections that are continuous, measurable, smooth, or equivariant; being many-to-one is compatible with all of these. What Corollary 6.6, Corollary 6.7 identify is a specific incompatibility between the act of selecting a representative and the symmetry that the readout preserves. It is the symmetry, not the mere coarseness, that does the work.

Two further observations delimit the result. First, the obstruction is sensitive to the group: enlarging or shrinking GGG changes the stabilizers GtG_{t}Gt​ and can create or destroy fixed points. A section may exist equivariantly for one symmetry and not for another. Second, the obstruction is to deterministic selection. Section 7 exhibits a case in which no equivariant deterministic section exists while an equivariant probability-valued assignment on fibers does.
