Representative selection is not reconstruction

Noninjectivity of ppp excludes recovery of the realized source state by a map of readout data. It does not, by itself, exclude the weaker operation of choosing, for each readout value, some source state that would have produced it. The two are routinely conflated, and the distinction matters.

The substantive question is never whether some section exists, but whether one exists that is compatible with the structure the problem carries: equivariance, continuity, smoothness, measurability, locality, covariance. What a section is not, in any case, is an inverse.

The asymmetry of the two identities (15) and (16) is the whole point. The first says: whatever source state σ\sigmaσ nominates, it does produce the observed readout. The second says: the source state σ\sigmaσ nominates is the one that was actually there. The first is a consistency condition on a convention; the second is a claim of recovery. A section supplies a representative convention; only a two-sided inverse supplies source reconstruction, and by Proposition 5.2 a two-sided inverse exists precisely in the degenerate case in which nothing was lost.
