Invariant ensembles versus deterministic selection

The obstruction of Example 7.3 is an obstruction to equivariant deterministic selection. Weakening the demand from a point of the fiber to a probability distribution over the fiber removes it, and it is important to see both that this is so and that it does not amount to reconstruction. Making this precise requires typing the weakened object correctly: it is not a section of ppp, because its values do not lie in SSS.

An ensemble is not a realized state

The contrast is precise, and it locates the obstruction correctly. Symmetry is capable of determining a distinguished ensemble over a fiber: here the uniform distribution is in fact the unique SNS_{N}SN​-invariant element of Pfin(S)\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(S)Pfin​(S) supported on FnF_{n}Fn​, since invariance under a transitive action forces equal weights on all points of the fiber. What symmetry cannot do is single out a point. The reason is visible in the proof: the object that survives the group action is precisely the object that has forgotten which element of the fiber it is concentrated on.

Consequently an equivariant probability-valued lift is not a reconstruction of the realized source state, and the existence of the former is no evidence for the availability of the latter. In any given realization one specific assignment s∈Fns\in F_{n}s∈Fn​ obtains , one specific marked carrier is the one sitting alone in cell 111 , and μn\mu_{n}μn​ assigns that assignment probability 1/∣Fn∣1/|F_{n}|1/∣Fn​∣, exactly the same weight it assigns to every other element of the fiber. The kernel μ\muμ is a correct, symmetry-compatible description of the readout-conditional uncertainty about sss; it is not, and does not become, a determination of sss. Nothing in μn\mu_{n}μn​ distinguishes the realized state from the ∣Fn∣−1|F_{n}|-1∣Fn​∣−1 states that were not realized.

The general moral is that weakening the target of reconstruction weakens the conclusion by exactly as much. A statistical reconstruction and an exact realization reconstruction are different mathematical tasks with different success conditions, and success at the first is silent about the second.
