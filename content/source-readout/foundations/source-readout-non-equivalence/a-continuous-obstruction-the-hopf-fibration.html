A continuous obstruction: the Hopf fibration

The occupation model is finite, its symmetry group is finite, and no topology is involved. We now exhibit the obstruction in a continuous setting, where two logically independent mechanisms are simultaneously available. It is essential to keep them apart: one is an instance of Corollary 6.7 and uses no topology, the other is topological and uses no equivariance.

Second obstruction: no continuous section

The second obstruction is of an entirely different kind: it concerns continuity, not symmetry, and it does depend on the topology of the bundle.

Remark 30 (The two obstructions are independent).

Proposition 9.1, Proposition 9.3 are not two proofs of one fact. They exclude different things, by different means, and neither implies the other.

That the equivariant obstruction does not entail the topological one is settled by an example. Let P=S2×U(1)→S2P=S^{2}\times U(1)\to S^{2}P=S2×U(1)→S2 be the trivial principal U(1)U(1)U(1)-bundle, with U(1)U(1)U(1) acting by translation on the fiber factor and trivially on the base. Then c1(P)=0c_{1}(P)=0c1​(P)=0 and PPP admits continuous global sections in profusion , for instance x↦(x,1)x\mapsto(x,1)x↦(x,1). Nevertheless the hypotheses of Proposition 9.1 hold verbatim: every stabilizer is all of U(1)U(1)U(1), which acts freely and transitively on each fiber, so FixU(1)(Fx)=∅\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}_{U(1)}(F_{x})=\varnothingFixU(1)​(Fx​)=∅ and Corollary 6.6 excludes every U(1)U(1)U(1)-equivariant section. A bundle can therefore have no equivariant section while having continuous sections, and Proposition 9.1 cannot be a proof of Proposition 9.3 in disguise.

The converse direction must be argued differently, and the same trivial-bundle example does not serve; it establishes one implication’s failure, not both. What can be said is the following. The two propositions constrain distinct admissibility classes, and the class is not determined by the map: it is fixed by the additional data one specifies , a group action in one case, a topology in the other. Neither proof, taken by itself, establishes the other’s conclusion for an independently specified group action or an independently specified topology, since neither proof mentions the other’s data. Concretely, if one takes the group to be trivial, G={1}G=\{1\}G={1}, then every set-theoretic section of hhh is automatically GGG-equivariant, so the equivariant reconstruction class is unobstructed for that group; yet Proposition 9.3 continues to exclude every continuous section of hhh. The topological obstruction thus persists in a setting where the equivariant obstruction is vacuous, and it plainly does not follow from equivariance considerations, which in that setting say nothing at all.

Set-theoretic sections of hhh, meanwhile, exist whenever a choice function for the fibers does; the axiom of choice supplies one, and no obstruction of either kind touches them. The Hopf map is thus a single example on which three admissibility conditions , bare choice, U(1)U(1)U(1)-equivariance, continuity , yield three different verdicts, and the verdicts are logically separate.

The lesson generalizes beyond the example. The conditions one imposes on a reconstruction , equivariance, continuity, smoothness, measurability, locality, covariance , are not decoration; they determine what obstructions are available and what the failure of reconstruction means. A bare set-theoretic choice of fiber representatives is possible whenever choice is available and is almost never physically meaningful on its own. Conversely, a proof that no continuous section exists says nothing about equivariance, and a proof that no equivariant section exists says nothing about continuity. Each requirement must be stated, and the corresponding obstruction proved, on its own.

The modeling boundary, again

The results just proved are theorems about the bundle hhh, and as theorems they stand without qualification. Their physical reading, however, depends on a declaration that the mathematics does not make, and the situation is exactly the one already met in Remark 7.4.

Remark 31 (Two readings of the Hopf example).

Applying Theorem 10.1 with S=S3S=S^{3}S=S3 and T=S2T=S^{2}T=S2 treats distinct points of a U(1)U(1)U(1) fiber as distinct elements of the reduced source. Two readings are available, and they are not interchangeable.

Reading one: the phase is physical. If the theory has independently declared that the fiber distinctions are physically meaningful , that two unit vectors differing by a phase are different source states, not two descriptions of one , then S3S^{3}S3 is the reduced source space in the sense of Definition 2.1, the fibers Ft≅U(1)F_{t}\cong U(1)Ft​≅U(1) are nonsingleton fibers of physically distinct states, and Proposition 9.1, Proposition 9.3 are obstructions to reconstructing physically meaningful lost structure. This is the reading under which the example instantiates Theorem 10.1, and it is legitimate only if the declaration has actually been made.

Reading two: the phase is gauge. If instead the U(1)U(1)U(1) action (41) is declared to be gauge redundancy , as it is in the usual reading of the overall phase of a normalized state vector , then the action belongs to ≃phys\simeq_{\mathrm{phys}}≃phys​, and Definition 2.1 requires that it be quotiented out before any source–readout comparison is made. The reduced source space is then S′=S3/U(1)  ≅  S2,S'=S^{3}/U(1)\;\cong\;S^{2},S′=S3/U(1)≅S2, the induced readout p′ ⁣:S′→S2p'\colon S'\to S^{2}p′:S′→S2 is the identity map under this identification, every reduced fiber is a singleton, and by Proposition 3.2 the readout is source-equivalent. Nothing physical has been lost, and Theorem 10.1 has no instance here.

Under reading two the no-section results retain their mathematical content but change their meaning entirely. Proposition 9.3 then says that there is no global continuous gauge choice on S2S^{2}S2: one cannot continuously pick a phase representative over the whole base, and any concrete phase convention must be introduced patchwise. That is a genuine and well-known fact about the bundle, and it is the reason gauge fields on S2S^{2}S2 require more than one chart. It is not a statement that some physically meaningful source structure has been lost, because on this reading there is no such structure in the fiber to lose.

The point to be drawn is therefore a negative one, and it should be stated plainly: bundle nontriviality is not, by itself, evidence of a physically richer source. A nonzero Chern class obstructs global continuous sections whatever the physical status of the fiber, and it obstructs them just as firmly when the fiber is pure gauge as when it is not. To convert the topology into a claim about lost realization structure, one must first supply the declaration that the fiber distinctions are physical , and that declaration is an input to the framework, never an output of it.
