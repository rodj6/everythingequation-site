Introduction

The problem inherited from Papers 1 and 2

This is the third paper of a sequence on source–readout non-equivalence. The first companion manuscript [1] established that a readout map p ⁣:S→Tp\colon S\to Tp:S→T need not admit descent for a given source-defined quantity: a function r ⁣:S→Vr\colon S\to Vr:S→V factors as r=rˉ∘pr=\bar r\circ pr=rˉ∘p if and only if rrr is constant on every fiber Ft=p−1(t)F_t=p^{-1}(t)Ft​=p−1(t), and equivariant refinements of this statement produce genuine obstructions. The second companion manuscript [2] sharpened the significance of that failure. Information destroyed by a readout is not by itself a defect; it becomes consequential only relative to a nominated target. Paper 2 characterized exactly when a target with correct-answer map a ⁣:S→AQa\colon S\to A_Qa:S→AQ​ is solvable from the readout alone (precisely when aaa is ∼p\sim_p∼p​-invariant), and, when it is not, constructed the coarsest extension of TTT sufficient to determine that single answer map.

What Paper 2 does not supply is the family-level construction. Physical practice almost never nominates a single answer map. One nominates a family: a collection of invariant source relations rαr_\alpharα​, indexed by a set AAA, each entering some independently specified mathematical or physical target. Assembling the single-target completions of Paper 2 one at a time does not answer the family question, because the completions do not simply compose: the object one wants is a single extension of the readout on which all the nominated relations become simultaneously well defined, and one wants it to be canonical, adding no distinctions beyond those the nominated family forces. Neither existence, nor minimality, nor uniqueness of such an object is formal from Paper 2.

What this paper does not prove

The theorem is model-relative. Once a reduced source space, a readout, and a nominated family of invariant relations or targets are specified, there is a canonical coarsest sufficient quotient. Nothing here shows that the source space is empirically real, that observed physics is a projection of a hidden source, that every lost distinction must be restored, that the full source is in general recoverable, or that any particular relation family is physically privileged absent independent justification. In particular, “minimal completion” is not “reconstruction of the source”: the two coincide only under the separation criterion of Proposition 12.1. We return to these limits in Section 13.
