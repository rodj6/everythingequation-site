Reduced source, readout, and the nominated relation family

The reduced source

We assume, as in Paper 1, that SSS has already been reduced: all declared gauge redundancy and all coordinate-labelling redundancy have been quotiented out. Concretely, one begins with a raw configuration space Ω\OmegaΩ carrying an equivalence relation ≃phys\simeq_{\mathrm{phys}}≃phys​ of physical indistinguishability, gauge equivalence, diffeomorphism relabelling, choice of chart, and forms q ⁣:Ω⟶S=Ω/ ⁣≃phys.(2.1) q\colon\Omega\longrightarrow S=\Omega/\!\simeq_{\mathrm{phys}}.\tag{2.1}q:Ω⟶S=Ω/≃phys​.(2.1) The order of operations matters and is not a convention we are free to reverse: reduction precedes completion. Eligibility is exactly the condition under which the raw quantity is a function of the physical state rather than of a representative of it. A quantity failing (2.2), a gauge potential, a component in a chosen frame, a coordinate label, is not a candidate relation, and no completion of the readout can make it one.

External nomination

Fix a set AAA and a family of eligible relations rα ⁣:S⟶Vα,α∈A.(2.3) r_\alpha\colon S\longrightarrow V_\alpha,\qquad \alpha\in A .\tag{2.3}rα​:S⟶Vα​,α∈A.(2.3) We stress the epistemic status of the family, because the mathematics below is only as meaningful as the nomination that feeds it. The relations rαr_\alpharα​ are nominated externally: each is selected because it enters an independently specified mathematical or physical target, an amplitude, a spectrum, a transport law, an interference pattern. Physical relevance is not inferred from fiber variation. That a function happens to be nonconstant on FtF_tFt​ shows only that the readout does not determine it; it does not show that the distinctions it makes are physical. Confusing the two would convert every artifact of a redundant description into a physical observable. The theorems below are therefore conditional in exactly one place, and it is the right place: they take the family as given and tell one, completely, what follows from it.

Definition 2.2 (The family completion problem).

An extension of the readout sufficient for the family (2.3) is a space EEE through which SSS maps onto, such that

  1. the original readout is retained: ppp factors through EEE; and

  2. every nominated relation becomes a well-defined function of the completed state: each rαr_\alpharα​ factors through EEE.

The problem is to construct the coarsest such extension: one that makes no distinction between source states beyond those forced by requirements (1) and (2), and to prove it canonical.

A sufficient extension is emphatically not required to distinguish source states on which both the readout and every selected relation agree. This is the entire content of minimality, and it is what separates completion from reconstruction.
