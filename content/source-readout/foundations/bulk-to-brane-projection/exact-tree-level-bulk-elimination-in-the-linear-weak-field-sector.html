Exact tree-level bulk elimination in the linear weak-field sector

The homogeneous sector evaluates the nonlinear local response but hides the spectral structure of the eliminated bulk gravitons. That structure is explicit at linear order [1, 6, 7].

Remark 6.1 (What the branch cut does and does not exclude).

A finite-dimensional classical linear system, or a finite set of free fields with a finite-order quadratic action evaluated at tree level, has only a finite rational pole structure and cannot reproduce (55)–(56) exactly. It does not follow that every four-dimensional theory is excluded. An interacting four-dimensional field theory, a nonlocal theory, an infinite tower, a continuum of fields, or a conformal sector reproduces a branch cut without difficulty , multiparticle thresholds generically produce cuts. See Sections 10 and 15, item 8.

Remark 6.3 (Status of the elimination).

This is a tree-level, linear-response calculation, and must not be confused with the exact nonlinear equation (38). Gauge fixing isolates the physical TT operator; brane bending restores the response required by the conserved four-dimensional source and its Ward identity. At tree level, ghost fields carry no external classical source and contribute no exchange term between conserved brane stresses; ghost and graviton determinants enter only beyond this approximation. Ultraviolet local terms at coincident points renormalize the brane tension, the Newton coupling, and admissible higher-curvature operators; the nonanalytic continuum term and its 1/r31/r^31/r3 residue at r>0r>0r>0 are insensitive to those local subtractions. Covariant conservation of the resulting response agrees with (42). The bulk initial state enters the full closed-time-contour functional through its density matrix: under the no-incoming/regular choice the forced retarded kernel is fixed; changing that choice changes both the homogeneous response and the fluctuation kernel N\mathbb NN.

The same distinction appears in the four-dimensional dual description (Section 10). One AdS patch corresponds to one cutoff CFT contribution; a two-sided geometry has two identical bulk regions and hence a doubled CFT polarization contribution. Duff and Liu [9] present the one-patch normalization explicitly; doubling both the bulk contribution and its CFT counterpart gives the two-sided convention used here. Their equality of the nonanalytic 1/r31/r^31/r3 residue therefore remains valid, but only after this conversion. No equation in this paper combines a one-sided Newton-constant relation with a two-sided junction condition.
