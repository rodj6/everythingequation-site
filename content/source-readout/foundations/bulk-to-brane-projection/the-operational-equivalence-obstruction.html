The operational-equivalence obstruction

Sections 3–8 establish nonclosure of the instantaneous brane state. That statement must be sharply distinguished from the far stronger claim that no autonomous description using only brane observables can exist. The stronger claim is false when arbitrary history dependence is allowed.

Remark 11.2 (Status of Lemma 11.1).

The equality (96) is mathematically immediate from the definition of pushforward; nothing in the proof is deep, and no claim of mathematical originality is made for it. Its significance here is interpretive. It establishes the exact boundary on what brane-only observations can discriminate, and hence fixes the strongest conclusion that the positive results of Sections 3–7 can support. The substantive work lies in identifying which restrictions on the alternative class do permit discrimination (Section 11.3), and in exhibiting , through the four-dimensional CFT description of Section 10 , that the obstruction is realized concretely in RS2 and not merely as a formal possibility.

The exact boundary of the conclusion

The witness of Section 3 proves p∘Φt≠Ut∘p(103) p\circ\Phi_t\ne U_t\circ p\tag{103}p∘Φt​=Ut​∘p(103) for every deterministic flow UtU_tUt​ on the specified instantaneous brane state, and , for any preparation class that resolves the two witness states, so that the conditional future law differs within a readout fiber , it also defeats universal Markov lumpability. Remark 11.3 (Identifiability is not a finite-data testing theorem).

Condition (104) is exact identifiability. Uniform statistical discrimination from a composite alternative class additionally requires separation of the source law from the closure of that class in an operational metric appropriate to the allowed protocols , total variation, or a relative-entropy rate. If the source law lies outside the class but can be approximated arbitrarily closely by its members, exact equivalence is absent while finite-data discrimination may nonetheless be arbitrarily difficult.

Useful independent restrictions include: a fixed finite set of free or weakly coupled instantaneous fields at a stated perturbative order; a Markov or finite-memory condition; fixed finite-pole local quadratic dynamics; a finite EFT basis with coefficients fixed before the test; a prescribed preparation/boundary-state class. Locality alone, or finitely many interacting field species alone, is insufficient, because multiparticle states generate branch cuts. If arbitrary kernels, continua, initial correlations, and complete-history states are admitted, the pushforward theory belongs to the alternative class and discrimination is impossible.
