Einstein–Cartan and Aharonov–Bohm comparisons

These two examples distinguish a genuine propagating source degree of freedom from an auxiliary variable and from an incomplete choice of observable state: Einstein–Cartan–Dirac torsion is auxiliary and introduces no same-readout fiber, whereas the Aharonov–Bohm phase is already determined once the observable state is chosen completely.

Einstein–Cartan–Dirac.

Minimal Einstein–Cartan–Dirac theory fails as the required propagating source-projection antecedent , not as a theory , because its torsion is auxiliary: it introduces no independent propagating degree of freedom, and hence no same-readout fiber of the kind Proposition 3.1 exhibits. There is no independent propagating fiber analogous to a remote RS2 wave packet , which is exactly what RS2 supplies and Einstein–Cartan does not.

Aharonov–Bohm.

The Aharonov–Bohm effect [19] remains a clean control for readout incompleteness: equal local curvature can coexist with different gauge-invariant holonomy and different interference phases. But holonomy is already a standard observable of four-dimensional gauge theory. The effect shows that a curvature-only state is incomplete; it does not show that a deeper source ontology has been observed. It is the correct model for “the readout you chose was too small,” not for “there is a hidden dimension.”
