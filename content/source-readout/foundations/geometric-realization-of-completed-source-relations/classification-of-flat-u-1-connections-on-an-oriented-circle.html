Classification of flat U(1)U(1)U(1) connections on an oriented circle

This section supplies an exact classification example: a restricted domain on which the separation hypothesis of Proposition 5.5 can be proved, and on which the geometrically realized completion can be identified on the nose.

The model and its conventions

Convention 6.1 (Scope of the circle model).

This section is a deliberate one-dimensional specialization, and two stipulations of the general framework are suspended for it. Both are recorded here, since leaving either implicit would create a genuine inconsistency with Section 2.

Dimension. The base is M=SL1M=S^1_LM=SL1​, so dim⁡M=1\dim M=1dimM=1 rather than the dim⁡M=4\dim M=4dimM=4 of Convention 2.1, and the metric is Riemannian rather than Lorentzian. Nothing in Sections 2–5 used the dimension or the signature: the groupoid, the orbit space, Definition 4.1, Theorem 5.3, and Proposition 5.5 are all dimension- and signature-agnostic, and it is only these that the present section invokes. The descent theorem and the variational apparatus of Sections 7–8 are not used here.

The metric and the allowed maps. A tension must be resolved explicitly. The model fixes the standard metric dx2\mathrm{d}x^{2}dx2 on SL1S^1_LSL1​ while also admitting every orientation-preserving diffeomorphism as an allowed base map; under the groupoid of Definition 2.8, whose arrows satisfy f∗g=g′f_*g=g'f∗​g=g′, a fixed metric would instead restrict the allowed base maps to its isometries, namely the rigid rotations. Of the three consistent ways to proceed, restricting the allowed diffeomorphisms to orientation-preserving isometries; transforming the metric along with the connection; or defining the circle source purely as the gauge quotient and treating the diffeomorphism question separately, we adopt the third, which is the formulation the arguments below actually use.

Accordingly, in this section the metric dx2\mathrm{d}x^{2}dx2 and its measure dx\mathrm{d}xdx are treated as fixed background structure, not as part of the configuration: the configuration is the connection AAA alone, and the reduced circle source is defined to be the gauge quotient Sgeo  :=  Cgeo ∘/Gau⁡(P).(37) S_{\mathrm{geo}}\;:=\; \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{\,\circ}\big/\operatorname{Gau}(P) .\tag{37}Sgeo​:=Cgeo∘​/Gau(P).(37) The background metric is what gives the operator DA\mathcal{D}_ADA​ of (60) its meaning, and it is not transformed by the maps considered below. The role of the orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms is then a separate matter, treated separately: they are not arrows acting on a metric they would have to preserve, but are used solely through the fact that they fix the homology generator ccc, and hence preserve holonomy. Lemma 6.8(1) shows that they consequently identify no classes beyond gauge equivalence, so that (37) loses nothing by omitting them; and Lemma 6.8(2) shows that the spectral relation is invariant under them for the same reason. Definition 6.2 is to be read in this light.

Remark 36 (Where the hypotheses are used).

Three hypotheses were used and cannot be dropped. Smoothness and periodicity of a,a′a,a'a,a′ give smoothness of uuu across the seam in Step 2. Connectedness of S1S^1S1, with π1(S1)≅Z\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}π1​(S1)≅Z generated by a single class, is what makes one period integral a complete invariant. Orientation, fixed in Definition 6.2, is what makes ∫0L\int_0^L∫0L​ well defined with a sign: reversing orientation sends Hol⁡↦Hol⁡−1\operatorname{Hol}\mapsto\operatorname{Hol}^{-1}Hol↦Hol−1, and it is precisely this ambiguity that reappears in Corollary 6.9. The declaration of the allowed diffeomorphism group as the orientation-preserving one is therefore part of the hypothesis of the theorem.

Corollary 6.7 is the sharpest illustration in this paper of the mechanism the sequence is about. The readout pFp_FpF​ is maximally degenerate, it is constant, so on its own it distinguishes nothing. Yet the reduced source is not a point: it is a circle of genuinely inequivalent physical configurations. The completion recovers exactly that circle, from a single global relation, on a domain where local curvature vanishes identically. Holonomy here is not one invariant among many: it separates all gauge orbits and therefore reconstructs the reduced geometric source completely. This is the mathematical content of the Aharonov–Bohm and Wu–Yang observations [1, 13].
