The geometric source domain

Configurations

Convention 2.1 (Smoothness, signature, and index conventions). Throughout the general theory, MMM is a smooth, connected, paracompact, oriented four-manifold, possibly with boundary ∂M\partial M∂M. The choice dim⁡M=4\dim M=4dimM=4 is made for definiteness in the physical application and is used nowhere essentially: Sections 2–5 hold verbatim in any dimension. Section 6 is a deliberate one-dimensional specialization, declared as such in Convention 6.1, and the dimensional stipulation of the present convention is suspended there. All fields are smooth unless a weaker regularity class is explicitly declared as part of the admissibility data. A Lorentzian metric has signature (−,+,+,+)(-,+,+,+)(−,+,+,+); a time-oriented Lorentzian metric is a pair (g,t)(g,\mathfrak{t})(g,t) consisting of a Lorentzian metric ggg together with a choice t\mathfrak{t}t of one of the two continuous global orientations of the timelike cone bundle of ggg (equivalently, the choice of a connected component of the set of ggg-timelike vector fields, when one exists). Not every Lorentzian metric admits such a choice; admitting one is a nontrivial global condition on ggg, and it is imposed below as part of admissibility rather than derived (see Remark 2.3). Greek indices run over 0,1,2,30,1,2,30,1,2,3 and are raised and lowered with ggg. GGG denotes a finite-dimensional Lie group with Lie algebra g\mathfrak{g}g, equipped with a fixed Ad⁡\operatorname{Ad}Ad-invariant nondegenerate bilinear form ⟨⋅,⋅⟩\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle⟨⋅,⋅⟩ on g\mathfrak{g}g (for compact semisimple GGG one may take the negative Killing form; for G=U(1)G=U(1)G=U(1) the standard normalization on u(1)≅iR\mathfrak{u}(1)\cong i\mathbb{R}u(1)≅iR). Finally ρ ⁣:G→GL(W)\rho\colon G\to \mathrm{GL}(W)ρ:G→GL(W) is a fixed finite-dimensional representation; ρ\rhoρ is not assumed unitary, and where unitarity is needed it is hypothesized explicitly.

Definition 2.2 (Global geometric configuration). A global geometric configuration is a tuple Ψ=(M,  g,  P,  A,  Φ,  B),(2) \Psi=\bigl(M,\;g,\;P,\;A,\;\Phi,\;\mathcal{B}\bigr),\tag{2}Ψ=(M,g,P,A,Φ,B),(2) in which

  1. ggg is a Lorentzian metric on MMM;

  2. π ⁣:P→M\pi\colon P\to Mπ:P→M is a principal GGG-bundle;

  3. A∈Ω1(P,g)A\in\Omega^1(P,\mathfrak{g})A∈Ω1(P,g) is a principal connection on PPP [6, 3];

  4. Φ\PhiΦ is a section of the associated bundle P×ρW→MP\times_\rho W\to MP×ρ​W→M;

  5. B\mathcal{B}B denotes the boundary, asymptotic, causal, and global data required to make the model well posed. Among these data we always include a choice of time orientation t\mathfrak{t}t for ggg; the remaining components of B\mathcal{B}B are model-specific and may comprise, for instance, fixed induced data on ∂M\partial M∂M, a decay class at infinity, a fixed conformal boundary structure, a global hyperbolicity requirement, a spin structure, or a fixed topological sector.

Remark 3 (Where time orientation lives). Time orientation is a property of the pair (M,g)(M,g)(M,g), not of the bare smooth manifold MMM: it is meaningless to speak of MMM as “time-orientable” independently of a Lorentzian metric, since the timelike cone bundle whose components are being selected is defined by ggg. Accordingly the choice t\mathfrak{t}t is carried in B\mathcal{B}B, and the arrows of the redundancy groupoid are required below to preserve it. This has a consequence for the descent theorem, recorded explicitly in Theorem 3.4 and Remark 3.6: descent reconstructs the metric, but membership of the reconstructed (g,t)(g,\mathfrak{t})(g,t) in a time-oriented admissibility class is an additional global condition on the glued object, which either must be imposed after the fact or must be built into the descent data as compatible local time orientations.

Definition 2.4 (Admissibility class). An admissibility class Cgeo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​ is a specified class of tuples (2) satisfying declared conditions on regularity, signature, causal structure, boundary and asymptotic behaviour, and topological sector, and in which every ggg is time-orientable with a distinguished time orientation t∈B\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{B}t∈B. The class Cgeo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​ is part of the data of the model. All subsequent statements are relative to a fixed Cgeo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​.

The insistence that Cgeo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​ be declared is not pedantry. Every separation, realization, and conservation statement below is sensitive to it: a relation family may separate orbits in one admissibility class and fail to separate them in a larger one, and a Ward identity may hold with compactly supported variations and fail without a boundary term.

The redundancy groupoid

Gauge and coordinate freedom in a bundle-theoretic setting is not, in general, a group acting on a fixed configuration space: different admissible configurations may carry non-isomorphic bundles, and the diffeomorphisms that lift to a given bundle need not lift to another. The correct structure is a groupoid.

Remark 7 (The ambient group is not the isotropy group).

Proposition 2.6 describes the ambient group of allowed bundle automorphisms over a fixed PPP. It does not describe the isotropy group of the groupoid at a configuration. Aut⁡(Ψ)\operatorname{Aut}(\Psi)Aut(Ψ) is typically far smaller than Aut⁡B(P)\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{B}}(P)AutB​(P): for a generic (g,A,Φ)(g,A,\Phi)(g,A,Φ) it is a small or even trivial group, its base part being contained in the isometry group of ggg. Remark 10 (Set-theoretic hygiene).

SgeoS_{\mathrm{geo}}Sgeo​ is used below only as a set (equivalently, as the set of isomorphism classes of objects of the essentially small groupoid Ggeo\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{geo}}Ggeo​ obtained after fixing a set of bundle representatives). No smooth or stratified structure on SgeoS_{\mathrm{geo}}Sgeo​ is used or assumed; the orbit space of a gauge groupoid is in general not a manifold, precisely because isotropy jumps, as Remark 2.7 notes, and the arguments of Sections 5–6 do not require it to be. Where a topology is needed, as for the moduli space Mflat(S1,U(1))\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{flat}}(S^1,U(1))Mflat​(S1,U(1)) in Section 6, it is supplied and identified explicitly.

Convention 2.11 (Status of relation coordinates).

A relation coordinate rαr_\alpharα​ is a function on SgeoS_{\mathrm{geo}}Sgeo​, not an independent field on MMM. It is not varied independently in Section 7 unless a model explicitly promotes it to a field by adjoining it to the configuration tuple (2) and enlarging Cgeo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​ accordingly. Where rαr_\alpharα​ is a functional of (g,A,Φ)(g,A,\Phi)(g,A,Φ) its variation is computed by the functional chain rule of Section 7.2; where it labels a fixed topological or boundary sector it is held fixed under variations within that sector. Both cases are handled in Section 7; they are not conflated.

Every realization relation used in this paper is, by construction, a function on SgeoS_{\mathrm{geo}}Sgeo​ and never a function of an arbitrary coordinate chart or gauge potential.
