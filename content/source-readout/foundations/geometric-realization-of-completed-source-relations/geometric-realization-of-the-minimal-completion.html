Remark 29 (Relative, not absolute).

Theorem 5.3 is restricted in two independent senses, both essential. First, it realizes the completion only relative to the declared family {Jα}\{J_\alpha\}{Jα​}: a different family gives a different EAgeoE_A^{\mathrm{geo}}EAgeo​, generally neither finer nor coarser. Second, it realizes only what geometry attains: EAgeoE_A^{\mathrm{geo}}EAgeo​ is an image, and Corollary 3.7 shows that this image can be a proper subset of the abstract product. Neither the theorem nor anything else in this paper asserts that an arbitrarily nominated abstract completion EAE_AEA​ of Paper 3 is geometrically realizable. It is realizable exactly when it is isomorphic, over TTT, to EAgeoE_A^{\mathrm{geo}}EAgeo​ for some admissible Cgeo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​, readout, and invariant relation family, and establishing that in a given model is work.

Proposition 5.5 converts a question about completions into a question of invariant theory: does the declared family separate the orbits? No general answer exists, and none is asserted (Remark 4.6). Separation must be proved on the restricted domain relevant to the model. Section 6 carries out exactly such a proof in a case where the answer is affirmative and complete.
