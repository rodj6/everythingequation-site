Minimal sound uncertainty without completion

Suppose the obstruction is real: aQa_QaQ​ genuinely varies on some fiber, so no readout-only construction returns the correct answer everywhere. It does not follow that the readout is useless for QQQ. It follows that the readout cannot deliver a single answer, and the honest question becomes: what is the most that the readout does license? The answer is a set-valued rule, and there is a canonical least one.

Remark 5.3 (P\mathfrak{P}P is the power set, not a space of probability measures).

We use the deliberately distinct symbol P(AQ)\mathfrak{P}(A_Q)P(AQ​) to forestall a conflation that would destroy the meaning of the results in this section. A set-valued rule assigns to each readout value a subset of admissible answers. It does not assign a probability distribution, a measure, a density, or a credence. Nothing in Definition 5.2 presupposes a σ\sigmaσ-algebra on AQA_QAQ​, a measure on SSS, or a prior on the fibers, and no such structure is introduced anywhere in this paper. See Remark 5.6 below for why this distinction is substantive and not merely notational.

Remark 5.6 (Set-valued uncertainty is not probability, and probability is not recovery).

AQ(t)\mathsf{A}_Q(t)AQ​(t) is the exact residual uncertainty that the readout partition induces about the answer to QQQ , and it exists prior to, and independently of, any probabilistic structure. Two consequences must be kept apart.

First, imposing a probability law on SSS, or on the fibers, changes the object under discussion but does not shrink AQ(t)\mathsf{A}_Q(t)AQ​(t). A distribution on AQA_QAQ​ can rank the compatible answers, declaring some more likely than others. It cannot make two distinct correct answers into one answer, and it cannot identify which source state was in fact realized. We are careful about how to phrase the constraint this places on the distribution. We do not claim that an arbitrary probability law must assign positive mass to every element of AQ(t)\mathsf{A}_Q(t)AQ​(t); a law is free to assign mass zero wherever it likes, and no full-support condition has been imposed here. What is true is conditional: a probabilistic representation intended not to exclude any source-compatible outcome must have support containing AQ(t)\mathsf{A}_Q(t)AQ​(t). Under that stipulation , and it is a stipulation, not a theorem , the support still contains both aQ(s0)a_Q(s_0)aQ​(s0​) and aQ(s1)a_Q(s_1)aQ​(s1​) whenever aQ(s0)≠aQ(s1)a_Q(s_0)\neq a_Q(s_1)aQ​(s0​)=aQ​(s1​) and p(s0)=p(s1)p(s_0)=p(s_1)p(s0​)=p(s1​), so the rule has not told us which of s0,s1s_0,s_1s0​,s1​ obtained, and Corollary 3.2 still forbids a correct deterministic answer.

Second, and consequently, a probabilistic prediction answers a different target. Replacing QQQ by the target “what is the distribution of answers, given ttt and given a specified law on the fibers?” is a legitimate move, but it is a change of question, and it is one that requires additional input , namely the law on the fibers, which is a piece of source-level information not contained in ppp. It is not a solution to the original QQQ, and it should not be reported as one.
