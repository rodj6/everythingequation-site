Theorem 3.1 is the fiber–descent theorem of [8] specialized to the correct-answer map, and we have given the argument in full so that the present paper stands on its own. What is new here is not the criterion but the object it is applied to: by taking the descending map to be the answer to a nominated question rather than an arbitrary source relation, the criterion becomes a statement about the adequacy of the readout for that question, and hence acquires the target-relativity that the rest of the paper develops.

The structure of this argument deserves emphasis, because it is the pattern on which all the necessity results of this paper are built, and because it is easy to counterfeit. We did not assume that no readout-only construction exists and then declare completion necessary. We assumed only (6) , a fact about the pair (p,aQ)(p,a_Q)(p,aQ​), checkable in examples, and verified concretely in Section 10 , and then derived the failure of every candidate bbb by a counting argument: b(t)b(t)b(t) is one element of AQA_QAQ​, while correctness at s0s_0s0​ and s1s_1s1​ demands two different elements. Impossibility follows from variation of the correct-answer map along a fiber, not from a stipulation of impossibility.

Proposition 3.4 closes an escape route that is attempted surprisingly often. One cannot evade a fiber-variation obstruction by manufacturing additional quantities out of the readout , derived fields, filtered or smoothed versions of ttt, spectral transforms of ttt, statistics of ttt, or any other post-processing. Whatever is computed from ttt is a function of ttt; composing functions of ttt leaves one with a function of ttt; and a function of ttt is constant on FtF_tFt​ while aQa_QaQ​ is not. Genuine repair must inject information that is not a function of the readout. That is what Section 6 formalizes.
