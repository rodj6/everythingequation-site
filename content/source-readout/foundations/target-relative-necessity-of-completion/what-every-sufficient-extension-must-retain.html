It is useful to restate Theorem 7.1 in partition language, where its content is transparent. Let ∼e\sim_e∼e​, ∼p\sim_p∼p​, and ∼aQ\sim_{a_Q}∼aQ​​ denote the equivalence relations on SSS induced respectively by eee, by ppp, and by aQa_QaQ​ (so s∼aQs′s\sim_{a_Q}s's∼aQ​​s′ iff aQ(s)=aQ(s′)a_Q(s)=a_Q(s')aQ​(s)=aQ​(s′)). A readout extension is sufficient for QQQ precisely when its induced partition refines both the readout partition and the answer partition.

Indeed the converse of Theorem 7.1 holds as well and is worth recording, since together they characterize sufficiency exactly.

Proposition 7.3 (Separation characterizes sufficiency).

A readout extension e ⁣:S→Ee\colon S\to Ee:S→E is QQQ-sufficient if and only if (17) holds.

Proof. Necessity is Theorem 7.1. For sufficiency, assume (17). Its contrapositive reads e(s)=e(s′)  ⟹  aQ(s)=aQ(s′)e(s)=e(s')\implies a_Q(s)=a_Q(s')e(s)=e(s′)⟹aQ​(s)=aQ​(s′), i.e. aQa_QaQ​ is constant on every fiber of eee. By Lemma 6.5, eee is QQQ-sufficient. ◻

We emphasize that Theorem 7.1 does not say that eee must separate every pair of distinct source states, i.e. that eee must be injective, i.e. that the source must be reconstructed. It says only that eee must separate pairs whose answers differ. Pairs with equal answers may be freely collapsed. That is the loophole that Section 8 exploits to construct a completion that is often dramatically smaller than SSS, and it is why a target-relative theory of completion is not a disguised demand for full reconstruction.
