That the holonomy is physically consequential , that it is not a gauge artifact but an observable feature of the configuration , is the content of the Aharonov–Bohm effect [1, 11], and it is worth stating the geometry of that effect accurately.

In the standard physical realization, the apparatus consists of a solenoid or flux tube together with the region exterior to it. The charged particle is confined to the accessible region, which excludes the flux-carrying interior and is therefore multiply connected; on that accessible region, and only there, the connection is flat and the field strength vanishes. The excluded region does carry field: the flux Φ\PhiΦ threading the solenoid is nonzero, and it is exactly that flux which fixes the phase. It would be wrong to say that the electromagnetic field vanishes everywhere in the apparatus , it does not , and we do not say so. What is true, and what matters, is that the interference phase measured by the particle is determined by the gauge-invariant holonomy of the connection around a loop in the accessible region, a loop along which the particle never encounters any field.

The circle S1S^1S1 of this section models such an accessible loop. Its states eiθe^{i\theta}eiθ are distinguished by holonomy alone, with no local field anywhere along them, which is the physical warrant for treating Sflat≅U(1)S_{\mathrm{flat}}\cong\mathrm{U}(1)Sflat​≅U(1) as a space of genuinely distinct physical states rather than as gauge copies of a single trivial one.

Remark 9.1 (The curvature readout is a deliberate restriction, not a claim about physics).

We are not asserting that curvature exhausts what can be observed of a U(1)\mathrm{U}(1)U(1) gauge field. It plainly does not: in the Aharonov–Bohm interference experiment the measured interference phase is determined by the gauge-invariant holonomy, and an experiment sensitive to holonomy would supply a strictly finer readout than pFp_FpF​ , indeed the finest possible one on SflatS_{\mathrm{flat}}Sflat​, since hhh is injective there. The map pFp_FpF​ is the readout under examination. It is nominated, not discovered, and it is nominated precisely because its extreme degeneracy makes the target-relative machinery of Section 3, Section 4, Section 7, Section 8 maximally visible. A reader who objects that “real physics sees the holonomy” is agreeing with the analysis: enlarging the readout to include holonomy is exactly the move that Section 6 formalizes, and Section 11 shows that for the spectral target one need not even go that far.
