Readout extensions and target sufficiency

If the readout does not determine the answer, one may enlarge the readout. The following definition says what an enlargement is, and the crucial point is that it need not be an enlargement all the way back to the source.

Definition 6.1 (Readout extension).

A readout extension (or enrichment) of p ⁣:S→Tp\colon S\to Tp:S→T is a surjective map e ⁣:S⟶Etogether with a mapu ⁣:E⟶Tsuch thatp=u∘e.(15) e\colon S\longrightarrow E \qquad\text{together with a map}\qquad u\colon E\longrightarrow T \qquad\text{such that}\qquad p=u\circ e .\tag{15}e:S⟶Etogether with a mapu:E⟶Tsuch thatp=u∘e.(15)

Lemma 6.2 (3) is the content of the word “extension”: EEE is at least as informative as TTT, because anything determined by EEE determines the TTT-value. Equivalently, the equivalence relation ∼e\sim_e∼e​ induced by eee is contained in ∼p\sim_p∼p​. But EEE need not be SSS, and ∼e\sim_e∼e​ need not be equality: EEE sits somewhere between the readout and the source, an intermediate quotient of SSS, or, in partition language, a refinement of the readout partition that need not be the discrete partition. The two extreme cases are E=TE=TE=T (with e=pe=pe=p, u=id⁡u=\operatorname{id}u=id), which adds nothing, and E=SE=SE=S (with e=id⁡e=\operatorname{id}e=id, u=pu=pu=p), which retains everything. The interesting extensions are strictly between, and the point of the next two sections is that for a given target there is a canonical one, generally far closer to TTT than to SSS.

Definition 6.3 (Sufficiency for a target).

A readout extension e ⁣:S→Ee\colon S\to Ee:S→E is sufficient for QQQ, or QQQ-sufficient, if there exists a map b ⁣:E⟶AQsuch thataQ=b∘e.(16) b\colon E\longrightarrow A_Q \qquad\text{such that}\qquad a_Q=b\circ e .\tag{16}b:E⟶AQ​such thataQ​=b∘e.(16) We also call a QQQ-sufficient readout extension a completion of ppp for QQQ.

Remark 6.4 (What “completion” means here).

The word “completion” is used in Definition 6.3 in a sense that must be separated from several established and entirely different uses. It is not metric completion, not topological or uniform completion, not Cauchy completion, not the completion of a local ring, and not the adjunction of arbitrary hidden variables posited to restore determinism. A completion in the present sense is an enrichment of the readout that suffices for a nominated target: a surjection eee through which both ppp and aQa_QaQ​ factor. It is target-relative by construction; an extension may complete ppp for one target while remaining insufficient for another. And it is constrained: Theorem 7.1 shows that a completion cannot be arbitrary, and Theorem 8.3 shows that there is a least one.

A readout extension e ⁣:S→Ee\colon S\to Ee:S→E is QQQ-sufficient if and only if aQa_QaQ​ is constant on every fiber of eee; and then bbb in (16) is unique.

Proof. Apply Theorem 3.1 with ppp replaced by the surjection eee and TTT by EEE. ◻

Note the immediate sanity check: e=id⁡Se=\operatorname{id}_Se=idS​ (with E=SE=SE=S, u=pu=pu=p, b=aQb=a_Qb=aQ​) is always QQQ-sufficient, so completions exist trivially. The theorems that follow are therefore not existence theorems. They are theorems about how little a completion can get away with, and what it must contain no matter what.
