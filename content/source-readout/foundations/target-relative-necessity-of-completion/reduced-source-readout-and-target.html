Reduced source, readout, and target

The reduced source space

Throughout, SSS denotes a reduced source space. By this we mean the space obtained from whatever raw description one began with after quotienting by every redundancy that has been declared physically inert: local gauge transformations, coordinate reparametrizations, relabelings of identical constituents, and any further identification that the modeling commitments of the problem require. The point of insisting on this is not fastidiousness. If SSS still carried gauge copies, then ppp would appear to lose information whenever it merely forgot a gauge label, and every theorem below would be vacuous or misleading: one would be proving obstructions that could be dissolved by a change of gauge. By working on the reduced space we guarantee that two distinct points of SSS are genuinely distinct physical configurations, so that a fiber of ppp containing two points is a genuine collapse of physical distinctions and not a bookkeeping artifact.

It asserts that the question has a determinate answer for each reduced source state , that once the physical configuration is fixed, so is the correct answer. It does not assert that the answer is computable, that it is known, or that it is accessible from any particular data. Indeed, the entire content of the paper is the analysis of when the answer, though determinate as a function on SSS, fails to be a function of p(s)p(s)p(s). If a purported target does not have a determinate answer per reduced source state , if, say, the answer depends on further facts not encoded in SSS , then it is not a target on SSS in the sense of Definition 2.2, and the source space must be enlarged before the present analysis applies.

Remark 2.4 (Solving a target is weaker than reconstructing the source).

Definition 2.3 asks only that the correct answer be recoverable from t=p(s)t=p(s)t=p(s). It does not ask that sss itself be recoverable. This is the asymmetry that makes the whole subject nontrivial. A readout can be violently noninjective , collapsing an infinite-dimensional fiber to a point , and still solve a target exactly, provided the target is blind to everything the fiber varies over. Conversely, an almost-injective readout that fails to separate a single critical pair of states can fail to solve a target that hinges on that pair. Injectivity of ppp is therefore sufficient for exact solvability of every target, but it is wildly stronger than necessary for any given target, and the gap between the two is where the useful mathematics lives.
