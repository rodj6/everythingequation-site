Remark 12.10 (What EQτE_{Q_\tau}EQτ​​ is, and what it is not).

Accordingly, the joint target image (61) should be described in exactly one way: it is the universally coarsest completion of the readout for predicting the TTT-valued readout at the fixed time τ\tauτ. By Theorem 8.3 every QτQ_\tauQτ​-sufficient extension maps onto it, and by Proposition 8.2 it is itself sufficient for that prediction.

Its QτQ_\tauQτ​-sufficiency does not by itself make it an autonomous state space, supply an iterable dynamics, or furnish a semigroup completion. Nothing proved here supplies a map EQτ→EQτE_{Q_\tau}\to E_{Q_\tau}EQτ​​→EQτ​​ satisfying (63), and the results of this section do not assert one. By Proposition 12.8, autonomy would be a strictly stronger property than the QτQ_\tauQτ​-sufficiency that Theorem 8.3 establishes for EQτE_{Q_\tau}EQτ​​, and Example 12.9 shows that the two can come apart; the stronger property is not established here.

This leaves the question open rather than settled against it. In a particular system, EQτE_{Q_\tau}EQτ​​ may or may not happen to admit an autonomous VτV_\tauVτ​; what does not hold is that any such evolution follows from the universal construction of Theorem 8.3. Whether a given extension admits an autonomous VτV_\tauVτ​, whether such extensions can be chosen compatibly across a range of times τ\tauτ, and whether the resulting family composes as a semigroup are all questions beyond Theorem 12.3, Proposition 12.7, which are finite-time factorization statements at a single fixed τ\tauτ. Those questions belong to [10] and are not addressed here.

What the obstruction does and does not license

Corollary 12.4 rules out one thing precisely: a deterministic autonomous law on TTT alone. It is silent about what to do instead, and it is worth being explicit about the available modeling responses, none of which is an automatic consequence of the theorem.

  • State refinement. One may replace TTT by a QτQ_\tauQτ​-sufficient extension EEE, on which, by Proposition 12.5, a deterministic prediction bτ ⁣:E→Tb_\tau\colon E\to Tbτ​:E→T of the time-τ\tauτ readout does exist. Obtaining a deterministic evolution on EEE is a further demand, requiring (64), and is not delivered by QτQ_\tauQτ​-sufficiency. Whether an extension meeting either condition is available in practice, and whether its variables are measurable, are separate questions the theorem does not settle. What the theorem does settle is a lower bound on what EEE must distinguish.

  • Memory. One may allow the evolution law to depend on the past history of the readout rather than on its instantaneous value. This changes the shape of the modeling problem , the state is now a trajectory segment, not a point of TTT , and whether the history supplies the missing distinctions is not settled by Theorem 12.3, which concerns the instantaneous readout. Projection-operator methods in nonequilibrium statistical mechanics [12, 5] are the classical setting in which such history-dependent reduced equations arise; the construction of exact memory equations for the present framework belongs to [10] and is not developed here.

  • Probabilistic modeling. One may posit a probability law and predict a distribution over future readouts. See Remark 12.11.

  • Domain restriction. One may restrict SSS to a subset on which (59) does hold, obtaining an exactly closed law on the restricted problem. This is legitimate and often useful, but it is a change of the source domain, and the theorem then applies to the restricted problem rather than the original one.

Remark 12.11 (Stochasticity is not exact trajectory recovery).

It is sometimes suggested that the obstruction of Corollary 12.4 is dissolved by making the reduced law stochastic. It is not, and the reason is the one already given in Remark 5.6. Suppose p(s0)=p(s1)=tp(s_0)=p(s_1)=tp(s0​)=p(s1​)=t but the futures differ. A stochastic law on TTT assigns to ttt a distribution over TTT. It therefore does not tell us which of the two actually occurs, and if the system is in fact in state s0s_0s0​, the stochastic law has not predicted the realized trajectory; it has predicted a spread that contains it. This is a strictly weaker claim, and it answers a different target , “what is the distribution of the future readout, given the present readout and a specified law on the fibers?” , than the one we posed.

Absent such a law, the most that can be said from the readout alone is the set-valued statement of Theorem 5.4, namely that the future readout lies in AQτ(t)=p(Φτ(Ft))\mathsf{A}_{Q_\tau}(t)=p\bigl(\Phi_\tau(F_t)\bigr)AQτ​​(t)=p(Φτ​(Ft​)), and that this set is the least sound prediction available.
