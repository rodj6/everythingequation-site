Active and inactive information loss

We now sharpen the picture. Corollary 3.2 tells us that fiber variation of aQa_QaQ​ obstructs. But in practice one usually knows something more specific: one knows which source-level structure the readout has forgotten, and one wants to know whether that particular forgotten structure is what the target depends on. This is the distinction between information that is merely lost and information whose loss has consequences.

Definition 4.5 (Structural versus consequential loss).

Let r ⁣:S→Vr\colon S\to Vr:S→V be a source relation that does not factor through ppp; that is, rrr is not constant on some fiber. We call this structural information loss: the readout genuinely fails to determine rrr. If in addition rrr is active for a target QQQ on some fiber, we call the loss consequential for QQQ, or target-active.

Theorem 4.3, Theorem 4.4 should be read with their standing hypothesis kept in view. Both presuppose that the nominated variable rrr mediates the target, in the precise sense of (7): aQ(s)=ΦQ(p(s),r(s))a_Q(s)=\Phi_Q(p(s),r(s))aQ​(s)=ΦQ​(p(s),r(s)). Relative to such an rrr, the two theorems say that activity of rrr is exactly the condition under which the loss of rrr obstructs QQQ , active loss obstructs, and globally inactive loss does not. This is a statement about the chosen mediating variable, not about information loss in the abstract, and it is only as sharp as the choice of rrr.

The fully general criterion, carrying no such hypothesis, is the one already established in Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2: QQQ is obstructed from the readout precisely when aQa_QaQ​ is nonconstant on some fiber of ppp. Every statement about activity is a refinement of that criterion, obtained by localizing the fiber variation onto a particular mediating variable, and it adds diagnostic content , which forgotten structure is responsible , rather than logical strength.

With that scoping in place, the moral stands. Structural loss without activity is invisible to QQQ; it is what makes coarse descriptions viable rather than defective. It is therefore not a theorem of this framework , and we do not assert , that every noninjective readout requires completion, nor that every lost source distinction must be restored. Whether a given loss must be repaired is a question one can only answer after nominating a target, and the answer may legitimately be no. The examples of Section 9, Section 10, Section 11 include both verdicts for the same readout.

Remark 4.6.

It is worth recording the logical relation between activity of rrr and descent of rrr. If rrr itself descends along ppp (is fiber-constant), then r(Ft)r(F_t)r(Ft​) is a singleton for each ttt and rrr is automatically inactive for every target that factors through (p,r)(p,r)(p,r); the loss is not merely inconsequential but nonexistent. The converse fails: rrr can vary on fibers , structural loss , while being inactive for QQQ. So activity implies structural loss, but structural loss does not imply activity, and it is precisely this asymmetry that forbids the inference from “noninjective” to “must be completed.”
