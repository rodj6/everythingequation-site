Remark 24 (What (6.5) is, and what it is not).

Equation (6.5) is an identity, valid for every source solution, obtained by nothing more than variation of constants. It is the projection-operator identity of Mori and Zwanzig [20, 10, 21] in the special case of a bounded generator and an orthogonal projection. In that literature the second term is often called the “fluctuating force” or “noise”, and K\mathcal{K}K the “friction kernel”. We use neither word without qualification, and we record explicitly what has not been done here:

  1. ηz0\eta_{z_{0}}ηz0​​ is not stochastic. It is a deterministic, linear, continuous function of the single vector z0∈HQz_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{Q}z0​∈HQ​, given by the closed formula (6.6). It becomes a random process only if one additionally posits a probability measure on the unresolved initial data, an equilibrium ensemble, say, and that is an extra modelling hypothesis with its own physical content. We impose no such measure.

  2. K\mathcal{K}K is not a friction coefficient. It is the exact kernel (6.7). No fluctuation–dissipation relation has been derived, because no ensemble has been introduced with respect to which one could be stated.

  3. No Markov approximation has been made. The convolution in (6.8) is over the entire history [0,t][0,t][0,t]. Replacing K\mathcal{K}K by δ\deltaδ-correlated kernel, or taking a timescale-separation limit, would produce a different (approximate) equation; we take no such limit.

  4. No ensemble average or coarse-graining functional appears. PPP is an orthogonal projection on a Hilbert space, chosen by the modeller, and nothing else.

The physical content of (6.8) is therefore modest and exact: if the resolved sector does not close, then the resolved trajectory is determined by resolved initial data plus an inhomogeneity carrying unresolved initial data plus a convolution over its own past. These are three structurally distinct dependences, and (6.8) says they are the only ones.

Remark 26 (The quantifier is not decorative).

Theorem 6.4 is a statement about all source states. Three distinct weaker phenomena must not be mistaken for autonomous closure:

  1. Closure on a prepared state. If z0∈NQz_{0}\in N_{Q}z0​∈NQ​, see (7.2), then ηz0≡0\eta_{z_{0}}\equiv0ηz0​​≡0 and the initial-state term drops out for that state. The memory kernel K\mathcal{K}K is unaffected: by (6.7) it is determined by LLL and PPP alone and does not depend on z0z_{0}z0​ at all, so no preparation of the unresolved initial data can remove it. The resolved motion of such a state is therefore still history-dependent, and other initial states fail even the weaker condition. Vanishing of the initial-state term in one specially prepared state is not closure.

  2. Static quotient. That HP≅H/HQ\mathcal{H}_{P}\cong\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{H}_{Q}HP​≅H/HQ​ is a perfectly good Hilbert space says nothing whatever about PLQPLQPLQ. This is (D1)/(D4) again.

  3. Small singular values. That PLQPLQPLQ has small norm makes the coupling weak and may justify a perturbative approximation, with a controllable error. It does not make the projected equation autonomous, and no truncation of (6.8) is exact unless PLQ=0PLQ=0PLQ=0 identically. This is (D3)/(D4).

Finally, observe that Theorem 6.4(3) is precisely the invariance condition (5.8) of Proposition 5.5 for the observation operator K=PK=PK=P, whose kernel is HQ\mathcal{H}_{Q}HQ​. Theorems 5.2 and 6.4 therefore agree, as they must: the semigroup descends exactly when the projected equation closes.
