Elimination of a source sector: Schur-complement effective operators

We now turn from state-space projection to field-theoretic elimination: the source variables split into a visible field φ\varphiφ and a completion field χ\chiχ, and χ\chiχ is removed by solving its own stationarity condition and substituting back. In the quadratic case this can be done exactly, and the result is a Schur complement.

Remark 41 (Nonlocality of the effective operator).

The correction to the visible operator is Δ:=− BC−1B∗,(9.5) \Delta:=-\,BC^{-1}B^{*} ,\tag{9.5}Δ:=−BC−1B∗,(9.5) and it is derived, not postulated. What the theorem establishes is exactly the form of Δ\DeltaΔ; whether Δ\DeltaΔ is nonlocal is a further question about the composed operator, and must not be read off from C−1C^{-1}C−1 alone. In field theory, when Hχ\mathcal{H}_{\chi}Hχ​ is a space of functions on a spacetime region and CCC is (the bounded realization of) a differential operator, C−1C^{-1}C−1 is an integral operator with a Green kernel G(y,y′)G(y,y')G(y,y′), and (Δφ)(x)=−∫ ⁣ ⁣∫ ⁣ ⁣∫B(x,y) G(y,y′) B∗(y′,x′) φ(x′) dy dy′ dx′.(\Delta\varphi)(x)=-\int\!\!\int\!\!\int B(x,y)\,G(y,y')\,B^{*}(y',x')\,\varphi(x')\,dy\,dy'\,dx' .(Δφ)(x)=−∫∫∫B(x,y)G(y,y′)B∗(y′,x′)φ(x′)dydy′dx′. This composition can be nonlocal: the effective equation for φ\varphiφ at a point then involves φ\varphiφ elsewhere, weighted by the Green function of the eliminated sector, even though the coupled source equations for (φ,χ)(\varphi,\chi)(φ,χ) were strictly local.

The qualification is not pedantic, because nonlocality of C−1C^{-1}C−1 is not sufficient. The correction depends on the coupling as well: if B=0B=0B=0 there is no correction at all, and more generally BBB may fail to couple to the nonlocal directions of GGG, or the two compositions BBB and B∗B^{*}B∗ may conspire so that BC−1B∗BC^{-1}B^{*}BC−1B∗ collapses to a local (e.g. multiplication or differential) operator. A concrete instance: if BBB is a rank-one coupling B=∣f⟩⟨h∣B=\lvert f\rangle\langle h\rvertB=∣f⟩⟨h∣, then BC−1B∗=⟨h,C−1h⟩ ∣f⟩⟨f∣BC^{-1}B^{*}=\langle h , C^{-1}h \rangle\,\lvert f\rangle\langle f\rvertBC−1B∗=⟨h,C−1h⟩∣f⟩⟨f∣, whose kernel is the product f(x′)‾f(x)\overline{f(x')}f(x)f(x′)​f(x) times a single scalar, the whole Green function GGG has entered only through the number ⟨h,C−1h⟩\langle h , C^{-1}h \rangle⟨h,C−1h⟩. We stress what this example does and does not show. The rank-one operator ∣f⟩⟨f∣\lvert f\rangle\langle f\rvert∣f⟩⟨f∣ is itself generally nonlocal (its kernel f(x′)‾f(x)\overline{f(x')}f(x)f(x′)​f(x) does not concentrate on the diagonal), so the example does not exhibit a local effective operator; what it exhibits is that the detailed structure of GGG, the very thing one might expect to be imprinted on the correction, can collapse to a single number, leaving a correction whose nonlocal structure is dictated entirely by BBB and not by the eliminated sector’s propagator. Nonlocality of the effective operator is therefore a property of BC−1B∗BC^{-1}B^{*}BC−1B∗, requiring that BBB couple to the relevant Green-operator directions without cancellation; it is an additional verification, not a corollary of Theorem 9.2.

Subject to that verification, elimination of a sector converts locality-with-extra-fields into nonlocality-with-fewer-fields, and there is an exact structural analogy with the memory kernel of Theorem 6.2: there, eliminating HQ\mathcal{H}_{Q}HQ​ converted a first-order ODE into an equation nonlocal in time, and there too the kernel K=PLQ eτQLQ QLP\mathcal{K}=PLQ\,e^{\tau QLQ}\,QLPK=PLQeτQLQQLP can degenerate, it vanishes identically when PLQ=0PLQ=0PLQ=0 or QLP=0QLP=0QLP=0, so that nonlocality in time likewise depends on the couplings and not on the eliminated sector’s propagator alone. In both settings nonlocality is a possible price of the reduction, not an automatic one, and never a feature of the source.

Remark 42 (Unbounded operators, and what would have to be supplied).

Theorem 9.2 is stated for bounded AAA, BBB, CCC with CCC boundedly invertible, and the proof uses boundedness at every step (in the differentiation, in the existence of C−1C^{-1}C−1 as a bounded operator, in the adjoint manipulations). It does not automatically cover the case where AAA and CCC are unbounded differential operators, which is the case of primary interest in field theory. To extend it one must supply, and verify: a common dense domain, or a formulation in terms of closed, densely defined, semibounded quadratic forms with form domains [7, Ch. VI]; boundary conditions making CCC self-adjoint and rendering the integration by parts in the variation legitimate; invertibility of CCC (equivalently, that 0∉σ(C)0\notin\sigma(C)0∈/σ(C), which fails whenever the eliminated sector has zero modes, e.g. a massless field on a compact region with the wrong boundary conditions); and the boundedness of C−1C^{-1}C−1 from the form dual back to the form domain, so that BC−1B∗BC^{-1}B^{*}BC−1B∗ is defined on the form domain of AAA. None of this follows from the bounded proof, and we do not assert it here. Where the physics literature performs such eliminations with unbounded operators, the Green operator C−1=GC^{-1}=GC−1=G is being used, and the validity of the manipulation is exactly the validity of the associated elliptic/hyperbolic solvability theory, which must be supplied case by case.

Remark 43 (Tree level, not the quantum effective action).

Theorem 9.2 is a statement of classical stationary elimination: χ\chiχ is replaced by the solution of its classical field equation (9.2). Equivalently, in a functional-integral language, (9.4) is the tree-level (saddle-point) contribution. It is not the quantum effective action for φ\varphiφ. Integrating χ\chiχ out of a Gaussian functional integral with weight eiS[φ,χ]e^{iS[\varphi,\chi]}eiS[φ,χ] produces, in addition to (9.4), a φ\varphiφ-independent determinant factor (det⁡C)−1/2(\det C)^{-1/2}(detC)−1/2; if CCC depends on φ\varphiφ (a nonquadratic coupling, absent from Assumption 9.1) the determinant becomes φ\varphiφ-dependent and contributes a genuine one-loop term i2Tr⁡log⁡C[φ]\tfrac{i}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\log C[\varphi]2i​TrlogC[φ]; and beyond one loop there are further corrections [12, 17]. Nothing in Theorem 9.2 computes or bounds these. The theorem is exactly what it says: substitution of a stationary point into a quadratic functional.
