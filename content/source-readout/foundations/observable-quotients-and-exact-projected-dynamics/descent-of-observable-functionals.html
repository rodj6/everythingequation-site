It is essential to notice what Definition 3.1 does and does not require. It requires a factorization, A=Aˉ∘KA=\bar A\circ KA=Aˉ∘K, and nothing more: Aˉ\bar AAˉ is a map on the set Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK, and no continuity of Aˉ\bar AAˉ with respect to any particular norm on Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK has been demanded. But continuity in the inherited observable norm, the norm an experimenter actually has access to on Hobs\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{obs}}Hobs​, is a strictly stronger demand, and it is governed by a different, sharper condition.

Remark 10 (Algebraic descent versus stable readout: exact preimage and closure).

Equation (3.4) contains a closure and one must not delete it, and Theorem 3.4 says exactly what the closure costs. Combining the two:

  • a∈Ran⁡K∗‾=(ker⁡K)⊥a\in\overline{\operatorname{Ran}K^{*}}=(\ker K)^{\perp}a∈RanK∗=(kerK)⊥   ⟺  \iff⟺ AAA descends algebraically through KKK (Corollary 3.3), with Aˉ\bar AAˉ continuous in the transported quotient norm;

  • a∈Ran⁡K∗a\in\operatorname{Ran}K^{*}a∈RanK∗   ⟺  \iff⟺ AAA descends to a functional that is bounded in the inherited observable norm (Theorem 3.4).

The two conditions coincide precisely when Ran⁡K∗\operatorname{Ran}K^{*}RanK∗ is closed, which by the closed range theorem is equivalent to Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK being closed [1, Thm. 2.19], the same dichotomy as in Theorem 2.3. So for a compact KKK with infinite-dimensional range, for instance Example 4.5, Ran⁡K∗\operatorname{Ran}K^{*}RanK∗ is dense in (ker⁡K)⊥(\ker K)^{\perp}(kerK)⊥ but proper, and there exist source functionals which descend perfectly well as maps of the observable state, yet possess no exact representing probe ω\omegaω with a=K∗ωa=K^{*}\omegaa=K∗ω. Such a functional is a limit of representable ones, but the approximating probes ωk\omega_{k}ωk​ have ∥ωk∥→∞\lVert\omega_{k}\rVert\to\infty∥ωk​∥→∞: reading the quantity off the observable state amplifies observational error without bound. This is (D2) reappearing on the dual side. Accordingly we say that the elements of (ker⁡K)⊥(\ker K)^{\perp}(kerK)⊥ are the observable linear source directions, and reserve stably observable for those in Ran⁡K∗\operatorname{Ran}K^{*}RanK∗; the phrase should never be read as asserting inherited-readout stability where only algebraic descent has been established.
