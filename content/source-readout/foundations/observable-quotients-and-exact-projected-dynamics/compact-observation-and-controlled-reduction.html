Example 4.5 decouples the three static phenomena completely. KKK is compact, so it admits arbitrarily good, quantitatively controlled finite-rank approximations (D3), and, more sharply, Theorem 4.4 gives the exact approximation error. KKK is injective, so (D1) fails: nothing is exactly identified, and the quotient HK\mathcal{H}_{K}HK​ is Hsrc\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{src}}Hsrc​ itself. Yet K−1K^{-1}K−1 is unbounded, so (D2) holds in full force: the source is determined by the readout, but not continuously, and no stability estimate as in Theorem 2.3(3) is available. Compactness does not imply a nontrivial kernel, what it does imply is the controlled finite-rank approximation of Theorem 4.4, and a trivial kernel does not imply stable inversion. Note also that for this KKK, (D4) cannot occur under any source semigroup whatever: ker⁡K={0}\ker K=\{0\}kerK={0} is invariant trivially, so by (1.1) the dynamics always descend, however violently the inversion is ill-conditioned. Instability of the inverse and failure of dynamical descent are different diseases. Any argument that slides between “the observation is compact”, “information is lost”, and “the source is nonidentifiable” has already conflated three different theorems.
