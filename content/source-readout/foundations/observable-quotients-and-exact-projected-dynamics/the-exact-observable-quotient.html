Remark 4 (The two norms on Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK).

Theorem 2.3 always allows us to transport the quotient norm to Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK by declaring ∥KΨ∥♯:=∥[Ψ]∥K\lVert K\Psi\rVert_{\sharp}:=\lVert[\Psi]\rVert_{K}∥KΨ∥♯​:=∥[Ψ]∥K​; with this transported norm K~\widetilde{K}K is by construction an isometric isomorphism, and (Ran⁡K,∥⋅∥♯)(\operatorname{Ran}K,\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sharp})(RanK,∥⋅∥♯​) is a Hilbert space. What is not generally true is that ∥⋅∥♯\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sharp}∥⋅∥♯​ coincides with, or is even equivalent to, the norm inherited from Hobs\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{obs}}Hobs​. The inequality ∥⋅∥Hobs≤∥K∥op∥⋅∥♯\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{obs}}}\le\lVert K\rVert_{\mathrm{op}} \lVert\cdot\rVert_{\sharp}∥⋅∥Hobs​​≤∥K∥op​∥⋅∥♯​ always holds on Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK; the reverse inequality holds, with some constant, exactly in the closed-range case. We therefore never write HK≅Ran⁡K\mathcal{H}_{K}\cong\operatorname{Ran}KHK​≅RanK without saying which norm Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK carries. Every later statement about topologies on the observable side is subject to this distinction.

Corollary 2.5 (Exact loss criterion).

The observation map KKK loses exact source distinctions, i.e. there exist Ψ≠Ψ′\Psi\neq\Psi'Ψ=Ψ′ with Ψ∼KΨ′\Psi\sim_{K}\Psi'Ψ∼K​Ψ′, if and only if ker⁡K≠{0}\ker K\neq\{0\}kerK={0}. Proof. Immediate from (2.1): Ψ∼KΨ′\Psi\sim_K\Psi'Ψ∼K​Ψ′ with Ψ≠Ψ′\Psi\neq\Psi'Ψ=Ψ′ iff Ψ−Ψ′∈ker⁡K∖{0}\Psi-\Psi'\in\ker K\setminus\{0\}Ψ−Ψ′∈kerK∖{0}. The second sentence is the content of Theorem 2.3: injectivity is the statement ker⁡K={0}\ker K=\{0\}kerK={0} and is logically independent of closedness of Ran⁡K\operatorname{Ran}KRanK, which is what governs boundedness of the inverse. ◻

Corollary 2.5 is the formal separation of (D1) from (D2). Nonidentifiability is a statement about the kernel; instability is a statement about the range. An injective compact operator exhibits the second without the first, as we show in Example 4.5.
