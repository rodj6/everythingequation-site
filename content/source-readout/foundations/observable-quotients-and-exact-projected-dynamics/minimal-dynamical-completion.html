Minimal dynamical completion

Theorem 6.4 says that when PLQ≠0PLQ\neq0PLQ=0 the resolved state x(0)x(0)x(0) is not enough to determine the resolved future. The natural question is then: how much unresolved information must be adjoined? The answer is not “all of HQ\mathcal{H}_{Q}HQ​”. Distinct unresolved initial states may produce identical resolved responses, and directions that never affect the resolved trajectory need not be retained. We now identify exactly the right quotient.

Remark 32 (Minimality is relative to the target).

EdynE_{\mathrm{dyn}}Edyn​ may be strictly smaller than HQ\mathcal{H}_{Q}HQ​, as Case B of Example 6.6 shows. The directions in NQN_{Q}NQ​ are not “physically absent” in any absolute sense: they may evolve nontrivially under etQLQe^{tQLQ}etQLQ, they may carry energy, they may be visible to a different readout. They are invisible to the resolved response function, and that is the target relative to which minimality is asserted. Change the target, ask instead for the resolved response and some other functional of the unresolved sector, and the minimal completion changes accordingly. This target-relativity is exactly the phenomenon isolated in [23]; it is not a defect but the content of the theorem.

Remark 35 (Relation to Kalman observability).

Equation (7.10) is the observability map of linear control theory [6, 15] for the finite-dimensional linear system with state z∈HQz\in\mathcal{H}_{Q}z∈HQ​, state matrix QLQQLQQLQ, and output matrix PLQPLQPLQ: the pair (PLQ, QLQ)(PLQ,\,QLQ)(PLQ,QLQ) is observable exactly when rankOm=m\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\mathcal{O}_{m}=mrankOm​=m, i.e. exactly when NQ={0}N_{Q}=\{0\}NQ​={0}, i.e. exactly when every unresolved direction leaves a trace in the resolved response and the whole of HQ\mathcal{H}_{Q}HQ​ must be retained. The dictionary must be read with care, however. What (7.10) tests is observability of the unresolved sector through the resolved-response output. It is not a claim that the full source system (H,L)(\mathcal{H},L)(H,L) is observable from the resolved readout, which would additionally require that the resolved sector’s own dynamics and the coupling QLPQLPQLP be taken into account. We assert only what (7.9) states: NQN_{Q}NQ​, and hence dim⁡Edyn\dim E_{\mathrm{dyn}}dimEdyn​, is computable by a finite rank condition. The general Hilbert space case has no such finite reduction; there NQN_{Q}NQ​ is defined by (7.2) and closed by Lemma 7.3, and no analogue of Cayley–Hamilton is available.
