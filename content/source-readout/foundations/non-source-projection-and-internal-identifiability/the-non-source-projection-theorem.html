Remark 23 (Direction of the universal map, and what it means).

The map runs from an arbitrary sufficient extension onto EQE_QEQ​, not the reverse. The reading is: EQE_QEQ​ is the coarsest sufficient extension , it retains exactly the readout plus the answer to QQQ and nothing else , and any other sufficient extension, being at least as fine, projects onto it. In categorical language EQE_QEQ​ is terminal in the category of exact extensions sufficient for QQQ (with morphisms the maps commuting with the eee’s), and terminality gives both the uniqueness of ϕ\phiϕ and, in the usual way, uniqueness of EQE_QEQ​ up to canonical isomorphism. It is exactly the wrong intuition to expect a map EQ→EE_Q\to EEQ​→E: a completion that remembers more than QQQ requires cannot be manufactured out of QQQ’s answer.

Remark 28.

Corollary 4.11 obstructs equivariant selectors only. A non-equivariant selector may well exist , indeed, for ppp surjective, the axiom of choice always supplies some set-theoretic section σ\sigmaσ with pσ=idp\sigma=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}pσ=id. The content is that any such section must break the symmetry GGG: reconstruction is possible only at the cost of a choice that the readout’s own symmetry group does not sanction. This is the precise sense in which the obstruction is a symmetry obstruction and not a cardinality one.

The scope of Theorem 4.5

Theorem 4.5 is conditional on four supplied objects: the source domain SSS, the physical equivalence ≃phys\simeq_{\mathrm{phys}}≃phys​ used to build it, the readout ppp, and the essential witness (13). It derives none of them. Its strength is precisely delimited: once those four are established for a model, the non-source conclusion follows with no further interpretive step , one does not additionally need to argue that the projection “loses something real”, because the witness (13) says so in the model’s own terms. Its weakness is equally precise: it says nothing whatever about whether our universe supplies them. We return to this in §10, and we do not claim more anywhere.
