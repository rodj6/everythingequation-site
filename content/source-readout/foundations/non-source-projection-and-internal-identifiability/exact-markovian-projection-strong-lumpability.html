Exact Markovian projection: strong lumpability

The dynamical descent problem of §5 has a sharp stochastic counterpart, in which the question “does the projected process close?” admits a complete and checkable answer.

Definition 9.2 (Strong lumpability).

The chain is strongly lumpable with respect to ppp if, for every initial source distribution π(0)\pi(0)π(0), the projected process (p(Xt))t≥0\bigl(p(X_t)\bigr)_{t\ge0}(p(Xt​))t≥0​ is a time-homogeneous Markov chain on TTT whose transition law depends on the initial data only through the initial block distribution πˉ(0)\bar\pi(0)πˉ(0).

The quantifier “for every initial distribution” is the whole point, and it is what distinguishes strong lumpability from the weaker, initial-distribution-dependent notion. A projected process can perfectly well be Markov for one carefully chosen π(0)\pi(0)π(0) , e.g. one supported on a single state, or a stationary within-block law , while failing to be Markov for another. Such a coincidence gives an observer a Markov description that is valid for their particular preparation and invalid in general. Strong lumpability demands that the projected description be universally valid, and that is the property that corresponds to genuine closure of the readout dynamics in the sense of §5.

Corollary 9.4 (Failure of lumpability).

Remark 56 (What failure of lumpability does and does not establish).

Corollary 9.4 is a negative result: the instantaneous projected state does not support a universal Markov law. It does not by itself establish that the projected process is governed by any particular memory kernel or non-Markovian equation. Producing such a kernel requires an additional reduction construction , a Nakajima–Zwanzig projection with a chosen within-block reference law, or a completion in the sense of Definition 4.3 , and different choices give different kernels.

It is worth being exact about what determines the within-block conditional law ν\nuν, since this is the discarded information. The generator GGG together with an initial distribution π(0)\pi(0)π(0) determines it: the pair (G,π(0))(G,\pi(0))(G,π(0)) fixes the full law of the source process, hence the conditional law of XtX_tXt​ given any positive-probability projected history. The generator alone does not: without an initial law , equivalently, without a specification of how the system was prepared , there is no such thing as “the” within-block distribution, and different preparations consistent with the same projected state yield different ν\nuν, which is exactly why Corollary 9.4 bites. Compare §5.2(2): in the deterministic setting likewise, (S,p,Φt)(S,p,\Phi_t)(S,p,Φt​) does not determine the within-fiber distribution, which must be supplied. What is theorem here is the impossibility; what is modelling is the replacement.
