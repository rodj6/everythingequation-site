The source-to-readout architecture

Reduction and readout

The order of operations in (1) is the crux of the entire paper and we pause on it. If one did not quotient first, then a noninjective readout would be uninformative: its fibers could consist entirely of gauge copies of a single physical state, and their nontriviality would be an artifact of description. By constructing SSS first, we ensure that s≠s′ in S⟹s,s′ are not related by any redundancy the model declares.(2) s\neq s'\ \text{in}\ S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s,s'\ \text{are \emph{not} related by any redundancy the model declares.}\tag{2}s=s′ in S⟹s,s′ are not related by any redundancy the model declares.(2) Consequently a nontrivial ppp-fiber in SSS is a genuine multiplicity of physically inequivalent states by the model’s own standard. This does not make it physically real , the model may be wrong , but it removes the cheapest objection.

We record the terminology that the rest of the paper turns on.

Definition 2.4 (Exactness).

We call ppp an exact readout to mean precisely (4): T1T_1T1​ is, up to canonical bijection, the quotient of SSS by the relation “has the same readout”. That is, “exact” means exact as the quotient by the readout’s own kernel relation ∼p\sim_p∼p​, and nothing more: it does not mean that ppp is injective, that the readout is source-complete, or that information is preserved at the source level. Exactness is a statement about no information being lost within a fiber’s label; it is emphatically not a statement that fibers are singletons. The whole content of this paper lies in that gap.

Specializations

The abstract triple (S,p,R)(S,p,\mathcal R)(S,p,R) has three standard realizations, which we record to fix the reader’s picture. They are specializations of one architecture, not competing definitions.

Example 2.8 (Geometric orbit space and geometric realization).

Let Cgeo\mathcal C_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​ be a space of field configurations and ≃geo\simeq_{\mathrm{geo}}≃geo​ the action of the gauge and diffeomorphism group. Then Sgeo=Cgeo/ ⁣≃geoS_{\mathrm{geo}}=\mathcal C_{\mathrm{geo}}/\!\simeq_{\mathrm{geo}}Sgeo​=Cgeo​/≃geo​ is the orbit space, and the readout is a map pgeo ⁣:Sgeo→T1p_{\mathrm{geo}}\colon S_{\mathrm{geo}}\to T_1pgeo​:Sgeo​→T1​ recording whatever subset of invariants it resolves. The relations are now geometric: write JA=(Jα)α∈A,Jα ⁣:Sgeo→Vα,J_A=(J_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A},\qquad J_\alpha\colon S_{\mathrm{geo}}\to V_\alpha ,JA​=(Jα​)α∈A​,Jα​:Sgeo​→Vα​, for a family of gauge- and diffeomorphism-invariant functionals: integrated, spectral, or relational curvature invariants; holonomy conjugacy classes; and Wilson-loop observables based on invariantly specified loops. Specializing Definition 2.6 gives the geometric realization of the completion, EAgeo:=Im(pgeo,JA)={(pgeo(s), JA(s))  :  s∈Sgeo},(7) E_A^{\mathrm{geo}}:=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\bigl(p_{\mathrm{geo}},J_A\bigr) =\bigl\{\bigl(p_{\mathrm{geo}}(s),\,J_A(s)\bigr)\;:\;s\in S_{\mathrm{geo}}\bigr\},\tag{7}EAgeo​:=Im(pgeo​,JA​)={(pgeo​(s),JA​(s)):s∈Sgeo​},(7) the set of completed signatures actually attained by configurations.

The word “realization” carries the entire content of (7), and it is a restriction, not a licence. EAgeoE_A^{\mathrm{geo}}EAgeo​ is by construction an image: it contains only those pairs (readout value, invariant values) that arise from an admissible, globally compatible field configuration in Cgeo\mathcal C_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​. It is emphatically not the full product T1×∏αVαT_1\times\prod_\alpha V_\alphaT1​×∏α​Vα​. An abstract signature , an arbitrary assignment of values to the invariants, consistent with no constraint one has bothered to impose , need not be realized by any global field at all: field equations, Bianchi identities, integrality and holonomy constraints, and global topological obstructions all cut the attainable set down. Determining which completed signatures are realized by compatible global geometric fields is thus a substantive geometric question about Cgeo\mathcal C_{\mathrm{geo}}Cgeo​, to be settled by geometry, and none of the theorems below settles it or presumes an answer.

We make no smoothness, Hausdorffness, or slice claims about SgeoS_{\mathrm{geo}}Sgeo​: nothing below requires them, and orbit spaces are frequently badly behaved.
