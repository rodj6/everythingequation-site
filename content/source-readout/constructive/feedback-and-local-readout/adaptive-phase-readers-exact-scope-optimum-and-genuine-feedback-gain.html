Stopping early and retaining an irrevocable decision can be represented by ignoring subsequent records, but no saved-copy or random-stopping resource optimum is asserted. For equal axis efficiencies and visibilities, the experiment is invariant under the four-element group generated by exchanging X/Y and reversing all signs. Sign reversal also reverses the target and final decision. This group acts transitively on the four source labels. Consequently the uniform source prior is least favourable for the complete randomized policy class: every minimax risk is at least its optimal uniform-prior Bayes risk, while averaging a Bayes optimal policy over the group makes each of its four errors equal to that Bayes risk. The group transformation is selected before the experiment and translated into actual settings, record signs and terminal decisions; it is not knowledge of the hidden source. Two independent fair root-control bits suffice for this group choice; a terminal posterior tie may additionally consume a fresh fair decision bit. This standard symmetrization argument applies both to adaptive policies and to the smaller randomized-nonadaptive class.
