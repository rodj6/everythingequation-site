Exact rational weighted contraction and target-dependent precision

The supplied OpenAI preprint’s Appendix D.1[7] contracts Boolean-wire networks over a truncated polynomial ring modulo a power of two. Rational signed weights are not an automatic instance of that coefficient ring. We give an explicit transfer, including signed recovery and all precision costs, and a sharp improvement when the target supplies an independently justified interval. The resulting source-likelihood interface is useful without implementing the paper’s asymptotic contraction algorithm. Weighted sum–product and variable elimination are established methods. Scott–Sorkin[13], Sections 1–2 and 6, treat polynomial and ring-valued constraint satisfaction; Kschischang–Frey–Loeliger [4], Sections II–III, explain factorized marginalization and its relation to forward/backward algorithms. We do not claim weighted contraction, denominator clearing, modular reconstruction or dynamic programming as newly discovered techniques. The contribution here is their precise transfer to the supplied ETH theorem and a checked, typed interface for the nominated source–readout tasks.

Verification, backends and an easy-instance ceiling

The reusable weighted.py exposes exact rational factor evaluation, ordinary variable elimination, integer denominator clearing, modular enumeration, interval decoding, posterior queries and a Boolean-wire network converter. It uses Python’s exact integers and Fraction. Its backends are not an implementation of the ETH paper’s recursive contraction theorem.
