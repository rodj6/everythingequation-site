An information lower boundary for terminal-risk certification

A direct decision-risk gate has a weaker output contract than identifying an entire continuous detector response. We can nevertheless bound its best possible resource cost in a precise black-box interface, and compare an attainable sequential gate with that boundary. This distinguishes genuine task-relative economy from a claim of universal physical-calibration savings.

The declared validation experiment

A frozen reader uses fresh source copies and returns a sign at its fixed deadline. This is a lower bound for an unrestricted terminal-Bernoulli validation model. Its one-bad-label alternatives need not all be realizable within a specified low-dimensional Pauli detector family. Therefore it does not lower-bound every structured detector-calibration method, a protocol using full quantum records, or a method with extra known preparation information. It also does not assert that all physical apparatus resources equal sample count. Those boundaries are necessary to interpret the comparison fairly.
