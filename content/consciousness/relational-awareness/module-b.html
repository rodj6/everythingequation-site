Occurrence, inclusion and ownership

Relational appearance requires an actual presentation of counterparts together with their relation. This chapter makes the distinctions exact: a shared description, a shared source and a shared occurrence are different achievements. Its mathematical results expose the extra commitments that turn evidence about descriptions into claims about jointness and ownership.

Compatible descriptions of two qualities do not establish relational appearance. The counterparts and their relation must be given in an actual presentation. This module distinguishes four questions: whether descriptions descend faithfully from occurrence tokens, whether several tokens have a common presentation, whether ownership is exclusive, and whether a physical relation supplies an occurrence law. These distinctions refine the source–aperture–readout framework of Rodgers (2026e) without assuming its constitutive assignment. A new presentation can meet the relational target; the original tokens need not fuse or continue.

Throughout, PPP denotes a physical performance, AAA an experience-inclusive act, and QQQ its actual qualitative mode. A finite token set EEE, a partition Π\PiΠ, and a downward-closed family KKK are mathematical objects. Their interpretation as experiential occurrences or domains requires independent grounds. In particular, a physical port, an equal content label, and a numerically identical qualitative occurrence are different things.

2.7 Native articulation, manifestation and categorical grounding

The earlier proposals motivate distinct constitutive laws. The following arguments also identify the premises each law still requires.

Native Articulation Law

The Native Articulation Law (NAL) assigns an elementary presentation to each actual irreducible native transformation. Its motivation is immanence and structural fidelity: do not add a phenomenal fusion across independently operating transformations or a phenomenal division inside a native prime without further lawlike structure. It addresses organization rather than an ingredient list and needs no additional spectator. Its strongest objection is carrier-centered: an irreducible physical event can still have several carriers with separate qualitative states. Rejecting their experiential division already adopts the event-centered individuation criterion. Native elementary-event grain and qualitative duration also need independent specification. A content map could be constant, so one presentation per prime would not itself supply two qualitative counterparts and their relation.

Relational Manifestation

Relational Manifestation (RM) proposes that a concretely realized intrinsic relation has a manifest counterpart preserving its distinction, with some common presentation supported by its contributors. Ontological uniformity motivates the proposal: if intrinsic qualitative terms are real, wholly unmanifest connections between them should not simply be assumed. A serious rival nevertheless retains qualitative terms and real intrinsic relations without their being given together; it need not introduce another substance. Uniformity therefore supplies a reason to investigate the law, not a proof of its co-presentation clause.

This proposal also changes the object represented by the family. If R\mathcal RR lists required realizing contributor supports, the least downward-closed family containing them is

Fmin⁡={∅}∪{{e}:e∈E}∪⋃R∈R2R. \mathcal F_{\min}=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{\{e\}:e\in E\}\cup\bigcup_{R\in\mathcal R}2^R. Fmin​={∅}∪{{e}:e∈E}∪R∈R⋃​2R.

It records common realizing support. It is not the earlier family KKK of qualitative occurrences jointly given. A subset of a realizing support need not be sufficient for the target. A compressed parity can preserve a distinction without presenting both full argument qualities. Identifying F\mathcal FF with KKK would need content fidelity and actual occurrence, neither supplied by set closure.

Intrinsic Qualitative Grounding

Intrinsic Qualitative Grounding (IQG) adds: (i) actual concrete categorical character is qualitative; (ii) irreducible relational facts have categorical grounds; (iii) the nominated relation’s ground is a presentation in which that relation is manifest. The positive rationale integrates intrinsic actuality and accepted relational phenomenology without an added spectator. Clause (iii) is not a consequence of the first two: a relational ground can contain qualitative terms without one common presentation. The objection concerns assembly/ownership, not whether the relation exists. The broader categorical and whole-act alternatives are developed in Module H. None of these principles is assumed to establish the main paper’s native-human induction.

2.13 Awareness, access and the construction of a personal event

The awareness-first account distinguishes the proposed knowing aspect of reality, denoted UUU in the monograph, from the differentiated contents available through a particular apparatus and history. This ground is not an additional localized subject. The monograph’s starting commitment and the development paper’s operational distinctions have different roles (Rodgers, 2026e,c). The former proposes an ontological basis; the latter distinguishes a source, the access available to an observer, and the representation constructed through that access. An operational model can characterize dependence on apparatus or acquired constraints without deriving the fundamental commitment. Conversely, granting that commitment does not determine the extension of a particular experiential predicate.

This distinction matters even if one accepts that all differentiated terms belong to one underlying reality. Two differentiated processes may have separate perspectives; they may contribute to a new shared presentation; or their interaction may introduce no additional joint perspective. A premise about the underlying basis alone does not decide among those local possibilities. The present paper’s current-act argument supplies a scoped positive attribution rather than treating fundamental awareness as a substitute for its activity and bearer premises.

Perceptual presentation is formed through bodily and sensory processing and acquired organisation, including classifications, memories, expectations, feelings and beliefs. These conditions shape the sensory presentation itself; they are not merely descriptions added after a consciously viewed raw image. Ongoing seeing is perceptual processing, which can continue when reflective naming, commentary or personal appropriation is suspended. Perceptual construction, recognition and the sense of an event happening to me are different functional roles; they can operate concurrently within one activity. A practical history can therefore alter the organisation of current access itself. In the development account, a relational product can be retained and become available to subsequent interactions; it need not be a third substance (Rodgers, 2026c, §3.1). Retained constraints then shape activity, and activity can revise them. The reflective-alignment paper develops that reciprocity (Rodgers, 2026b, §4.1); its operational description does not by itself establish one phenomenal subject.

The personal observer is also constructed within this process. A central self-image organizes ownership, relevance and narrative continuity. The proposed reflective recognition of its constructed character need not erase memory, practical identity or the usable self-model. It changes the model’s claim to be the exhaustive centre (Rodgers, 2026c, §5.4). Whether an artificial or biological implementation has such recognition as experience requires its own warrant; recursive updating or an articulate self-report does not settle it (Rodgers, 2026j,b).

2.14 A conditional connection to the full constitution

The current-act argument is developed independently of assuming the full A1–A3 constitution. A defensible application of SPC-2 requires a justified physical realisation and native operating doctrine (Rodgers, 2026e, ch. 17). A1 assigns a perspective to a qualifying maximal internal strongly connected component. Qualification requires an executable covering return and at least two distinct native predictive classes in the actual operating/protocol type and resource context. Incompatible control modes cannot be combined into a fictitious return. These conditions are not strengthened here by a new requirement for language, autobiographical reflection or a rich human scene.

A2 assigns phenomenal relational organisation as a structural copy of the core’s complete endogenous predictive object over all physically admitted finite continuations. The actual phenomenal point corresponds to the predictive class of the realised intrinsic state. Native operation and outcome labels, finite test laws, compatible horizon restrictions and well-defined class transition instruments are preserved. This is the full structural assignment, not a selected finite report profile or an investigator’s preferred time horizon. A3 then determines episode continuation through nonbranching realised process provenance; matching memories or outputs alone do not supply that continuity.

To connect the present case to those laws, an appropriate phase of its performance would have to be shown to belong to a justified qualifying core, with the relational mode QQQ identified as an aspect of the core’s current scene. A performance’s executing contributors and a subject’s boundary are different nominations requiring a bridge. An environmental source or instrument can contribute causally without thereby becoming part of that subject. With the realisation supplied and SPC-2 accepted, phenomenal states, their timing and continuation are assigned at its declared native temporal resolution. No such complete application is demonstrated here. The local current-act argument neither validates A2 nor independently selects the first onset of a scene, and it does not turn the subject boundary into the set of everything causally involved in the performance. 2.15 The October draft and the scope of the recollective thesis

The unpublished 12 October 2026 draft of Experience as Recollective Interpretation: Awareness, Scaffolding and the Question of Machine Experience (Rodgers, 2026d) defines experience as the interpretation of a retained occurrence as having occurred. It does not place a consciously viewed, unprocessed image before all interpretation: perceptual construction already shapes what is seen.

The comparison also limits inferences about artificial systems. Section 8’s narrow verdict concerns interpretation and recollection without the human personal layer. It does not supply the present paper with an independent appearance anchor, jointness test or physical bearer identification for an artificial interpreter. Conversely, failure to replicate human autobiographical or emotional organisation alone would not exclude every possible appearance. Both claims concern the scope of an inference, not a verdict about a particular system’s consciousness.

2.16 Occurrence without recognition and the limiting memory question

Something occurring, its currently appearing, its recognition as a particular object or event, its appropriation as happening to a person, and its subsequent interpretation are distinct questions. They can overlap in ordinary life. The locally supported current presentation need not await a later autobiographical account, and no such account is required by the concrete-performance principle. This does not decide the companion draft’s universal identification of experience with recollective interpretation.

The limiting condition that removes all acquired scaffolding and the ability to form memory must be distinguished from suspending naming or personal recognition. Its proposed underlying basis does not itself specify an impression carrying a discriminating record. Something occurring and its recognition as an event are different claims; absence of recognition does not establish absence of every process or retained dependence. Neither the absence of a named object nor the inability to describe a condition entails that no change took place. Equally, the stronger limiting condition should not be replaced with an ordinary recognised scene and a failed report.

The positive recollective model in the October draft explicitly includes retention. Its Section 5 distinguishes such minimal retention from conceptual or autobiographical memory; its limiting seeing case withholds interpretative experience while preserving an occurrence. Those claims do not establish an unrecognised retained trace in the separate limiting condition that removes the ability to form memory. No retention mechanism for that stronger condition is supplied by this comparison.

The remaining explanatory question is specific. If later interpretations differ with a particular antecedent, what makes their dependence on that antecedent possible and their historical reference warranted? The claim that memory categories are supplied later does not by itself answer that question. The following elementary result identifies a stronger condition than absence of conscious, conceptual or autobiographical memory. It is a conditional diagnostic; it does not establish that the limiting condition actually satisfies that stronger condition.

A continuing external source, nonconceptual state or separately proposed nonphysical relation would constitute a different dependence route and would need to be specified and justified. Mere persistence of one undifferentiated basis does not yet explain discrimination among antecedents. Absence of an ability to form memory also does not by itself establish the independence premise for all these variables. The stronger no-dependence stipulation and the no-memory limiting condition must therefore not be silently identified.

If the apparent historical reference is instead entirely a present construction, the account must explain what warrants relating it to one particular antecedent. This is an open explanatory obligation, not an established retained-information mechanism. The current-act conclusion does not depend on choosing a resolution: ordinary current sensory evidence and the constitutive argument have their own stated domain.

The further proposal that fundamental awareness may be known as conceptual scaffolding falls away is philosophical and motivated by first-person experience. It does not follow from the impossibility of reproducing an occurrence in a description. The distinction preserves that proposal while keeping particular claims of occurrence, content, basing and bearer assessable.
