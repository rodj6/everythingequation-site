A.1 Finite contact gas and its Poisson comparison

This appendix supplies the quantitative implication used in the pilot construction. It concerns a fixed finite graph and a fixed physical horizon, not a limit in which the graph, reference, or duration also grows. The primitive export and contact laws remain constitutive inputs [34, 37].

dTV(Bernoulli⁡(p),Poisson⁡(p))=p(1−e−p)≤p2. \TV(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p),\operatorname{Poisson}(p)) =p(1-e^{-p})\le p^2. dTV​(Bernoulli(p),Poisson(p))=p(1−e−p)≤p2.

The superposition of the Poisson counts with uniform event times is a Poisson point process on the physical interval with rate RRR. Any common causal processing of these marked contacts contracts total variation. This is why the bound controls subsequent contact-selected events rather than merely a total count.

The finite gas is not itself memoryless. Conditional on kkk crossings by time ttt, the residual rate is (M−k)/(M/R−t)(M-k)/(M/R-t)(M−k)/(M/R−t) while the remaining points are uniformly distributed in the unpassed interval.
