Radial jets, calibrated labels and the free Maxwell comparison hierarchy

From local coefficients to usable field labels. The first objects are finite Taylor coefficient spaces. Curl reads their linearized curvature, and radial reconstruction provides an explicit section on closed homogeneous two-forms. The Maxwell equation imposes a further condition on that curvature. Polynomial labels are then sampled by finite local cylinders, with calibrated coefficient maps and a free comparison hierarchy. Keeping polynomial position degree, engineering weight and field count distinct is necessary for the later detector and scaling arguments. The free hierarchy is a supplied comparison sector; its presence does not assert that the interacting hierarchy is free.

Before the detector on physical coefficient space can be constructed, the supplied construction builds the label algebra, its local lattice representatives, the calibrated coefficient changes and the complete free comparison hierarchy with its shell charges. This section gives that construction in full, from the radial Taylor variables to the elimination of higher-degree vector and angular defects; each subsection is followed by its source–readout formulation. The labels are the first place where the recovery problem is posed for fields and not only for measures: a label is a source record, its lattice cylinder is a realization, and its Taylor readings are the readout.
