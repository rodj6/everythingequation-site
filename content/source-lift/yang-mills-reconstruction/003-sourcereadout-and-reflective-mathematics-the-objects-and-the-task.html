Source–readout and reflective mathematics: the objects and the task

The framework distinguishes the mathematical input from its native antecedent. The supplied mathematical source is the expanded translation of OpenAI’s underlying Lean Yang–Mills construction. The native source support is an admitted object in Rodgers’s source–readout architecture: it carries a dependency order and marked finite supports. The physical lattice, group, fields and law enter in the realization over that support. This separation lets the paper construct and compare the actual readout operations while retaining the provenance of their physical assumptions.

In ordinary terms, a readout is a specified way of obtaining a mathematical object from a richer record. A lattice configuration can be read from links written in independently chosen endpoint frames. An effective action can be read from a function of retained and auxiliary variables by evaluating on its stationary graph. A physical field label can be read from a coefficient vector after identifying vectors that every allowed spectator test fails to distinguish. These are different maps, with different fibres. The paper proves their recovery statements on their respective domains; it does not treat them as interchangeable instances of an unrestricted inverse.

The term reflective mathematics means mathematics of the readout objects together with explicit accounting for their source–readout relations. In Rodgers’s Source Lift protocol this includes the maps and domains, admission conditions, distinctions removed by a map, residuals, available reconstruction routes and the status of each claim. Here those items are made concrete by the following definitions and calculations. Reflection is this source-aware accounting within the mathematics. It is not an additional dynamical law, and it does not assert that a theory selects its own physical inputs [3].

This use of reflective is distinct from reflection positivity, the positive-pairing property of Euclidean correlations used later to construct a Hilbert space. Reflection positivity is one of the specific mathematical operations accounted for here; it is not the definition of reflective mathematics. Likewise, matching status profiles does not establish an equivalence: an equivalence must preserve the specified objects and operations through actual maps.

The contribution of the reflective organization can now be stated precisely. It identifies which mathematical object a formula reads, whether a reverse section covers the next step, which operation descends, and which discrepancy or choice must survive a reduction. Its claims are proved by the actual maps and estimates, not by assigning a status word. The gauge group, dimension, Wilson action, physical normalization and analytic assumptions remain the supplied realization inputs except for the particular dependencies derived within the construction. Alternative realizations can be placed over the same native support; its admission therefore does not select those inputs.
