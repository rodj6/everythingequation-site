9.4
What two probes can and cannot identify
For arbitrary unknown effects, exact response on a set of target states can be determined from probe
expectations if and only if the target states lie in the real linear span of the probe density matrices.
Sufficiency follows by linearity. If a target lies outside that span, there is a Hermitian H orthogonal to
every probe and not to that target. Starting with a strictly interior POVM, perturb one effect by εH
and the null effect by −εH. For sufficiently small nonzero ε this remains physical, leaves every measured
probe response unchanged and changes the target response. This is an exact indistinguishability
counterexample, not a failed numerical search. Two distinct template density matrices are independent,
so two probes are minimal for full label-response identification in this unrestricted effect model. This is
not a minimum for every loose one-sided claim.
Our occupied templates require only the already nominated source’s relative-mode preparations.
No vacuum/occupied superposition probe is needed. Their unmeasured vacuum response remains
unidentified: adding d|v⟩⟨v| to a conclusive effect and subtracting it from the null is invisible to both
templates. It can change another input’s response and the full qutrit measurement-channel distance.
For a mixed target, an invisible traceless effect direction can likewise change a purifying reference’s
conditional assemblage while preserving its label probabilities. No full-POVM or arbitrary-input
diamond guarantee is claimed here. Future source deviations are charged through δS,h rather than
assumed harmless.

Preservation and scientific status
The companion’s proof interfaces differ in observable task and access permissions. Their numerical
examples are synthetic; tests corroborate the proofs rather than constitute empirical evidence. Original
source/controller, reference, clock, preparation, null/loss and retained-output conditions survive every
reuse. The general qutrit method estimates a full effect family; the two-template method certifies a
smaller task and does not identify the same quantum output. Their sample counts are therefore not a
universal tomography comparison. No physical horizon-to-biological channel has been supplied; no
actual-event affinity is inferred from an averaged density equation. The source–readout completion
framework remains compatible with unrestricted operational surrogates, so a constrained detector
distinction does not establish unique ontology.
