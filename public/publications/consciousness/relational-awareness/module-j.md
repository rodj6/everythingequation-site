## Presented form, empirical tests and the limits of transfer {#section-10}

The current-act account has to earn each connection it makes. Evidence can support an actual visual form without identifying its smallest bearer; a physically well-specified predictor can fail without undermining the testimony on which the form was identified. This chapter brings those cases together, developing the actual native applications while testing the reach of their physical interpretation.

### 10.1 From an extensive form to a modest native application {#section-10-1}

The presented-form argument concerns the qualitative organisation of an actual presentation. A sufficiently resolved patch can have differently situated aspects within one apparent extent. A location code, an unresolved point, a remembered shape label and an accurate drawing need not have that organisation. The empirical question is therefore whether the record gives reason to accept the extensive presentation, rather than merely a spatially competent response. The subsequent aspect inference and the concrete-performance/mode principle remain separate steps.

Neither protocol supplies a dedicated finite-aspect probe or a trialwise jointness rate. #### A surface with historical and current suppliers {#afterimage-surface}

Their appearance-directed account and centre-colour matching give modest ordinary warrant for an actual filled surface. #### An endogenous form during REM sleep {#dream-form}

LaBerge et al. (2018) combine physiologically staged REM, prearranged eye-movement signals bracketing a tracking task, and subsequent descriptions of following a perceived dream thumb or nail. The sensory testimony gives modest retrospective warrant for some extensive dream-target instances, although less directly than an explicit enlargement or contour probe. Pursuit geometry alone would not do so. The published sensory descriptions and duration/success criteria select the analysed records; their reported success fraction is not an all-attempt relational-occurrence rate. The supplementary narratives were not recovered for this assessment.

A single actually extensive target can meet the minimum without a second dream object or a simultaneously visible motion path. This revises what the evidence must establish, rather than adding an observation to the study. Its time reference remains retrospective: the physiology nominates a sleep engagement, while testimony attributes the visual form to that engagement. First formation at a current dream-time $J$ is compatible with the inference. Assigning all form only to the waking interview instead challenges the accepted retrospective reference; it does not preserve that reference while merely choosing a later phase. Neither interpretation is settled by eye movements alone.

These examples support separately delimited native applications at ordinary phenomenological strength. They do not silently enlarge the original visual predicate $V$ to every sleeper, implanted system or artificial implementation. For each, the appearance evidence, sensory-activity tether, physical nomination, time reference and constitutive placement must be assessed in their own right. The strongest exclusive-bearer alternative can grant all three actual forms and their genuine acquaintance while reserving qualitative inherence to $\mathbb{B}$. The additional applications therefore extend the reach of the defended current-act reasoning without becoming independent experimental proofs of its bearer principle.

### 10.4 A felt act and its bodily execution {#section-10-4}

Desmurget et al. (2009) supply a pertinent boundary on inferring execution from experience of acting. In seven awake neurosurgical patients, posterior stimulation could elicit reported urge without observed movement or detected activity in the monitored muscles; stronger stimulation could elicit illusory movement reports. Premotor stimulation could elicit movement without reported awareness of it. Responsive-site selection, different clinical cohorts, limited muscle monitoring and the absence here of the detailed supporting protocol restrict the comparison. It is not an all-trigger randomized double dissociation or a measurement of global phenomenal absence.

A genuine feeling of execution can thus misrepresent whether the nominated bodily movement occurred. This does not make the feeling unreal, remove its physical induction, or show that felt motives never contribute to action. Likewise, movement without reported awareness of that movement does not imply an entirely experience-free subject. What the current-act argument must track is the actual sensory or comparative activity, with its operative connection to any further execution independently warranted. The represented act, its present qualitative character and the peripheral bodily result cannot simply be identified. The study supplies a concrete limit on that transfer, rather than a new relational-minimum instance, mode-preserving intervention or bearer test.
